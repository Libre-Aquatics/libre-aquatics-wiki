The Ultimate Pace Clock is a pace clock system that
[Colorado Time Systems](../../../vendors/colorado-time-systems.md) (CTS) sold from the
mid-1990s, made up of a hand-held keypad console that stores and runs swimming workouts and
a portable deck display that shows them.[^f819][^ctsult] Buyers chose a light-reflective
display (`UPC-LR`), an LED display (`UPC-LED`) or the console alone (`UPC-C`), and the
console could also drive CTS's existing numeric scoreboards.[^f819] The displays doubled as
start, relay and turn reaction trainers when competition timing equipment was plugged into
them.[^f819] By 2008 CTS listed only the console, which it still sells as the
[pace clock controller](upc-c.md).[^cat08][^ctspc]

## Naming and product line

This section sets out the three packages and how the name changed.

In the guide the Ultimate Pace Clock is a system: a keypad console with a choice of display,
sold in three packages.[^f819]

| Package | Contents |
|---|---|
| Light-reflective display (`UPC-LR`) | Display, console, user guide, QuickStart user guide, 14 ft data cable, 7 ft adapter cable, 20 ft AC power cord and wall brick |
| LED display (`UPC-LED`) | As above, with the LED display |
| Console only (`UPC-C`) | Console, user guide, 14 ft data cable, 7 ft adapter cable |

The guide mostly calls the displays the LR and LED models and writes `UPC-LR` and `UPC-LED`
only once; it calls the keypad the Ultimate Pace Clock console or the UPC console.[^f819] A
CTS web page of 2002 already gives the console as the Ultimate Pace Clock Controller,
`UPC-C`.[^ctssasc9]

The meaning of the name shifted over time. In the 1995 guide it is the whole system.[^f819]
CTS's 2002 web page still describes a complete system, but with only the light-reflective
display.[^ctsult] By 2007 the console's own guide calls it the pace clock controller and
lists the Ultimate Pace Clock as a separate display that the controller can drive.[^f901]

## Design and hardware

This section describes the portable display; the console is covered in the next section.

CTS gives the light-reflective display 9 in digits and describes it as readable indoors and
outdoors. It measures 4 ft wide, 14 in high and 5 in deep and weighs 45 lb.[^ctsult] No
held or archived CTS document gives the size, digit height or colour of the LED model, which
the guide and a 2002 CTS page name but do not describe.[^f819][^ctssasc9]

All the display's connections are on one side panel.[^f819fig] From the top, it has:

- two modular jacks, one to the console or the previous display and one to the next
  display, so displays can be connected in a chain;
- five pairs of banana sockets, for an external horn, a pushbutton, a relay judging platform,
  a start system and a touchpad;
- a rocker switch with ON and OFF/CHARGE positions;
- a round multi-pin power connector.

The display runs from an internal rechargeable battery or from AC power. CTS recommends the
battery when the display stands on the pool deck, and AC power for charging or for a clock
mounted permanently on a wall.[^f819][^ctsult] The console sounds the display's built-in
buzzer to send swimmers off or mark a pace.[^f819]

## Console

This section summarises the keypad console, which has its own article.

The console is a hand-held keypad with an LCD that stores 50 workouts of up to 30 sets, each
set holding up to 15 subsets, and can run up to ten workouts at once on separate displays,
selected with its SELECT SCBD key.[^f819] Sets can repeat, count down instead of up, or
shorten their interval after each repeat, and workouts can be downloaded from Hy-Tek's
[Workout Manager](../../../software/hy-tek-workout-manager.md).[^f819] It turns itself off
after five minutes unused.[^f819] Its keys and operation are unchanged in the later
[pace clock controller](upc-c.md) guide, which documents them in full.[^f901]

CTS's figures for the console vary between documents. The 2002 web page gives it a
32-character display, one 9-volt battery and a 15 ft connector cable, and a size of 8 in high
by 3 in wide and a weight of 2 lb.[^ctsult] The 2014 controller datasheet gives
2.5 × 7.5 × 4.0 in and 0.8 lb.[^sheet] F819 ships a 14 ft data cable and a 7 ft adapter cable
instead of a single 15 ft cable.[^f819]

## QuickStart training inputs

This section covers the reaction-timing function built into the portable displays.

CTS describes the portable displays as unusual among programmable pace clocks because they
also work as reaction trainers, the job of its separate [QuickStart](quickstart.md)
product.[^f819] A display buyer received the QuickStart guide with the package.[^f819]

The trainer uses the competition equipment plugged into the side panel: a pushbutton, a
[relay judging platform](../../swimming/relay-judging/cts.md), a start system (an SS-1 or
SS-2) and a [touchpad](../../swimming/touchpad/index.md).[^f819] With them the display times
start reactions, break-outs after the start, relay exchanges and turns.[^f819]

The standalone QuickStart, which CTS was advertising as newly released in spring 1996, did
the same work on a smaller display with 4 in LED digits.[^st96qs][^ctsqs]

## Supported displays and timers

This section lists what the console could drive and the software it worked with.

| Display | Notes |
|---|---|
| `UPC-LR` and `UPC-LED` portable displays | Data cable to the side-panel jack; further displays chained from it[^f819][^f819fig] |
| CTS 4009 light-reflective scoreboard | Through the adapter cable, as a timer would connect; time of day on line 1 of a multi-line board[^f819] |
| CTS 3009 scoreboard | Through the adapter cable; no time of day[^f819] |
| [SASC9](sasc9.md) four-digit pace clock | Named on CTS's 2002 SASC9 page[^ctssasc9] |

The console posts the time of day only on a portable display or a 4009, and has no clock of
its own, so the time is keyed in at each use.[^f819] For Workout Manager downloads, the guide
tells the coach to follow Hy-Tek's instructions for the CTS pace clock software that ran on
the [System 5](../../swimming/timers/system-5.md) and [Swim IV](../../swimming/timers/swim-iv.md)
timers.[^f819]

The [4009](../scoreboard/4009.md) was CTS's light-reflective numeric scoreboard of the
period, with 9 in digits on modules 14 in high, the same digit size and height that CTS
gives for the Ultimate display.[^cts4009][^ctsult] The [3009](../scoreboard/3009.md) is
named in F819 and in a 2004 System 5 brochure but is not described in any document found.[^f819][^s5brochure]

## Care and maintenance

This section gives CTS's battery instructions for the portable displays.

CTS recommends recharging a portable display's internal battery after every use. To charge
it, the operator switches the display off, connects its AC cable to a 115 V outlet and leaves
it overnight for a full charge. CTS warns against leaving a display on charge for more than eight days in a
row.[^f819]

## History

This section traces the system from its first guide to the console's later sale on its own.

The user guide carries a 1995 copyright, and the earliest dated advertisement found is a
small CTS notice in the spring 1996 issue of Swimming Technique. That notice lists three
features: Workout Manager compatibility, storage for 50 workouts, and ten workouts running
at once.[^f819][^st96] The QuickStart, whose functions the portable displays shared, was
being advertised as a new release in the same issue.[^st96qs]

CTS's website showed the Ultimate Pace Clock among its light-reflective aquatic products
from at least December 2002 to December 2004, describing the light-reflective display only
and naming the 4009 as the scoreboard it could drive.[^ctsult] The guide held was revised in
September 2003.[^f819]

By 2007 the console had its own guide as the pace clock controller, and CTS's 2008, 2011 and
2015 catalogues list `UPC-C` among their pace clock part numbers with no `UPC-LR` or
`UPC-LED`.[^f901][^cat08][^cat11][^cat15] CTS still sells the console as the Pace Clock
Controller (UPC) in 2026.[^ctspc]
