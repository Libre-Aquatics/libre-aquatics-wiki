Research notes, Ultimate Pace Clock.

Surfaced while building out the pace clock controller, which is the same console under a
later name. Held source: cts-ultimate-pace-clock-user-guide-f819.pdf, with its text

What F819 establishes. The product is a system, not one device: CTS sold it three ways, and
the guide lists the contents of each package. A light-reflective portable display (model
UPC-LR), an LED portable display (UPC-LED), or the keypad console on its own. The console
is called "the UPC console" in several places in the guide, which is where the UPC-C part number
comes from. The console option ships with a 14 ft data cable and a 7 ft adapter cable; each display
option adds the display itself, a QuickStart guide, and a 20 ft mains lead with its wall
brick.

The console's capabilities are the same as those documented for the later pace clock
controller: 50 workouts of 30 sets of 15 subsets, ten displays addressed by SELECT SCBD,
five-minute auto power-off, the same key set, the same Hy-Tek Workout Manager download.

pass. What is distinctive to this page is the two displays and the QuickStart feature.

QuickStart. F819 says the portable displays double as reaction trainers, the same job as the
separate CTS QuickStart product, and that a display buyer receives a QuickStart manual
covering it. Four inputs sit along the display's side panel: a touchpad, a relay judging platform, a
start system (SS-1 or SS-2), and a banana-plug socket taking a pushbutton. CTS
lists the measurements as start reaction, break-out, relay exchange and turn speed. That
makes this a training device with a competition-equipment input set, which is unusual and
worth building out.

Target displays. The console drove the two portable displays or existing CTS 4009 and 3009
scoreboards. The 3009 is named only here in the whole corpus and is covered nowhere on this
wiki; the 4009 is discussed inside led-r.md without a page of its own. Both are stub
candidates from an earlier pass that have not been created.

Battery: CTS recommends recharging a portable display's internal battery after every use,
leaving it overnight to charge fully, and says not to leave one on charge beyond eight days
running.

This article is a stub. The Ultimate Pace Clock is a Colorado Time Systems training system
of the 1990s, sold in three forms: a light-reflective portable display (`UPC-LR`), an LED
portable display (`UPC-LED`), or the keypad console on its own (`UPC-C`), which could also
drive existing CTS scoreboards.

The console outlived the system and is documented separately as the
[pace clock controller](upc-c.md); see the [pace clocks overview](index.md) for the shared
background.
