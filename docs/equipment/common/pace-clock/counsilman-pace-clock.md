Swimming World advertisements (sources/periodicals/swimming-world/):
  - 196410: "The New Counsilman Pace Clock". An improved model: a new face said to be easier
    to read, a 60-cycle motor said to be three times stronger than in earlier models, an
    on-off switch, both hands settable by hand. Uses given: spectator interest, pacing
    distance swimmers, interval training. Wall brackets or legs, or both. $65.00, shipped
    by Rail Express. Order from James Counsilman, 1917 Marilyn Drive, Bloomington, Indiana.
    The "previous models" wording means earlier versions existed before October 1964.
  - 196511 onward: "Over 1000 Counsilman Pace Clocks have been sold"; brackets plus a swivel
    leg; balanced hands; same $65.00.
  - 196701: face three feet square. Adds the Porta-Pace Clock: plexiglass, 12 in square face,
    German-made jewelled movement on one flashlight battery said to last a year, under 5 lb,
    $40.00.
  - Last advertisement found: 197108.
Invention claims (see the overview's History section): Swim News Feb 2004 obituary says
Counsilman first manufactured pace clocks in 1948; Swimming World Nov 1994 timeline says 1949;
SwimSwam (paywalled, seen only as search summaries) says 1959 and a 15 in face. None of
these is corroborated by a dated product record; the earliest ad found is 1964.
Swimming World Nov 1994 also says he built "thousands" of the first purpose-designed clocks.

This article is a stub. The Counsilman Pace Clock was a sweep-hand training clock sold by mail by the Indiana University coach James Counsilman, advertised from 1964 at $65.00 with a 3 ft face, together with a 12 in battery model called the Porta-Pace.

See the [pace clocks overview](index.md) for the history and shared background that apply to
every pace clock.
