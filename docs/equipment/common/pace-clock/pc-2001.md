The PC-2001 is a portable LED pace clock made by [Daktronics](../../../vendors/daktronics.md),
designed in 2002 and documented from 2003, which runs from an internal battery and offers
eleven programs: a workout pace clock, a count-up timer, start, backstroke and relay reaction
timers, a game clock, a water polo shot clock and a time-of-day clock.[^manual][^spec] It is
operated from switches on its side, from the JC-100 handheld console, or from a Daktronics
OmniSport 2000 or All Sport console, and it can take workouts downloaded from Hy-Tek
software.[^manual][^spec] See the [pace clocks overview](index.md) for the background shared by
every pace clock.

## Models and history

Daktronics builds the clock in two versions that differ in their batteries. The outdoor
`PC-2001-11` carries two batteries and the indoor `PC-2001-13` carries one; the manual says
the clock may be used outdoors for a while but must not be mounted outside permanently or left
out in the wet.[^manual] A retailer listed it under the code `PC-2001-11A`.[^swimoutlet]

The design dates from 2002 and 2003. The schematic it shares with the TI-2022 timer was drawn
in October 2002, the riser diagrams for portable installation and for the Hy-Tek download were
drawn in July and August 2003, and every edition of the manual carries a copyright running from
2003.[^manual] The OmniSport 2000 console gained the pace clock program that drives it in a
software release of 18 July 2003.[^revhist] Hy-Tek's Team Manager refers to the clock as the
"PC 2000".[^hytek][^manual]

Two related displays share its documentation. The [PC-2002](pc-2002.md) is a mains-powered
version for permanent indoor wall mounting with a shorter list of programs, and the
[TI-2022](ti-2022.md) uses the same cabinet, batteries and circuit design with programs for
other sports instead of swimming.[^pc2002][^ti2022][^manual] The 2018 aquatics brochure offers
the PC-2001 for training and as a water polo shot clock, and points customers wanting larger
digits to the [TI-2101](ti-2101.md) indoor timing display.[^brochure]

As of 2026 the manual is still published and was last revised in August 2023, when the
schematic was changed for an 18 V input.[^manual] The clock has nevertheless left at least one
retail channel: SwimOutlet's listing for it is marked discontinued.[^swimoutlet] Daktronics
has also announced that it is ending the OmniSport timing system, one of the consoles that
controls the clock, with no new orders and service for up to seven more years.[^kbshot]

## Design and hardware

The cabinet is semi-gloss black aluminum (5052 alloy, per the specification sheet), with a
carrying handle and rubber feet. Its face holds four seven-segment digits 7 in (178 mm) tall,
lit by amber LEDs.[^spec][^manual] Brightness has sixteen steps and defaults to the
brightest.[^manual]

Inside there is one circuit board, mounted on the back of the front panel, that carries the
digit LEDs, the processors, the power connectors and the 12 V horn; every switch and jack
connects to it.[^manual] The controls and connectors sit together on the right-hand side:

- Four switches, labeled START, STOP, RESET (which also acts as a shift key) and HORN, with
  secondary functions for setting minutes, seconds, hours and the program number.
- Banana-jack input pairs labeled HORN START, TOUCHPAD, PLATFORM and RESET BUTTON, plus
  round SIGNAL IN and SIGNAL OUT jacks.
- A 4-pin AUX. PORT for the JC-100 console.
- A power switch, the external power inlet (marked 18V IN), and charge and battery-status
  lamps.[^manual]

A chart of the programs and what plugs in for each is printed on a vinyl decal on the back of
the cabinet.[^manual] The clock can be wall-mounted on an optional bracket or carried on a
tripod kit; Daktronics says a camera tripod is not strong enough and sells a speaker-stand
tripod for the purpose.[^manual]

## Power

The clock runs from 12 V sealed lead-acid batteries with a built-in charger. Daktronics rates
a full charge at roughly ten hours of use, asks for a 24-hour charge before the clock goes into
storage, and states that the charger can stay plugged in indefinitely without overcharging the
batteries. The clock can be used while it charges.[^manual] Power supply kits are sold for
120 V and 240 V mains.[^manual]

The manuals do not agree on the input voltage. The 2013 specification sheet describes a 12 V
transformer, and the power connection figure in the 2023 manual still labels the inlet 12 V,
but the control-panel drawing in the same manual reads 18 V. The schematic records the change
from 12 V to 18 V in April 2023, and Daktronics' support article on the supply gives its output
as 18 V DC at 3.62 A.[^spec][^manual][^kbpsu] The manual gives the draw as 1 A.[^manual]

To save the battery the clock turns itself off in two cases, both only when it is running on
battery power. After an hour with no counting, no switch presses and no input signal, it sounds
the horn, flashes its digits ten times and shuts down; the time-of-day programs are exempt.
When the battery falls below a minimum level it sounds the horn every second, and if it is not
connected to the charger within about a minute it switches off so that the battery is not
drained completely.[^manual]

## Programs

The program is chosen by holding RESET for two seconds, which shows `PR` and the program
number, then pressing HORN while RESET is still held to step to the next one. Preset times are
set the same way, with START advancing minutes (or hours) and STOP advancing seconds (or
minutes).[^manual]

| No. | Program | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Breakout (count-up) timer | Counts up to a set time between 00:00 and 99:59 and sounds the horn; with no time set it runs to 59:59 and starts again. A start input while running shows a split for ten seconds. |
| 2 | Start reaction, normally closed | Time from the start signal to the swimmer leaving a relay take-off platform; negative if the swimmer goes early |
| 3 | Backstroke reaction | Time from the start signal to the swimmer leaving a touchpad; negative if early |
| 4 | Relay exchange, normally closed | Exchange time, from the finishing swimmer's touchpad signal to the next swimmer's platform release; negative if early |
| 5 | Workout ("pace clock") | Counts up to a pace time, sounds the horn and repeats, or runs a programmed workout |
| 6 | Game clock | Counts down from a set time to zero and sounds the horn |
| 7 | Shot clock | Two-digit countdown of up to 99 seconds on the center digits |
| 8 | Time of day, 12-hour | Clock display |
| 9 | Time of day, 24-hour | Clock display |
| 10 | Start reaction, normally open | As program 2, for a normally open platform |
| 11 | Relay exchange, normally open | As program 4, for a normally open platform |

Program numbers and behavior follow the 2023 manual.[^manual] In the relay exchange programs
the clock shows `Err` if a touch arrives and no platform release follows within two seconds,
and with no touchpad connected the STOP switch can stand in for the touch.[^manual] The start
reaction programs are drawn with the [HS-200 Horn Start](../../swimming/starter/hs-200.md)
connected to the normally open start input, and the manual allows a horn start in addition to
the START switch.[^manual]

The shot clock's reset behaves differently depending on which switch was pressed last. After
START, RESET restores the preset and the count resumes as soon as RESET is released; after
STOP, RESET restores the preset and the clock waits for START. Selecting the shot clock also
sets the SIGNAL IN jack to accept a feed from an All Sport or OmniSport 2000 console, so the
clock can serve as a portable water polo shot clock.[^manual]

Daktronics' own documents disagree on some details. The 2018 brochure says the clock has twelve
programs, while the manual, the specification sheet and the support knowledge base all list
eleven.[^brochure][^manual][^spec][^kberr] The specification sheet describes the shot clock as
showing seconds and tenths, where the manual says whole seconds (tens and units). The
specification sheet also says the count-up program sounds the horn and then holds until START
is pressed, a detail the manual does not give.[^spec][^manual]

### Relay take-off platform wiring

Programs 2 and 4 and programs 10 and 11 are the same timers wired for opposite kinds of
platform. Daktronics labels the normally closed pair "Omega" and the normally open pair
"Daktronics", so a club can use either a platform from Omega (the brand of
[Swiss Timing](../../../vendors/swiss-timing.md)) or a
[Daktronics RTOP](../../swimming/relay-judging/daktronics.md).[^manual] The OmniSport 2000
manual agrees: to test an RTOP on a pace clock, the clock is
set to program 11 and the platform is plugged into the PLATFORM jack, and Daktronics says its
platforms work only with the OmniSport 2000 or a Daktronics pace clock.[^omni]

A clock that flashes `Err` constantly usually has the wrong program for the platform in use or
a banana plug inserted the wrong way round; Daktronics' support article says the ground tab
must face down, toward the black plug.[^kberr]

## Workouts

In program 5 the side switches set a single pace time from 00:00 to 99:59; the clock counts up
to it, sounds the horn, rolls over to zero and repeats. With the time left at zero it simply
counts to 59:59 and starts over.[^manual] The specification sheet adds the horn pattern: two short
beeps in the final two seconds of each interval, then a long one as the next swim begins.[^spec]

Longer workouts are entered on the [JC-100](../../diving/jc-100.md) handheld console, which
plugs into the AUX. PORT and uses a pace clock keypad insert (`LL-2584`). A workout is a list of
steps of six types:[^manual]

- Swim and rest steps, each with its own time.
- A pace step, which loops one interval without end and takes precedence over any steps after
  it in the workout or circuit.
- Begin circuit and end circuit steps, which bracket a group of steps to be repeated a set
  number of times.
- A stop step, which ends the workout.

Steps can be inserted, deleted, or cleared all at once, and the horn can be set separately for
swim and rest steps. Once a workout is loaded and its first step is not a pace step, the side
switches only start, stop, sound the horn and return to step one.[^manual] The JC-100 also sets
times, brightness and automatic or manual horn for the other programs, and shows whether the
platform or touchpad is open or closed before a start.[^manual]

### Hy-Tek download

Workouts written in Hy-Tek's [Team Manager](../../../software/hy-tek-team-manager.md) with
the [Workout Manager](../../../software/hy-tek-workout-manager.md) option can be sent to the
clock instead of typed in. The clock is put in program 5 with the JC-100 connected, and the
computer's serial port is cabled to the SIGNAL IN jack with the
[Hy-Tek data cable](hy-tek-data-cable.md), a 20 ft lead from a 9-pin serial plug to a
1/4 in stereo phone plug; a computer without a serial port needs a USB adapter.[^manual] In Team
Manager the operator opens the Daktronics pace clock screen, picks the PC-2000 target and the
port, assigns a workout to lane 1 and transfers it. Only the lane 1 workout is sent, and the
clock shows `donE` when the transfer has worked.[^manual] Hy-Tek's own guide lists the clock
among its pace clock targets but describes the link as a straight RS-232 cable rather than
the phone-plug lead Daktronics supplies.[^hytek][^manual]

## Console and radio control

The clock needs no console, but it can be cabled to one. Daktronics recommends at least
single-pair shielded 22 AWG cable (`W-1077`) for the signal, and prefers two-pair cable
(`W-1614`).[^manual] The specification sheet lists the JC-100, the OmniSport 2000 and the
[All Sport 1600](../scoreboard-control/all-sport-1600.md) and 5000 series consoles as
external controllers.[^spec] Daktronics' riser
drawing shows four ways of running several clocks together:[^manual]

1. One clock set up from its side switches, with further clocks chained from its SIGNAL OUT
   jack showing the same display.
2. The same chain with a JC-100 on the first clock's auxiliary port.
3. An [OmniSport 2000](../../swimming/timers/omnisport-2000.md) wired to the chain, with
   each clock's program deciding what it shows.
4. The same arrangement by radio.

For radio control the clock takes an optional receiver mounted inside the cabinet, with its
antenna through a hole in the side, and the controlling OmniSport 2000 or All Sport console
needs a radio transmitter. The manual refers to it as a [Gen VI](../scoreboard-control/gen-vi-radio.md)
receiver. During its power-on self test the clock shows the receiver's broadcast group and
channel, and both must match the console.[^manual] The 2013 specification sheet lists the
option as 2.4 GHz spread-spectrum radio control.[^spec]

Daktronics describes the clock as an addressable driver. When it runs from an OmniSport 2000,
the console's scoreboard output has to be set to multi-drop, or the shot time does not
appear.[^kbshot] An [All Sport 5000](../scoreboard-control/all-sport-5000.md) can drive it
for drills timed in tenths of a second, but only through the main game clock, because the
All Sport segment timer accepts whole seconds.[^kbtenths]

## Care and maintenance

The front and rear panels are held together by ten screws. Opening them gives access to the
circuit board, which comes off its eight studs as one part, and to the batteries, which sit in
a bracket held by four nuts.[^manual] Replacement batteries must be the same type, wired red to
positive and black to negative, and the old lead-acid battery is to be recycled.[^manual]
Daktronics' six-monthly maintenance checklist asks for a visual check of the clock's switches,
knobs and connections, a firmware check and an operating test.[^maint]
