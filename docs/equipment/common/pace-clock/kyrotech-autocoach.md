The AutoCoach was a programmable digital pace clock made by [Kyrotech](../../../vendors/kyrotech.md)
of Northbrook, Illinois. A microprocessor ran the clock, and a coach set it up from a small
pushbutton panel to run a whole workout of timed repeats. Kyrotech advertised it in Swimming
World from September 1982 to July 1986, and it was still listed in 1991.[^kt8209][^kt8607][^sw9108]

## Role in training

A pace clock lets swimmers read their own send-off and lap times; see the
[pace clocks overview](index.md) for the history of the class. The AutoCoach was built for
interval-repeat sets. Instead of swimmers watching a sweep hand and working out each send-off, the
clock held the set and counted the repeats as they were swum. Kyrotech called it "a workout
coach in a box".[^kt8209][^sw9108] Its Canadian distributor added that it showed lap and
cumulative splits and sounded horn tones when the program moved to its next stage.[^sc8404]

## Design and operation

This section describes the clock as the advertisements and one published review present it.

The clock was programmed from ten numbered pushbuttons and run from two more. A program could mix
intervals that grew longer, grew shorter or included rest, and it could hold up to 100 sets, each
of 1–99 repeats, at any interval up to 99:59.[^kt8209] The clock stepped in whole seconds by
default; Kyrotech offered other steps from 0.01 s to one hour. A coach's favourite drills could be
built in at the factory as permanent programs, at extra cost.[^kt8209] By 1986 users could add
their own workouts to the built-in memory, and the clock was offered with several send-offs at
once.[^kt8605]

Kyrotech's 1982 drawing shows a four-digit display made of round lamps, with the programming panel
below it. Later advertisements show segment-style digits.[^kt8209][^kt8303] A 1984 photograph
shows a handled control box, with its keypad and a printed program chart, sitting on top of a long
four-digit display.[^sc8404] Two display types were offered: a high-ambient-light version and one
using ordinary bright bulbs.[^sc8404] The 1986 advertisement gives incandescent digits 6 in high,
readable in anything short of direct sunlight, and mains power at 120 V AC.[^kt8605]

The clock signalled changes in the program with three horn tones at two loudness levels, and an
external horn was available.[^sc8404] It had a jack for an external speaker.[^bird87]

## Advertising and sales

Kyrotech advertised the AutoCoach in nearly every issue of Swimming World from September 1982 to
November 1984. A first version ran until February 1983, and a reworded one followed from March
1983.[^kt8209][^kt8303] From December 1984 Kyrotech ran rotating advertisements for its whole
line, each featuring one product above a footer that listed the AutoCoach, the
[Tec-Timer](../../swimming/timers/kyrotech-tec-timer.md) printing meet timer, the
[Tec-Start](../../swimming/starter/kyrotech-tec-start.md) start system, the
[Databoard](../scoreboard/kyrotech-databoard.md) display, touchpads, a meet-record board and an
unexplained "HAL".[^kt8412][^kt8506] The AutoCoach was featured in that series in May 1986, and
the older AutoCoach advertisement ran once more in July 1986, the last found.[^kt8605][^kt8607]

In Canada [The Woodchester Company](../../../vendors/woodchester-company.md) of Mississauga,
Ontario, advertised the clock in SwimCanada from April to June 1984. A product list from the same
address carried a Kyrotech programmable pace clock until at least June 1985.[^sc8404][^sc8410]

The August 1991 Swimming World computer survey still carries an AutoCoach entry from Kyrotech,
describing a pushbutton-operated training clock with a 100-set capacity.[^sw9108] Kyrotech's
September 1992 advertisement lists only its meet-timing products, and no later mention of the
AutoCoach was found.[^sw9209]

## Reception

In a March 1987 column for the National Interscholastic Swimming Coaches Association (NISCA),
Charles E. "Skip" Bird, who coached the boys' team at Valparaiso High School, Indiana, reviewed the
AutoCoach among several products his team had tried. He stated that his views were his own, not
NISCA's.[^bird87] He found the clock simple to run, even for complicated sets, and said a whole
practice could be programmed from the data printed on its case. It let him coach during practice
instead of acting as "a traffic cop". For more individual work it could be left running
continuously. Several fuses blew when the clock was switched off and straight back on, which he
thought might be particular to the unit he had. He wished it had a strobe that fired with the
beeper and considered wiring one to the external speaker jack.[^bird87]

## Relationship to the Kyroscope

The [Kyroscope pace clock](kyroscope-pace-clock.md), sold by Taroda Industries of Chicago, was an
earlier programmable digital clock with a similar repeat capacity (1–99 repeats of an interval up
to 99:59). Taroda's last Kyroscope advertisement found is from April 1982, and Kyrotech's first
AutoCoach advertisement is from September 1982. Kyrotech presented the AutoCoach as the newest of
many programmable digital pace clocks already in use, without naming the earlier ones.[^kt8209] The similar names, the Chicago-area
locations and the timing suggest a connection, but no source found names one: no sale, successor
or shared person is recorded.
