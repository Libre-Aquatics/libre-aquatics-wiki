The TI-2022 is a battery-powered, carry-around LED timing display made by
[Daktronics](../../../vendors/daktronics.md).
It offers nine timing programs, selected from switches on its
side: two count-up timers, an agility timer, a game clock, a qualifying lap timer, a seconds
counter, a lap counter and two time-of-day clocks.[^manual] It is built on the same cabinet,
batteries and circuit design as the [PC-2001](pc-2001.md) pace clock, which runs swimming
programs instead.[^manual][^pc2001] Daktronics describes it as a self-contained timer that needs
no console, although an All Sport or OmniSport console can drive it.[^manual] See the
[pace clocks overview](index.md) for the background shared by every pace clock.

## Models and history

Daktronics builds the TI-2022 in two versions that differ in their batteries. The outdoor
`TI-2022-11` carries two batteries and the indoor `TI-2022-13` carries one. The manual allows
temporary use outdoors but not permanent outdoor mounting or use in the wet.[^manual] A dealer
catalogue lists the display at 9.02 in high, 24.02 in wide and 5 in deep, the same size as the
PC-2001.[^aviq][^pc2001]

The timer belongs to the same family of manuals as the PC-2001 and the
[PC-2002](pc-2002.md). All three share Daktronics' project number P1153 and carry copyrights
from 2003, and the TI-2022 and PC-2001 share a single schematic, drawn in October 2002.[^manual][^pc2001][^pc2002]
Daktronics' aquatics maintenance checklist lists the TI-2022 manual beside the two pace clock
manuals.[^maint] As of 2026 the manual is still published, last revised in August 2023.[^manual]

## Design and hardware

The cabinet, carrying handle and internal layout follow the PC-2001: a single circuit board
mounted behind the front panel carries the digits, processors, power connections and horn, and
every switch and jack connects to it.[^manual][^pc2001] The face shows four digits with a
colon.[^manual] Neither the manual nor the dealer listing gives the digit height or color.

The controls sit on the right-hand side. Four switches marked START, STOP, HORN and RESET each
have a pair of banana jacks beside them, so that pushbuttons or photocells can be wired in
parallel with the switches. The same panel holds round SIGNAL IN and SIGNAL OUT jacks, a 4-pin
auxiliary port, the power inlet and switch, and lamps that show the battery as full, low or
charging.[^manual] The program chart that the manual prints as Figure 10 is also fixed to the
back of the cabinet as a decal.[^manual]

The auxiliary port is the one the PC-2001 uses for the [JC-100](../../diving/jc-100.md) handheld
console. Daktronics' JC-100 specification sheet lists the TI-2022 among the displays it
operates, but the TI-2022 manual does not mention the JC-100 or say what the port is for.[^jc100][^manual]

## Programs

A program is chosen by holding RESET for two seconds, which shows `PR` and the program number,
then pressing HORN while RESET is still held to step to the next. Preset times are set the same
way, with START advancing minutes and STOP advancing seconds, or hours and minutes for the clock
programs.[^manual]

| No. | Program | Inputs | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Count up with pushbuttons | Pushbuttons on START and STOP (normally open) | Counts up to a preset of up to 99:59 and sounds the horn; with no preset it rolls over at 59:59 and carries on |
| 2 | Count up with photocells | Photocells on START and STOP (normally closed) | As program 1, triggered by photocells |
| 3 | Agility timer | Photocells on START and STOP | Times a single start-to-stop run of up to 99.99 seconds; once stopped, it does not restart on a new start input |
| 4 | Game clock | Optional All Sport console on SIGNAL IN | Counts down from a preset of up to 99:59 and sounds the horn at zero |
| 5 | Qualifying timer | One photocell on START | Times successive laps of a car through one start/finish line |
| 6 | Flop clock | None | Counts whole seconds to 9999, then rolls over |
| 7 | Lap counter | None | START adds one to a count of 1 to 9999; STOP takes one away |
| 8 | Time of day, 12-hour | None | Clock display |
| 9 | Time of day, 24-hour | None | Clock display |

Programs and inputs are as given in the 2023 manual.[^manual] The two count-up programs have two
extra behaviors. A start input while the clock is running freezes the elapsed time for ten
seconds before the running time returns. A stop input received up to 9.99 seconds ahead of
the start is shown afterwards as a negative time, again for ten seconds.[^manual]

The qualifying timer works differently from the others. The manual describes it for cars on an oval track.
The first car through the photocell starts the clock, and every later pass shows that lap's time
for ten seconds while the next lap is timed. The photocell is ignored for three seconds after
each pass so that one car is not counted twice. When the run is over, STOP shows the best lap,
and the next pass through the beam begins the next driver's run.[^manual] RESET clears the
display and the stored best lap.[^manual]

None of the nine programs is a swimming program. The PC-2001's interval workout, its start,
backstroke and relay reaction timers and its shot clock are all absent.[^manual][^pc2001]

## Console and radio control

The TI-2022 needs no console. It can take a signal from an All Sport scoring console or an
[OmniSport 2000](../../swimming/timers/omnisport-2000.md) timing console on its SIGNAL IN jack,
and pass the signal on to further displays from SIGNAL OUT. Daktronics notes that a display
driven this way becomes an output device, not the self-contained timer it was designed as.[^manual]
For the signal run Daktronics recommends at least single-pair shielded 22 AWG cable (`W-1077`),
and prefers two-pair cable (`W-1614`).[^manual]

For radio control the console needs a transmitter, and the timer takes an optional
[Gen VI](../scoreboard-control/gen-vi-radio.md) receiver fitted inside the cabinet. The receiver
is fixed to the cabinet wall with hook-and-loop pads and plugged into the board's radio jack
through an adapter cable (`W-2913`). Its antenna passes through a hole in the side and must point
straight up in use. At power-on, its self test shows the receiver's broadcast and
channel settings, which must match the console's. If they match and control still fails, the
manual suggests interference from a neighboring radio-controlled display and a change of
settings at both ends.[^manual]

## Power

The timer runs from 12 V sealed lead-acid batteries with a built-in charger. Supply kits are
sold for 120 V and 240 V mains, the timer can be used while it charges, and the manual gives the
draw as 1 A.[^manual] Daktronics asks for a 24-hour charge before the timer is put away and warns
against running the battery flat.[^manual] Like the PC-2001, it switches itself off on battery
power after an hour without counting, switch presses or signal, except in the time-of-day
programs. It also switches off about a minute after a low-battery warning if it is not put on
charge.[^manual][^pc2001]

The manual is not consistent on several power details. Its power figure labels the inlet 12 V,
while the control-panel drawing reads 18V IN. The shared schematic records a change from 12 V to
18 V input in April 2023.[^manual] The safety notes promise at least ten hours from a full charge
and ask that mains power be used only when the battery is low. The power section gives about ten
hours for each battery and says the charger can stay plugged in indefinitely without harm.[^manual]

## Mounting

The timer can be wall-mounted on an optional bracket fixed to two anchors, which the manual
specifies as steel and at least 3/16 in (4.7 mm) in diameter. The display hangs on the bracket by
four screws in its sides, and it must not be hung permanently by its handle.[^manual] For use on
a stand, a tripod kit adds an adapter plate and bracket under the cabinet: the rubber feet come
off and go back on through the plate, and two knockouts in the base are pushed out. Daktronics says a camera tripod is not strong enough and sells a speaker-stand
tripod.[^manual]

## Care and maintenance

The front and rear panels are held by ten screws. Inside, the circuit board comes off its studs
after eight nuts are removed, and each battery sits in a bracket held by four nuts.[^manual]
Replacement batteries must be of the same type, wired red to positive and black to negative, and
the old lead-acid battery is to be recycled.[^manual] The exploded view in the manual labels the
board with two sets of part numbers: the TI-2022's own (`0P-1153-0002` indoor, `0P-1153-0004`
outdoor) and the PC-2001's (`0P-1153-0001` and `0P-1153-0003`). The parts table gives only the
TI-2022's.[^manual][^pc2001]
