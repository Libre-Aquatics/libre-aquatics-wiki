The Kyroscope pace clock was a programmable digital pace clock made by
[Taroda Industries](../../../vendors/taroda-industries.md) of Chicago under its Kyroscope brand.
It was first sold in 1974 through the dealer [Thrifty Timing](../../../vendors/thrifty-timing.md)
at $350. In place of the sweep hand of an analog clock it showed elapsed time in large digits,
and it could be set to run a sequence of intervals and count the repeats.[^thrifty74][^taroda75]
Taroda later sold it as the Model 2001 and Model 2001A, a clock for pacing, splits and game
timing, and advertised it until 1982.[^taroda78][^taroda80]

## Role in training

A pace clock lets swimmers in a workout read their own send-off and lap times; see the
[pace clocks overview](index.md) for the history of the class. The Kyroscope is the earliest digital pace
clock found in Swimming World's advertising. It differed from the sweep-hand
clocks of the period in being programmable: a coach set an interval and a number of repeats, and
the clock ran the set and displayed how many repeats were done.[^taroda75] By 1978 Taroda also
promoted it for timing water polo possession and game periods and for other sports.[^taroda78] The
US Merchant Marine Academy, given one in 1980, used it for both swimming and water polo.[^gift80]

## Design and operation

This section describes the clock as Taroda's advertisements present it.

The 1975 clock was a self-contained solid-state unit, 24 in long, 10-1/2 in high and 7 in deep,
weighing 25 lb. It had a carrying handle and a wall bracket, and its mains cord stored inside
the case.[^taroda75] Its display ran to 99 minutes 59 seconds. It could hold a preset program of
intervals or a sequence of intervals, repeat it from 1 to 99 times, and show the number of
repeats completed alongside the time.[^taroda75]

From late 1976 the clock gained a split function.[^taroda76] Taroda's advertisements disagree on
the digit height: a June 1976 advertisement gives 8 in, and the advertisements from November 1976
onward give 7 in.[^taroda7606][^taroda76]

number.

## Sales

Thrifty Timing of Eau Claire, Wisconsin, which described itself as a profit-sharing venture run by
coaches, swimmers and parents, was the first to advertise the clock, from October 1974. It also
sold Kyroscope timing systems.[^thrifty74] Taroda advertised the clock itself from April 1975 as
part of its Kyroscope line, which centered on the [Kyroscope](../../swimming/timers/kyroscope.md)
meet timer. Taroda offered bank financing and fund-raising help on the line, and from 1980 sold
the clock alongside the Kyroscope Mark II printing timer and the
[AH-1200](../../swimming/starter/kyroscope-ah-1200.md) horn start.[^taroda76][^taroda80] The last
Taroda advertisement found is from April 1982.[^taroda8204]
