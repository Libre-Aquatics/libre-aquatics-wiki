Research notes, multisport portable scoreboard.

Surfaced while building out the handheld segment timer, whose manual F1050 names portable
scoreboards as one of three display types it drives, alongside slim pace clocks and deck
clocks. Unlike those two, this one has no manual, no datasheet and no model number in the
collection, which is why the page stays at two sentences.

What is established, and from where.

The 2015 CTS catalogue
(sources/vendors/colorado-time-systems/start-systems/cts-complete-timing-scoring-training-and-display-solutions-2015-2015.txt)
carries a Multisport Portable Scoreboard entry in its water polo section: integrated 2.4 GHz
wireless with twelve channels and sixteen networks on each, a powder-coated aluminium
enclosure, conformal-coated digits and boards, 10 in digits for game time and for the home
and guest scores with 7 in digits for the period, designed to be worked from the tabletop or
handheld controller, a wired or wireless System 6 interface, usable with deck clocks, and an
optional caddy. No part number appears anywhere in the entry.

The WA-3 guide F1045 supplies the part-number rule that ties it to the rest of the family.
An MS- prefix is what marks a board as multisport, and F1045 puts three kinds of product
inside that class: the multisport mini scoreboards, the portable scoreboards and the deck
clocks. Where to look for the number is stated too, on the serial-number label, which CTS
places on the enclosure's right-hand side. So the product carries an MS- number; no held
document prints the number itself.

Three things this page must not be confused with, all of which use the words portable and
scoreboard.

The Portable Tabletop Scoreboards are a separate entry in the same water polo section of the
catalogue, closing p. 13 where this product opens p. 14, with 5 in digits rather than 10 in,
and an optional external horn.

The aquatic mini scoreboard, documented in F927 and covered at mini-scoreboard.md, is
repeatedly called portable in its own manual, meaning the variant with a battery, legs, a
handle and a caddy. It is a different product line and belongs to the aquatic boards, not
the MS- family.

The legacy light-reflective and LED portables P-1, P-3 and P-4, which appear in the

drove in the 1990s. They predate the 2.4 GHz equipment entirely.

This article is a stub. The multisport portable scoreboard is a Colorado Time Systems
portable board, offered with a caddy as an option, with 10 in digits for game time and the
two scores and 7 in digits for the period, worked over 2.4 GHz from a tabletop or handheld
controller, and one of the three display types a
[handheld segment timer](../pace-clock/whc-2.md) can run a workout on.

Its part number is not recorded in any source held for this wiki, beyond the `MS-` prefix
that marks the multisport family; see the [scoreboards overview](index.md) for the shared
background that applies to every CTS display.
