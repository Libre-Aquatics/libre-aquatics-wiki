The WP-2102 is a water polo penalty module made by [Daktronics](../../../vendors/daktronics.md).
It mounts directly below a [WP-2101](wp-2101.md) water polo scoreboard and cannot be used on its
own.[^manual][^spec] For one excluded player on each team it shows the player's cap number, the
time left on the exclusion and how many penalties that player has been given.[^spec][^drawing] It is run
by whichever console runs the WP-2101, normally an
[All Sport 5000](../scoreboard-control/all-sport-5000.md) or an
[OmniSport 2000](../../swimming/timers/omnisport-2000.md).[^manual][^spec] Daktronics lists the LED
version covered by its 2007 manual as made from 1999 to 2014 and still sells a module under the
same model number.[^discontinued][^product]

## Role in a water polo setup

This section explains what the module adds to a Daktronics water polo scoreboard. The
WP-2101 shows the game clock, scores, period, shot clock and timeouts, but has no fields for
excluded players.[^manual] The WP-2102 supplies them.

Under the current World Aquatics rules, an excluded player may return after 18 seconds of actual
play. Each exclusion or penalty foul counts as a personal foul against the player, and a third
personal foul excludes the player for the rest of the match.[^wacr] The module has a field for
each part of that record. The player field holds the cap number, the time field counts down the
exclusion, and the number field holds the player's penalty count, which the OmniSport 2000 caps at
three in line with the rule.[^drawing][^os2000][^wacr]

Both consoles can run more penalty times than the module can show. On the OmniSport 2000, as many
as three of a team's penalty times can run at once, and a player can be charged with at most
three penalties in a game.[^os2000] Daktronics' revision history records that All Sport 5000 software 2.1.0, released
in February 2005, added two driver addresses with three water polo penalty timers.[^revs] The
module itself has room for one player per team, and no Daktronics source says which running
penalty it shows when a team has more than one.[^spec][^drawing]

## Design and hardware

The cabinet is aluminum, 2 ft (0.61 m) high, 9 ft (2.74 m) wide and 6 in (0.15 m) deep, the same
width as the WP-2101 so that the two stack into one face.[^drawing][^spec] It weighs 70 lb
(32 kg) as mounted and 112 lb (51 kg) as shipped. Daktronics' specification gives its power as
200 W.[^drawing][^spec]

The face carries two identical groups of fields, one per team, each captioned PLYR, PENALTY TIME
and NO. There are no HOME or GUEST captions on the module itself.[^drawing]

| Field | Digits | Range |
|---|---|---|
| Player (cap number) | 2 | Two digits[^drawing] |
| Penalty time | 2 | Up to 99 seconds[^spec] |
| Penalty number | 1 | Up to 9[^spec] |

All ten digits are seven-segment digits driven by one LED driver.[^drawing] The 2002 drawings and
the 2007 manual give them as 7 in high. Daktronics' 2013 specification and current product page
give 8 in instead, as on the WP-2101, so the 7 in size appears to belong to the LED
series.[^drawing][^spec][^product]

## Control and wiring

This section covers how the module gets power and data. The module has no connection of its own
to the console. A cable with a 12-pin plug leaves the module through a hole in its top and plugs
into the WP-2101 above it, which carries both power and the console's signal to the
module.[^manual][^drawing] Its driver, transformer and signal terminal block sit behind the
left-hand penalty digits.[^manual]

The WP-2101's driver is set to address 13 and the WP-2102's to address 14, so the console can
send each board its own data.[^drawing][^wp2101drawing] Daktronics' specification states that no
separate controller is needed. The console that runs the WP-2101 also runs the module.[^spec]

## Penalty operation

This section summarizes how an operator puts a penalty on the module. On the All Sport 5000, the
operator presses a team's player penalty key, enters the cap number and the penalty number, and
the penalty time starts at once.[^as5000] Separate keys delete one penalty, chosen with the arrow
keys, or clear all of a team's penalties. Two more keys decide whether penalty times run with the
game clock.[^as5000] The OmniSport 2000 works the same way with its own keys. It also keeps
count of each player's penalties and lets the operator correct that count.[^os2000]

The All Sport 5000's default penalty time is 20 seconds.[^as5000] The current rules use 18
seconds, so for a game under those rules the default has to be changed in the console's
settings menu.[^wacr][^as5000]

## Installation

This section summarizes how the module is mounted. On a wall the WP-2102 is fixed to the
mounting struts first and the WP-2101 is then set on top of it. On outdoor beam mounts the lower
section is also attached first, and the two cabinets are bolted together at each end.[^manual]
The module's lifting eye is for installation only and has to come off before the WP-2101 is
fitted on top.[^drawing] The rear carries threaded inserts for pole mounting brackets.[^drawing]

## Care and maintenance

The module is serviced from the front like the WP-2101, through hinged digit panels, and a digit
with a failed LED is replaced as a whole panel.[^manual] The [WP-2101](wp-2101.md) article covers
the shared troubleshooting and parts list.

## Part numbers and accessories

The 2007 manual lists the module's parts together with the WP-2101's: the LED driver
`0P-1150-0127`, the 16 V transformer `T-1066`, and 7 in digit panels in red (`0P-1150-0187`) and
amber (`0P-1150-0082`) for indoor use, or red-orange (`0P-1150-0133`) and amber (`0P-1192-0104`)
for outdoor use.[^manual][^drawing] The full parts list is on the [WP-2101](wp-2101.md) page.
