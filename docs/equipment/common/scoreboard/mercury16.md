The Mercury16 is a discontinued LED matrix display made by
[Colorado Time Systems](../../../vendors/colorado-time-systems.md) (CTS). Besides scores and
times it can show text, graphics and animation. CTS announced it in 1996 as a new series of
indoor full-matrix boards, each sold with the company's
[DisplayLink](../../../software/displaylink.md) software.[^sw9604] Every pixel is a cluster of
diodes in a single color, and CTS gave the board 16 shades, which is where the name comes
from.[^webmerc] A red-only outdoor version, the Outdoor Sun Series Mercury16, was sold for
stadiums and outdoor pools.[^webmerc][^weboutdoor] The two-color [Myriad](myriad.md), introduced
the following year, shares its manual, modules and software.[^f853][^sw9701]

## Role in the facility

This section places the Mercury16 in a CTS installation; the architecture common to every CTS
matrix display is covered in the [scoreboards overview](index.md). The Mercury16 is an output
device. A dedicated computer running DisplayLink takes results from a CTS timing console or a
meet-management program and lays them out on the board, alongside graphics, messages and
animations.[^f837][^sw9604] CTS marketed the board on that flexibility. Its product page showed the
same 64 × 40 board laid out for three different sports, and suggested that selling advertising
space on it could cover its cost.[^webmerc]

## Models

This section covers the two forms CTS sold. The manuals and the web pages name them
differently.[^f853][^f837][^webmerc]

| | Mercury16 | Outdoor Sun Series Mercury16 |
|---|---|---|
| Use | Indoor | Outdoor, readable in direct sun[^weboutdoor] |
| LED color | Red or green[^webmerc] | Red[^webmerc] |
| Diodes per pixel | Four[^webmerc] | Five[^webmerc] |
| Shades | 16[^webmerc] | 16[^webmerc] |
| Operating temperature | 0–50 °C[^f853] | −10 to 70 °C[^f853] |

CTS called the outdoor board Outdoor Mercury16 in its manuals and Outdoor Sun Series Mercury 16 on
its website. The specification table in the manual heads it Mercury16 Sun Series.[^f853][^f837][^webmerc]
No source says outright that these are one product. They share the five-red-diode pixel and the
wider temperature rating, and are treated as one here. The aquatic product page sold both
boards. A separate page for the outdoor board sat in the stadium section of the CTS
site.[^webmerc][^weboutdoor]

## Design and hardware

This section covers how the board is built. A pixel is a cluster of LEDs. Clusters sit on a
circuit board 8 pixels square and about a foot on a side, conformal-coated against pool air or
weather.[^webmerc][^weboutdoor] Four of these boards make a module of 32 × 8 pixels, about
4 ft wide and 1 ft high. Modules are joined to any rectangle a facility needs, so a board's size
is given in pixels across by pixels high rather than as a model number.[^webmerc] The 8 × 8
boards are made to be replaced individually.[^webmerc]

Each module, with its share of the Unistrut mounting frame, weighs about 15 lb. A module draws
2.5 A at 115 V or 1.3 A at 230 V. The manual budgets 2 A per box and no more than four boxes across
on one circuit, so a wider board needs more circuits.[^f853] Data arrives over an Ethernet link from the DisplayLink
computer, and an RS-232 port takes a scoreboard feed.[^f853] The manual calls for a
ground-fault-protected circuit for any outdoor or poolside installation.[^f853]

These figures are common to the Mercury16 and the Myriad, and the [Myriad](myriad.md) article
covers the mounting frame, circuit planning and service procedures in more detail.

## Software

DisplayLink came with every Mercury16.[^sw9604][^webmerc] The 1996 advertisements called it a
Windows program written by CTS for graphics and animation. They said it worked with CTS timing
equipment and with meet-management software from [Hy-Tek](../../../vendors/hy-tek.md) and,
from September 1996, from [LSA](../../../software/lsa-swimmeet.md).[^sw9604][^sw9609] CTS sold
four additions for the board:[^webmerc]

- [ScheduleLink](../../../software/schedulelink.md), for running advertisements and messages
  to a timetable
- the [GameLink console](../scoreboard-control/gamelink-console.md), so more than one person
  can keep score
- a [template editor](../../../software/standalone-template-editor.md), for arranging where
  names, times and graphics appear
- libraries of ready-made animations

The [DisplayLink](../../../software/displaylink.md) article covers the program itself.

## History and marketing

This section traces the line through CTS's own advertising, which is the only dated source. In
April 1996 CTS advertised a new series of indoor full-matrix LED displays under the name
Mercury.[^sw9604] The two-color Myriad followed in January 1997. From March 1997 the
advertisements offered the two boards together.[^sw9701][^sw9703] The name Mercury 16 appears
in CTS advertising from early 1999.[^st9901] That year's copy described the Mercury 16 and
Myriad together as multi-color displays.[^sw9903] That wording sits awkwardly with the
single-color pixel CTS described on its own website, and is the company's advertising
phrase.[^webmerc]

The product pages were still online in late 2004.[^webocust] CTS's 2004 DisplayLink 3.6 guide
names the Mercury16 and the Outdoor Mercury16, while the later guides of the same revision speak
only of matrix display systems.[^f837] By the 2008 catalogue CTS's matrix line was a different
product, and the [Myriad](myriad.md) article sets out the evidence for when both boards left the
catalogue.

## Compared with the Myriad

The Mercury16 and the Myriad share a manual, module size, mounting method, power figures and
control software.[^f853] They differ in the pixel. The Mercury16 uses four diodes of one color
for 16 shades, or five red diodes outdoors. The Myriad mixes four red and five green diodes for
what CTS called 256 shades.[^webmerc][^webmyriad] The Mercury16 came first, by about nine months,
and was the only one of the two sold in an outdoor version.[^sw9604][^sw9701][^webmerc]

## Part numbers and accessories

No part number for a Mercury16 board or module has been found. CTS quoted boards by size in
pixels.[^webmerc] The manual names the Unistrut framework, the 8 × 8 boards and their faceplates,
vinyl-clad trim, and the optional [HWPM 110 and HWPM 220](hwpm.md).[^f853]
