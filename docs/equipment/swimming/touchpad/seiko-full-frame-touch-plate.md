The full-frame touch plate is a swimming touch panel made by
[Seiko](../../../vendors/seiko.md) Time Creation Inc. of Tokyo, sold as the
1,900 mm full-frame type. It senses a swimmer's finish and turn at the lane end and
converts the touch into an electrical signal that reaches the printing timer through
that lane's lane box, the same role the company's larger
[AQUA touch plate](seiko-aqua-touch-plate.md) fills.[^jp][^catalogue] It is the
narrower and much shallower of the two, and Seiko certifies it for Japanese domestic
competition only: it carries a Japan Swimming Federation Class A listing with no World
Aquatics compliance line.[^jp][^catalogue]

## Role in the timing system

The [touchpads overview](index.md) covers the history of automatic touchpad timing and
the World Aquatics requirements that apply to every touch panel. This plate reports to
a lane box, one per lane, which sorts that lane's signals and passes them to the
`PT-9000` printing timer at the head of Seiko's competitive swimming
system.[^catalogue][^lanebox][^pt9000]

Its certification sets it apart from the rest of that system. Seiko lists the starting
block, the backstroke ledge, the lane box, the AQUA touch plate, the printing timer and
the [PS-1400 start sound generator](../starter/seiko-ps-1400.md) as conforming to World
Aquatics rules and as Japan Swimming Federation Class AA and Class A. This plate is
listed as Class A alone.[^catalogue][^jp] The company does not say why. World Aquatics
facilities rules set a minimum touch panel width of 2.4 m, and this panel is
1,900 mm.[^frules][^jp]

## Naming and model numbers

Seiko publishes no model number for this plate in Japanese. The product page and the
swimming catalogue both call it タッチプレート（1,900mmフルフレームタイプ）, "touch
plate (1,900 mm full-frame type)", and give a four-row specification table with no part
number on it.[^jp][^catalogue] The name distinguishes it from the AQUA type, but Seiko
does not explain what the frame consists of.

Seiko's English swimming pages do carry model numbers, `TP-3000` and `TP-3019`, for a
pair of framed touch pads with an aluminium and stainless-steel frame and a PVC front
panel finished in a yellow resin fine-particle coating, rated in accordance with FINA
rules. The `TP-3019` is the narrower of that pair at 1980 W × 917 H × 68 D mm and about
20 kg, with a `TF-40` storage cart.[^en] That framed construction is closer to this
plate than to the AQUA type, but none of the figures match the current Japanese ones:
this plate is given as 1,900 W × 900 H × 15 D mm and about 15 kg, on a high-impact resin
panel.[^jp][^catalogue]

Seiko's English swimming section has been only partly updated, so its pages cannot be
read as a single generation. The `PS-1400` starter page there is current and cites World
Aquatics rules, while the touch pad, lane box and printing timer pages still cite FINA
rules, still name a `PT-8000X` timer where the Japanese pages name the `PT-9000`, and
still power the lane box from a
`PT-8000`.[^en][^enps1400][^swimidx][^enlanebox][^pt9000] This article treats the
Japanese product page and the December 2025 catalogue as current and does not apply the
`TP-3019` number to this plate, because Seiko publishes no statement connecting the two.

## Design and construction

Seiko gives the surface panel material as a high-impact resin board, the same material
as the AQUA plate, the plate's overall dimensions as 1,900 W × 900 H × 15 D mm, and its
weight as about 15 kg.[^jp][^catalogue] The sensing area is 10 mm thick. That figure
comes from the catalogue alone: the product page's table runs to three rows, and its
sensing-surface row carries the weight where the catalogue gives a
thickness.[^catalogue][^jp] The 900 mm
height and the 10 mm sensing thickness match the figures the World Aquatics facilities
rules give for a touch panel; the width does not.[^frules]

Seiko labels that row 感動面 in the catalogue and on both product pages, where the
expected term is 感応面, "sensing surface". The spelling is consistent across its
material, so a search on the correct term will not find these
specifications.[^jp][^ff][^catalogue] Catalogue photographs show it
finished in yellow with a dark cross marking at the centre.[^catalogue]

The 15 mm overall depth leaves only 5 mm beyond the sensing area, so this plate is close
to a flat panel, where the AQUA type's 77.5 mm maximum depth implies a substantial frame
or bracket behind the face.[^jp][^ff] Seiko does not publish the number of switches or
sensing zones behind the face, the activation force, or an operating temperature
range.[^jp][^catalogue]

## Sensing and wave rejection

Seiko uses the same sentence for this plate as for the AQUA type, describing it as
detecting only the swimmer's touch, without responding to water pressure or splashes,
and converting that touch instantly into an electrical signal sent to the printing
timer.[^jp][^catalogue] The company publishes no sensing principle for either plate. The
[AQUA touch plate](seiko-aqua-touch-plate.md) article discusses a 1995 Seiko Instruments
patent that addresses the same discrimination problem, and the
[touchpads overview](index.md) sets that approach alongside the air-filled and
conductive-plate designs other manufacturers use.

## Mounting

Seiko does not publish the plate's mounting method, its fixing hardware, or whether it
hangs from the gutter or fixes to a flat wall, and no installation document exists:
the company's manual download index lists no touch plate, and its swimming category
holds only the `SB-5010` and `SB-5020` scoreboards.[^jp][^catalogue][^manualidx]

Handling off the wall is documented. A steel storage cart takes ten full-frame plates,
measures 2,100 W × 1,548 H × 849 D mm including its wheels and excluding protrusions,
and weighs about 130 kg empty.[^catalogue][^cart] It is the heavier of Seiko's two touch
plate carts, holding ten plates where the AQUA cart holds six. Portability is consistent
with the facilities rules, which allow a touch panel to be removable so it can be taken
out when no race is being held.[^frules]

## Connection to the timer

The plate wires to its lane box rather than to the timer. The lane box takes the signals
from that lane's starting block and touch plate, sorts them by lane, and passes them to
the printing timer; it is an ABS case in dark grey measuring 170 W × 50 H × 100 D mm
excluding protrusions, weighing 450 g with a 230 g cable of 3.7 m, and it uses waterproof
connectors.[^lanebox] Timekeepers' grip switches feed the same box as a manual
backup.[^grip] Seiko does not publish the connector type on the plate itself, the plate's
own cable length, or a pin assignment.

## Compared with the AQUA touch plate

The two plates share their stated function and their panel material; the description of
what they do is word for word the same on both product pages.[^jp][^ff] They differ in
size, depth, weight, certification and storage.

| | Full-frame type | AQUA type |
|---|---|---|
| Width | 1,900 mm | 2,400 mm |
| Height | 900 mm | 900 mm |
| Depth | 15 mm | 77.5 mm (maximum) |
| Sensing surface | 10 mm thick | 10 mm thick |
| Weight | Approx. 15 kg | Approx. 22 kg |
| Panel material | High-impact resin board | High-impact resin board |
| Certifications | Japan Swimming Federation Class A | World Aquatics rules; Japan Swimming Federation Class AA and Class A |
| Storage cart | 10 plates, approx. 130 kg | 6 plates, approx. 40 kg |

The practical distinction is where each may be used. Only the AQUA plate meets the World
Aquatics minimum panel width, and only the AQUA plate carries the company's World
Aquatics compliance line and the Class AA listing.[^frules][^jp][^ff] Against that, this
plate is about 7 kg lighter and far shallower, at 15 mm against 77.5 mm, and its cart
carries ten panels rather than six.[^jp][^ff][^catalogue]

## Part numbers and accessories

- Touch plate (1,900 mm full-frame type), sold without a published part number in
  Seiko's Japanese material.
- Touch plate storage cart for the full-frame type, ten plates, also unnumbered.
- Lane box, one per lane, carrying the plate's signal and the starting block's to the
  printing timer.
- `TP-3019`, the model number Seiko's older English pages give for a 1980 mm framed
  touch pad, with a `TF-40` storage cart. Seiko does not state how it relates to this
  plate.
