The AquaGrip touchpad is a swimming touch panel made by
[Colorado Time Systems](../../../vendors/colorado-time-systems.md) (CTS).
It is the panel hung at a lane end that senses the swimmer's finish and turn: a
touch closes an internal switch and sends the timing signal that stops that lane's
clock on the [timing console](../timers/index.md). AquaGrip is CTS's current
touchpad line, distinguished by a patented non-slip surface texture; it uses the
same all-plastic, air-filled body CTS has built since 1972 and replaces the earlier
Standard touchpad. A single user guide (F147) covers both.[^aquagrip][^datasheet][^f147]

## Role in the timing system

The [touchpads overview](index.md) covers the history of automatic touchpad timing and
the World Aquatics requirements that apply to every touch panel. The AquaGrip pad
connects through a cable harness or in-deck plates back to the timing console, which
measures the elapsed time and drives the scoreboard; on the CTS Gen7 platform the
touchpads are supplied as lane systems that pair with the
[Gen7 timer](../timers/gen7-serial.md).[^f147]

Because the panel doubles as the wall a swimmer pushes off during backstroke starts
and turns, its face has to give reliable grip when wet. CTS describes the AquaGrip
surface as responding to a very light touch while giving a firm push-off.[^datasheet]

## Design and construction

The pad is of all-plastic construction built on a solid quarter-inch plastic
backbone, with molded one-piece connectors and a non-corrosive finish.[^datasheet]
Its face carries internally sealed switches across the fully active front, and
gutter-mounted models add a switch along the top edge, so a touch anywhere on the
face or top edge registers with no dead spots.[^datasheet]

The distinguishing feature of the AquaGrip line is its surface texture. CTS covers it
with a patented ultra-fine non-slip texture: US Patent 5,702,799, "Slip resistant
texture for wet skin contact surfaces," describes a texture of particles under about
100 microns applied to a water-resistant sheet, which the inventors found gives
unexpected grip on wet skin while still feeling relatively smooth.[^patent799] The
patent, filed in 1995 and granted in 1997, was assigned to CTS and is now held by its
parent, Everlast Climbing Industries.[^patent799] The Standard touchpad uses CTS's
older textured non-skid surface without this treatment; otherwise the two share the
same body and manual.[^standard][^f147]

## Pneumatic operation and sensitivity

The touchpad is an air-filled panel, and its sensitivity is set by the amount of air
inside it. A typical competition setup activates at an internal pressure of about
25 psi (172 kPa).[^f147] Because that pressure acts over the contact area, a small
fingertip touch triggers the pad more easily than a flat palm; a correctly inflated
pad feels firm, "similar to pushing your fingers into a thick magazine," while an
over-inflated pad feels spongy and may float or lose sensitivity.[^f147] Too little
air, or excess internal vacuum, leaves the pad shorted so it reports a false finish
soon after the start.[^f147]

Operators tune a pad out of the water using a needle to add air and a vacuum pump to
release it, working against a test meter until the switch reads correctly; the pad
must be dry at the cable end during the adjustment, because water drawn inside will
ruin it.[^f147] This pneumatic design is how the panel meets the governing-body
sensitivity requirement described in the [touchpads overview](index.md): the pad must
not be triggered by water turbulence but must respond to a light hand touch, and stay
sensitive on the top edge.[^frules]

## Mounting

Gutter-mounted pads hang over the pool's gutter and are held by a mounting system
that needs no holes in the deck. With Velcro link brackets the pads install without
tools: a bracket is laid on the gutter between each pair of lanes, and each pad is
eased into place and pressed down to bond, with end brackets between the two outside
pads and the pool corners to stop the pads shifting sideways during a meet.[^f147]
Pads with a mounting hole in the top edge instead hang from separate brackets.[^f147]

Flat-wall pads bolt to the deck. Two stainless-steel brackets are fixed to each pad,
the pad is set flush against the wall and centred in the lane, and holes are drilled
in the deck for stainless expansion anchors that the brackets bolt down to.[^f147]
CTS notes that flat-wall pads sit against a vertical wall rather than hanging from a
gutter, so a facility chooses the version that matches its pool. A meet can run with the
pads left in place throughout, though the manual advises lifting them out whenever the
pool reverts to general swimming among people who do not know what they are.[^f147]

## Connection to the timer

Each pad has a 10-foot cable ending in a dual banana plug.[^datasheet] The plug
connects to a cable-harness pod or to an in-deck plate, which routes the lane's
signals back to the timing console; the pads run on under 5 VDC of deck power that is
electronically isolated at the timer.[^datasheet] The timer arms every lane at the
start, and each armed pad stops its own lane's clock at the finish, with a pushbutton
provided as the backup input required for fully automatic officiating.[^f147][^frules]

CTS supplies a touchpad test meter that shows a pad's open or closed state on a bar
graph and also tests pushbuttons, the cable harness, deck plates, and the scoreboard;
harness and deck-plate connections should read in the 4.5 to 5 V range.[^f147] The
two-pronged banana plug has to be kept free of corrosion and can be cleaned or, if
badly corroded, replaced (CTS part `R-025-045`).[^f147]

## Care and maintenance

CTS treats the touchpad as electronic equipment whose life depends on regular
cleaning. The manual's guidance includes: never lift or handle a pad by its cable;
keep gutter-mounted pads clear of the lane-line hardware; do not puncture the front
of the pad, since even a pinhole lets water in and destroys it, and use the venting
needle only in the orange plug; and keep pads out of direct sunlight when out of the
water, which can warp the pad or lift the AquaGrip surface.[^f147]

Connectors are cleaned with isopropyl alcohol, cotton pipe cleaners, and a light coat
of dielectric grease, flushed with fresh (not pool) water; lime or calcium build-up
is removed with a diluted calcium-lime-rust cleaner, which the manual warns must not
be used as a routine cleaner or on wall plates and multi-pin connectors.[^f147] Pads
should be stored between about 50 and 110 °F, away from traffic and hard objects, and
a pad that has become cold should soak in warm pool water for 15 to 30 minutes before
use; CTS sells caddies that each hold up to ten pads for storage and transport.[^f147][^datasheet]

## Compared with the Standard touchpad

The AquaGrip and Standard touchpads are the same panel with different surfaces. Both
use the all-plastic body, quarter-inch backbone, internally sealed full-face and
top-edge switches, 10-foot cable and dual banana plug, and the same pneumatic
operation and F147 manual.[^f147][^standard] AquaGrip adds the patented non-slip
surface texture; the Standard pad uses the plain textured non-skid surface and is now
discontinued.[^aquagrip][^standard] A facility replacing worn Standard pads, or
equipping a new pool, buys the AquaGrip version in the same sizes and part-number
scheme.
