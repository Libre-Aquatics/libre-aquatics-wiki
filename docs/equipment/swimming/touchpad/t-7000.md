The T-7000 series is a line of swimming touch panels made by Daktronics, Inc., of
Brookings, South Dakota. It is the panel hung at a lane end that senses the swimmer's
finish and turn: a touch signals the [timing console](../timers/index.md) to stop
that lane's clock. Daktronics pairs the series with the OmniSport 2000 timing
console.[^manual][^support] The panel differs from
the [air-filled Colorado Time Systems pad](aquagrip.md) in how it senses a touch: it
is built from three conductive stainless-steel plates and reads a touch as a change in
electrical charge rather than air pressure.[^support][^manual] Daktronics lists the
T-7000 series both on an active touchpad support page and among its discontinued
product resources, so a facility may encounter these pads described either way.[^support][^discontinued]

## Role in the timing system

The [touchpads overview](index.md) covers the history of automatic touchpad timing and
the World Aquatics requirements that apply to every touch panel. A Daktronics pad
connects through a cable to a lane module or in-deck plate, which routes the lane's
signal back to the OmniSport 2000 console; the console measures the elapsed time and
drives the scoreboard.[^manual] Because the panel doubles as the wall a swimmer
pushes off during backstroke starts and turns, its face is textured to give grip when
wet, and Daktronics warns that paddles, fins, and kickboards must not be used against a
pad in the water.[^support][^manual]

## Design and construction

The touchpad is built from three conductive plates of non-corrosive stainless steel: a
back sheet, a center sheet, and a front plate, separated so a touch on the face brings
the layers together electrically.[^support][^manual] The front carries a non-slip,
non-abrasive textured surface, and the pad is designed to be self-draining, with drain
holes on the back sheet.[^support][^manual] A rubber boot runs along the top front bend,
and snap-on vinyl edge covers protect the sides and bottom over raised stainless-steel
edge protectors.[^manual] Each pad is stamped with a manufacture date and serial number
in the lower-right corner of the rear.[^manual]

A Daktronics pad is designed to fill with water once lowered into the pool, which
weights it so that it hugs the pool wall; the drain holes let it fill and drain rather
than float.[^manual] The face is 0.3 in (8 mm) thick on every model, within the World
Aquatics thickness limit noted in the [touchpads overview](index.md).[^manual][^frules]

## Electrical operation and sensitivity

The pad works by holding a small standing voltage that a touch collapses. With the pad
installed and untouched, about 0.25 VDC is measured between its positive and ground
connectors; when the face is pressed the voltage drops to approximately 0 VDC, which is
the signal the timer reads as a touch.[^manual] After the press is released the voltage
climbs back over roughly 1.5 to 3 seconds and levels out at 0.25 VDC again, so the pad
needs a moment to recharge before it can register the next touch. Daktronics' own test
procedure has the operator count "1001, 1002, 1003" between touches for this
reason.[^manual]

Sensitivity depends on how the pad is touched. Daktronics notes that a touch must be
delivered with the energy a swimmer carries into the wall; a press that is too light or
too slow may not register.[^manual] This is how the panel meets the governing-body
sensitivity requirement described in the [touchpads overview](index.md).[^frules] The
charge-based, layered-plate approach is one of the two dominant touchpad designs; it
contrasts with the air-filled pneumatic panel Colorado Time Systems uses, where
sensitivity is set by internal air pressure.[^support][^aquagrip]

The pad terminates in a cable with a dual banana plug, red for the positive connector
and black for ground. Its ground tab has to seat against the black socket on the lane
module or deck plate. Reverse a few pads in a system and they begin registering touches
that never happened.[^manual]

## Models and dimensions

The T-7000 series comes in two families. The T-7060, T-7078, and T-7096 are shorter
pads, 22 to 24 in (0.56–0.61 m) high, with a 2.8 in (71 mm) top flange. The FT-7150,
FT-7190, FT-7190T, and FT-7240T are full-height pads, 3 ft (0.9 m) high, tall enough to
span the water surface as the governing-body rules require; the plain FT models have a
2 in (51 mm) flange and the wedge-profile models ending in "T" have a 3.9 in (99 mm)
flange.[^manual] The face thickness is 0.3 in (8 mm) on all models; the depth figures
below are for the top flange.[^manual]

Aquatics competition minimum set out in the [touchpads overview](index.md), while the
narrower or shorter models suit narrower lanes and training pools.[^frules][^manual]

## Mounting

Each pad ships with a universal adjustable stainless-steel bracket that clamps to the
pool gutter and adjusts to both shallow and narrow-lip gutters; the brackets are secured
with a 7/16 in socket wrench.[^manual] The pad is lowered into the pool by one person at
each end, centered in the lane, and left to fill with water so it hugs the wall. If the
pool has no gutter, the pad is instead tied to the starting block with rope run through
the holes along its top edge.[^manual]

Where the pool wall has obstructions or the gutter protrudes, Daktronics has installers
add spacers (for example strips of PVC board) between the wall and the pad, bonded with
3M VHB tape, taking care not to cover the drain holes; the company does not supply these
spacers.[^manual] The pad connects either to an on-deck system, where its plug goes to
the touchpad jack on a lane module, or to an in-deck system, where it plugs into the
touchpad jack on a lane deck plate set into the deck.[^manual]

## Connection to the timer

Daktronics touchpads report to the [OmniSport 2000](../timers/omnisport-2000.md) timing console.[^manual][^omnisport]
Swimming mode covers a ten-lane pool, taking two pads in each lane, one at either end for
near-end and far-end splits, and reading them alongside
[relay take-off platforms](../relay-judging/index.md) and anywhere from one to three
backup buttons in the lane.[^support] The console reaches
a computer through Daktronics Pro Swimming software, which gathers touchpad, button,
relay-exchange, and start-reaction times over a serial or Ethernet
connection.[^prosoftware] Each pad is tested through the console's lane-module test
menu, which flashes a lane indicator for about two seconds when the pad is touched, and
the operator repeats the touch across the whole face to confirm there are no dead
spots.[^manual]

## Care and maintenance

After each meet Daktronics has the pad rinsed with non-chlorinated tap water, ideally by
submerging it in a fresh-water tank; it warns against using a pressure washer or treated
pool water.[^manual] A long event interrupts that cycle every second day: the pads come
out, are inspected, and dry for roughly six hours before going back in.[^manual] Inspection covers the cable
and banana plug for cuts and corrosion, the vinyl edge covers and top boot, and the face
for delamination, which shows as large bubbles or a loose surface.[^manual] Between meets
the pads are stored out of the pool environment and away from chemicals on a wheeled
touchpad cart, at 32 to 90 °F (0 to 32 °C) with fresh air circulation.[^manual]
