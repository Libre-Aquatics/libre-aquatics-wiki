Multizone Pads is a name Colorado Time Systems (CTS) gave to a swimming touch panel in
its 2008 catalogue. The company listed it among its training tools and never described
it: no part number, size, price or photograph accompanies the name, in that catalogue or
in any other source consulted for this article.[^mtc2008] By the 2011 catalogue the
name is gone.[^cat2011] No evidence has been found that the product was built or sold.

## The 2008 catalogue listing

CTS's Making Time Count catalogue, published in 2008, opens its Training Tools section
with a strip naming what the section covers. Six items appear there, and Multizone Pads
is one of them, alongside the Pace Clock Pro, the QuickStart Pro, relay judging
platforms, touchpads and an underwater speaker.[^mtc2008]

The section that follows covers the QuickStart Pro at length, down to its display size,
weight, power and the contents of its one-lane bundle, and gives the underwater speaker
its own entry. It never returns to the Multizone Pads. The name appears once in the
32-page document and nowhere else in it.[^mtc2008]

## A contemporaneous patent application

This section sets out an inference rather than a documented connection, because it is the
only candidate explanation the record offers.

On 23 January 2008 CTS filed a patent application titled "Aquatic Event Timer Apparatus
and Methods", published the following year as US 2009/0185455 A1 and assigned to the
company on the day it was filed.[^app455] The application describes
two or more independent touch-actuated switches, each 16–58 in wide and 1–3 ft high,
held side by side on a structural connector and fitted inside a single regulation lane
of 5½–9 ft, sitting at the lane end and at least partly underwater. Each switch carries
a visual design a swimmer can distinguish from the one next to it, the assembly takes
either gutter or flat-wall mounting hardware, and it wires back to a timer. The stated
problem is that a competition touchpad fills more than half a lane, so swimmers sharing
a lane in training cannot each have automatic timing.[^app455]

The name Multizone Pads describes a lane divided into separately timed areas, and the
application comes from the same company in the same month as the catalogue carrying that
name. The application was abandoned on 26 August 2011, after CTS did not
respond to an office action, in the same period the name disappeared from the
catalogues.[^app455][^cat2011] Against that, the application never uses the word
multizone and names no product, so nothing in the documents themselves ties the two
together.

## What Colorado Time Systems sold instead

The training pad CTS did sell is a smaller version of its ordinary competition panel
rather than a divided one. The Train Right package pairs a pace clock, a pushbutton, a
relay judging platform and a start system with a 30-inch "mini" touchpad, or a
76 × 90 cm pad on the 220 V versions, as `TR-SYS-1` and `TR-SYS-2`.[^trainright] By 2011
that package had taken the Multizone Pads' place in the Training Tools list.[^cat2011]
For competition CTS sold the [Standard touchpad](standard.md) and, from 1998, the
[AquaGrip touchpad](aquagrip.md), both full-lane panels with a single active face.

## Documentation status

The record for this product is one line in one catalogue. There is no CTS manual, form
number, datasheet, product page or press release for it, and no part number has been
found, which leaves its size, construction, sensing method and price unrecorded. Beyond
the single catalogue mention it appears in no other CTS document and nowhere in the
swimming press. No dealer listing names it.
A trademark search was attempted and could not be completed, so whether CTS ever
registered the name is unresolved.[^mtc2008]

Absence of a record is not proof that nothing was built. A product CTS named in a
catalogue left no other trace in the sources available here, and the company's
training-tool listing had replaced it within three years.[^cat2011]
