The Standard touchpad is a swimming touch panel made by
[Colorado Time Systems](../../../vendors/colorado-time-systems.md) (CTS). It hangs at
a lane end, and a swimmer's touch closes an internal switch that stops that lane's
clock on the [timing console](../timers/index.md). Standard was the plainer of the two
surfaces CTS offered on a common touchpad body, the other being the
[AquaGrip touchpad](aquagrip.md); CTS lists the Standard pad as discontinued and now
sells the AquaGrip surface alone.[^standard][^legacy] A single user guide, F147, still
covers both pads.[^f147]

## Role in the timing system

The [touchpads overview](index.md) covers how automatic touchpad timing developed and
the World Aquatics requirements that apply to any touch panel. A Standard pad reaches
the console the same way every CTS pad does, through a cable harness or an in-deck
plate, and the console measures the elapsed time and drives the
scoreboard.[^f147][^arch]

## Design and construction

The Standard pad's face carries CTS's textured non-skid finish, which the company
describes as gentle on hands and feet. It does not have the ultra-fine slip-resistant
texture of US Patent 5,702,799, which is what defines the AquaGrip
line.[^datasheet][^patent799] CTS presented the two as alternative finishes available
on most of its touchpads rather than as separate products.[^datasheet]

The rest of the pad is common to both surfaces. It is all-plastic throughout and runs
about 0.30 in (0.76 cm) thick, stiffened by a quarter-inch backbone; its connectors are
molded as single pieces and its finish resists corrosion.[^datasheet][^arch] Sealed
switching runs across the whole front, with a second switch along the top edge on
gutter-hung models, which CTS describes as leaving the face active throughout with no
dead spots.[^datasheet] F147 identifies the sensing element as a tape switch, the
top-edge run of which sits on the pad's right-angle corner; the manual notes that a
switch dented or shifted by rough handling can sometimes be worked back into shape by
hand.[^f147]

## Pneumatic operation and sensitivity

The Standard pad is air-filled, and how hard it is to trigger depends on how much air
is inside. CTS puts a typical competition setting at around 25 psi (172 kPa), and
because that pressure works against the area being pressed, a fingertip sets the pad
off more readily than an open palm.[^f147] Operators trim sensitivity out of the water
with a needle and a vacuum pump while watching a test meter. The
[AquaGrip touchpad](aquagrip.md) article covers that procedure and its failure modes
in full, since it is identical for both pads, and the
[touchpads overview](index.md) covers the governing-body sensitivity rule the design
answers.[^f147][^frules]

## Models and dimensions

Standard part numbers are the AquaGrip numbers without the `G`: a gutter-hung pad is
`TP-` plus its size, and a flat-wall pad adds `F`. CTS published nine sizes, in metric
widths for international-style pools and imperial widths for American
ones.[^standard][^legacy]

| Size | Part number | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| 188.5 × 90 cm | `TP-188.5` / `TP-188.5F` | 47 lb (21.3 kg) |
| 190 × 90 cm | `TP-190` / `TP-190F` | 49 lb (22.2 kg) |
| 195 × 90 cm | `TP-195` / `TP-195F` | 49 lb (22.2 kg) |
| 240 × 90 cm | `TP-240` / `TP-240F` | 66 lb (30 kg) |
| 60 × 22 in (152.4 × 55.9 cm) | `TP-60` | 28 lb (12.7 kg) |
| 60 × 24 in (152.4 × 61.0 cm) | `TP-60F` | 33 lb (15 kg) |
| 78 × 22 in (198.1 × 55.9 cm) | `TP-78` | 35 lb (15.9 kg) |
| 78 × 24 in (198.1 × 61.0 cm) | `TP-78F` | 40 lb (18.1 kg) |
| 96 × 22 in (243.8 × 55.9 cm) | `TP-96` | 45 lb (20.4 kg) |

The imperial pads differ in depth by mounting type: 22 in for the gutter-hung version
and 24 in for the flat-wall one. The 96-inch pad was sold gutter-hung only, with no
flat-wall counterpart, and custom widths were available between the catalogued
sizes.[^standard][^arch] CTS recommended the 60-inch pad for lanes 66–84 in wide and
the 78-inch pad for lanes 84–96 in.[^legacy]

The Standard line also included a 190 cm pad, `TP-190` and `TP-190F`, which has no
equivalent in the AquaGrip range: the AquaGrip datasheet lists 188.5, 195 and 240 cm,
with nothing between the first two.[^datasheet][^legacy] CTS's current catalogue does
not carry that size at all, and instead lists a 90-inch Standard gutter-hung pad,
`TP-90`, that neither product page includes.[^catalog] The
larger metric pads meet the World Aquatics minimum panel size given in the
[touchpads overview](index.md), and CTS's own facility guidance labels the 195 cm and
240 cm sizes by that standard.[^arch][^frules]

## Mounting

Gutter-hung pads rest over the pool's gutter and are held by brackets sized to the
gutter profile, in polycarbonate with Velcro facing, which need no holes in the deck
and no tools to fit.[^f147][^arch] Flat-wall pads instead carry a stainless-steel
bracket at each end and bolt to the deck on expansion anchors.[^f147] Either version
can stay in the water for a whole meet, which saves re-measuring the course, though
F147 advises taking the pads out when a shared pool returns to general
swimming.[^f147][^arch] The [AquaGrip touchpad](aquagrip.md) article gives the
step-by-step for both methods; they do not vary by surface.

## Connection to the timer

Each pad ends in a 10-foot cable with a molded connector and a dual banana plug, which
goes either into a cable-harness pod or into a lane's deck plate.[^datasheet][^f147]
Deck power is under 5 VDC and is electronically isolated at the timer.[^datasheet] A
harness or deck-plate connection in good order registers between 4.5 and 5 V on the
`TPM-D` test meter; a lower reading points to a dirty connector or a fault further
back.[^f147] The plug itself corrodes if neglected and can be cleaned or replaced, CTS
part `R-025-045`.[^f147]

## Care and maintenance

CTS treats a touchpad as electronic equipment whose working life depends on routine
cleaning, and warns that a puncture anywhere in the front lets water in and ends the
pad's life.[^f147] The [AquaGrip touchpad](aquagrip.md) article sets out the cleaning
routine, the storage range of 50–110 °F, and the caddies CTS sells.

One of the manual's cautions does not apply to these pads. The 2026 revision of F147
warns that sunlight can both warp a pad and lift its AquaGrip surface, where the 2014
revision of the same manual warned only about warping; the peeling risk belongs to the
AquaGrip treatment, not to the plain non-skid finish.[^f147][^f147old]

## Compared with the AquaGrip touchpad

The two pads are the same panel with different finishes. Body, backbone, sealed
full-face and top-edge switching, air-filled operation, 10-foot cable, dual banana
plug, mounting hardware and maintenance routine are common to both, and F147 documents
them once.[^f147][^datasheet] The patent behind the AquaGrip texture treats it as a
surface laid over a touchpad that already exists, describing the unit underneath as any
of those known in the field and naming CTS's own as an example.[^patent799]

The Standard surface is the older of the two. Swimming World reported in May 1998 that
CTS had announced a US patent for the AquaGrip pads, granted that December, so the plain
surface is what the company sold before then.[^sw1998][^patent799] The manual agrees:
F147 was titled simply for touchpads in its 2014 revision and names both pads only in
the 2026 one.[^f147][^f147old] That patent expired in January 2015, twenty years after
it was filed.[^patent799]

Which sizes came with which surface has shifted, and the sources disagree about the
range. The 2011 and 2015 catalogues offer AquaGrip in the imperial sizes only and give
the metric pads no AquaGrip variant, where CTS's current AquaGrip page lists metric ones
throughout.[^cat2015][^datasheet] The current catalogue still sets the two surfaces side
by side, Standard as `TP-60F`, `TP-78F`, `TP-90` and `TP-96`, and names no surface at
all against the metric pads, which does not match the discontinued notice on the
product page.[^catalog][^standard] A facility retiring worn Standard pads buys the
AquaGrip version of the same size, with the `G` added, wherever one exists.[^datasheet]

## Part numbers and accessories

- `TP-…`, gutter-hung Standard pads (`TP-60`, `TP-78`, `TP-96`, `TP-188.5`, `TP-190`,
  `TP-195`, `TP-240`).
- `TP-…F`, flat-wall Standard pads (`TP-60F`, `TP-78F`, `TP-188.5F`, `TP-190F`,
  `TP-195F`, `TP-240F`).
- `TPM-D`, touchpad test meter (it also checks the scoreboard, deck plates,
  pushbuttons and cable harness).
- `VP-2`, vacuum pump for adjusting pad air.
- `CAD-TP-P`, touchpad caddy (holds up to ten pads).
- `R-025-045`, replacement dual banana plug.
