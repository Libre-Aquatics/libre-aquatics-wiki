The AQUA touch plate is a swimming touch panel made by
[Seiko](../../../vendors/seiko.md) Time Creation Inc. of Tokyo. It is the panel hung at
a lane end that senses the swimmer's finish and turn, converting the touch into an
electrical signal that reaches the printing timer through that lane's lane
box.[^jp][^catalogue] It is the larger of the two touch plates Seiko currently sells,
and the only one the company lists as conforming to World Aquatics rules; the narrower
[full-frame touch plate](seiko-full-frame-touch-plate.md) carries a Japanese
certification only.[^jp][^ff][^catalogue] Seiko's own system diagram names this plate as
the touch plate of its competitive swimming system.[^system]

## Role in the timing system

The [touchpads overview](index.md) covers the history of automatic touchpad timing and
the World Aquatics requirements that apply to every touch panel. The AQUA plate sits at
the wall end of a chain that Seiko builds as a single certified system: the plate
reports to a lane box, one per lane, which sorts that lane's signals and passes them to
the `PT-9000` printing timer, while the
[PS-1400 start sound generator](../starter/seiko-ps-1400.md) supplies the start signal
at the other end.[^catalogue][^lanebox][^pt9000]

Seiko applies the same pair of compliance lines to the plate as to the rest of that
system, stating that it conforms to World Aquatics rules and is certified Class AA and
Class A by the Japan Swimming Federation.[^jp][^catalogue] Those are the company's own
statements of certification, not an independent test result.

## Naming and model numbers

Seiko publishes no model number for this plate in Japanese. The product page and the
swimming catalogue both call it タッチプレート（AQUAタイプ）, "touch plate (AQUA type)",
and give a four-row specification table with no part number anywhere on
it.[^jp][^catalogue] The storage cart, the lane box and the starting block are named the
same way, by description rather than by number, while the printing timer and the start
sound generator in the same catalogue do carry model numbers.[^catalogue]

Seiko's English swimming pages do give model numbers, `TP-3000` and `TP-3019`, but they
describe a different pair of panels and none of their figures match. The English page
gives the `TP-3000` as 2420 W × 917 H × 68 D mm and approximately 20 kg, with an
aluminium and stainless-steel frame and a PVC front panel finished in a yellow
resin fine-particle coating, and cites FINA rules rather than World Aquatics; the
`TP-3019` is the same construction at 1980 mm wide.[^en] The Japanese material gives a
high-impact resin panel, 2,400 W × 900 H × 77.5 D mm and about 22 kg.[^jp][^catalogue]

Seiko's English swimming section has been only partly updated, so its pages cannot be
read as a single generation. The `PS-1400` starter page there is current and cites World
Aquatics rules, while the touch pad, lane box and printing timer pages still cite FINA
rules, still name a `PT-8000X` timer where the Japanese pages name the `PT-9000`, and
still power the lane box from a
`PT-8000`.[^en][^enps1400][^swimidx][^enlanebox][^pt9000] The description of the
`TP-3000` as a framed panel also matches the construction Seiko still sells as the
full-frame type rather than the AQUA type. This article therefore treats the Japanese
product page and the December 2025 catalogue as current and does not apply the
`TP-3000` number to this plate; Seiko does not publish a statement connecting the two.

## Design and construction

Seiko gives the surface panel material as a high-impact resin board and the plate's
overall dimensions as 2,400 W × 900 H × 77.5 D mm, described as a maximum
figure.[^jp][^catalogue] The sensing area is 10 mm thick, which is the panel thickness
the World Aquatics facilities rules specify for a touch panel, and the 2,400 mm width
and 900 mm height are the minimum panel size those rules set.[^jp][^frules] The plate
weighs about 22 kg.[^jp][^catalogue] Catalogue photographs show it finished in yellow
with a dark cross marking at the centre, the end-wall line marking the rules
require.[^catalogue][^frules]

Seiko labels the sensing-surface row 感動面 in the catalogue and on both product pages,
where the expected term is 感応面, "sensing surface". The spelling is consistent across
its material, so a search on the correct term will not find these
specifications.[^jp][^ff][^catalogue]

The 77.5 mm overall depth is much greater than the 10 mm sensing area, so the figure
covers the plate's frame and mounting hardware rather than the panel alone. Seiko does
not break the dimension down, and does not publish the number of switches or sensing
zones behind the face, the activation force, or an operating temperature
range.[^jp][^catalogue]

## Sensing and wave rejection

Seiko describes the plate as detecting only the swimmer's touch, without responding to
water pressure or splashes, and converting that touch instantly into an electrical
signal sent to the printing timer.[^jp][^catalogue] The company does not say how the
panel makes that distinction, and publishes no sensing principle for the current
product.

One approach the group has taken to that problem appears in a patent.
Japanese patent application JPH08257196A, 競泳用タッチ板, filed in March 1995 by
Michio Tomita and published in October 1996, describes a touch plate built as a
substrate carrying a bonded panel switch with a fine-mesh member laid over it. A
swimmer's touch bears on the raised points of the mesh and concentrates enough pressure
on the switch to close it, while the distributed load of wave action spread across the
whole face does not, so the panel discriminates by how the force is applied rather than
by its total magnitude. The patent claims a simpler assembly and more even sensitivity
across the face than the designs it compares itself with, and notes that two switches
can be stacked as a backup within the thickness the rules allow.[^patent]

That patent is assigned to Seiko Instruments Inc., a separate company within the Seiko
group from Seiko Time Creation, and it lapsed for non-payment of fees in
2012.[^patent][^grouplist] Nothing published connects it to this product, and it is
cited here as evidence of how the group has approached the sensing problem, not as a
description of the plate on sale. The [touchpads overview](index.md) sets this
alongside the air-filled and conductive-plate designs other manufacturers use.

## Mounting

Seiko does not publish the plate's mounting method, its fixing hardware, or whether it
hangs from the gutter or fixes to a flat wall. The catalogue and the product page give
the specification table and a photograph only, and no installation document for the
plate is available: Seiko's manual download index lists no touch plate, and its
swimming category holds only the `SB-5010` and `SB-5020`
scoreboards.[^jp][^catalogue][^manualidx] The 77.5 mm maximum depth against a 10 mm
sensing area implies a frame or bracket, but Seiko does not describe it.

The company does document handling off the wall. A dedicated steel storage cart
takes six AQUA plates, measures 2,500 W × 1,380 H × 610 D mm including its wheels and
excluding protrusions, and weighs about 40 kg empty.[^catalogue][^cart] Portability is
consistent with the facilities rules, which allow a touch panel to be removable so it
can be taken out when no race is being held.[^frules]

## Connection to the timer

The plate does not wire directly to the timer. Each lane has a lane box, which takes
the signals from that lane's starting block and touch plate, sorts them by lane, and
passes them to the printing timer.[^catalogue][^lanebox] The lane box is an ABS case in
dark grey measuring 170 W × 50 H × 100 D mm excluding protrusions, weighing 450 g with a
230 g cable of 3.7 m, and it uses waterproof connectors.[^lanebox] Seiko does not
publish the connector type on the plate itself, the plate's own cable length, or a pin
assignment.

Timekeepers' pushbuttons run through the same box. Seiko sells a backup grip switch
system in which three timekeepers per lane operate grip switches feeding the printing
timer, using an `LB-10M` lane box and `GS-72` grip switches on a 5.5 m cable, with
twelve lane boxes and thirty grip switches in a ten-lane configuration. The timer
resolves the manual times by rule: where two of the three agree it takes that time,
where all three differ it takes the middle one, where only two are available it averages
them, and where only one is available it takes that.[^grip] Seiko's older English lane
box page describes the same box sorting five signals from touch pads, relay take-off
sensors and three grip switches.[^enlanebox]

At the timer the plate's signal is one of several the operator can check and correct.
The `PT-9000` can verify touch plate signals separately on the starting and turning
sides, and its hold time, the interval during which a further signal from the same plate
is ignored, is settable between 5 and 90 seconds to prevent spurious inputs during a
race.[^catalogue][^pt9000]

## Compared with the full-frame touch plate

The two plates Seiko sells differ in size, depth, weight and certification rather than
in stated function; the description of what they do is word for word the same on both
product pages.[^jp][^ff]

| | AQUA type | Full-frame type |
|---|---|---|
| Width | 2,400 mm | 1,900 mm |
| Height | 900 mm | 900 mm |
| Depth | 77.5 mm (maximum) | 15 mm |
| Sensing surface | 10 mm thick | 10 mm thick |
| Weight | Approx. 22 kg | Approx. 15 kg |
| Panel material | High-impact resin board | High-impact resin board |
| Certifications | World Aquatics rules; Japan Swimming Federation Class AA and Class A | Japan Swimming Federation Class A |
| Storage cart | 6 plates, approx. 40 kg | 10 plates, approx. 130 kg |

World Aquatics facilities rules set a
minimum touch panel width of 2.4 m. The AQUA plate meets that figure exactly and the
1,900 mm plate does not, and Seiko lists only the AQUA plate as conforming to those
rules.[^frules][^jp][^ff] The full-frame plate is much shallower, at 15 mm against
77.5 mm, and lighter by about 7 kg.[^ff][^catalogue]

## In competition

Seiko touch plates have been in use for decades. The Seiko Museum Ginza holds a swimming
touch pad dated 1986 in its sports timing collection, described in the same terms the
company still uses, as disregarding water pressure and wave splashes while reliably
sensing the swimmer's touch, and notes that the equipment is still used in major
swimming competitions.[^museum] Seiko is the Japan Swimming Federation's official
timekeeping partner and supplies the timing for the major domestic
championships.[^jasf][^catalogue]

Seiko's touch plates were also the subject of a dispute in international competition.
Seiko timed the swimming at the 2001 World Aquatics Championships in Fukuoka,
where the touchpads malfunctioned repeatedly across the eight days of competition and
times had to be corrected from backup timing or video evidence. The system recorded an
early take-off of minus 0.06 s that disqualified the United States women's
4 × 200 m freestyle relay on a successful British protest; the disqualification stood,
and the governing body, then FINA, awarded the United States a second set of gold medals
without changing the official result. It defended the equipment, saying swimmers had to
strike the pads in the right way, and then signed a three-event deal with Swiss Timing
covering the 2002 world short-course championships and the 2003 and 2005
championships.[^sw2001][^wp2001] Swimming World proposed on 30 July 2001 that FINA ship
the lane touchpads, the timing system terminal and the backup system to an independent
testing laboratory in Germany, and offered to publish the results; its editor-in-chief,
Phillip Whitten, described this as a way to settle the question of the system's accuracy
for the athletes who had competed.[^sw2001b]

Seiko does not document which touch plate was in use at Fukuoka, and no source connects
the equipment of 2001 to the plate described here. The
[Seiko](../../../vendors/seiko.md) vendor page sets the episode alongside the company's
wider timing record.

## Part numbers and accessories

- Touch plate (AQUA type), sold without a published part number in Seiko's Japanese
  material.
- Touch plate storage cart for the AQUA type, six plates, also unnumbered.
- Lane box, one per lane, carrying the plate's signal and the starting block's to the
  printing timer.
- `LB-10M` lane box and `GS-72` grip switches (5.5 m cable), the backup grip switch
  system, supplied to order.
- `TP-3000`, the model number Seiko's older English pages give for a 2420 mm framed
  touch pad, with a `TF-40` storage cart. Seiko does not state how it relates to the
  AQUA type.
