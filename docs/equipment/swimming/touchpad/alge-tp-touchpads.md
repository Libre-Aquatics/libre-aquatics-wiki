The TP touchpads are [ALGE-Timing](../../../vendors/alge-timing.md)'s swimming touchpads: stainless
steel cases holding plastic slats over four tape switches.[^cat2026] ALGE has used TP
numbers since at least 1997, first for pads with a closed steel back and later for pads with holes
that let water through.[^guide97][^leaflet] The current models are the `TP2400C`, `TP2300C`, `TP1890C` and
`TP914C`.[^cat2026][^p914]

## Role in the timing system

This section places the pad in an ALGE installation; the general background on touchpads is on the
[touchpads overview](index.md).

A pad hangs at each timed end of a lane and plugs into that lane's terminal, beside up to three push
buttons and a relay take-off sensor.[^manual] The terminal is an
[SWT3](../deck-cabling/alge-swt3.md) in a [TM-SWIM](../timers/alge-tm-swim.md) system and an
[SWT](../deck-cabling/alge-swt.md) in the older [SWIM 2000](../timers/alge-swim-2000.md). In the
[Timer S4](../timers/alge-timer-s4.md) system of the 1990s, two lanes shared a
[FIN1](../deck-cabling/alge-fin1.md) adapter.[^manual][^brochure][^guide97] A
[Timy2](../timers/alge-timy.md) running ALGE's training program reads two pads directly, one per
lane.[^trainer]

## History

This section sets out the three generations of the pad.

The 1997 installation guide for the Timer S4 system describes the TP24, for 2.5 m lanes. Its drawings,
and the later Timer S4 manual, call the pad TP1.[^guide97][^s4man] A 2002 brochure for the SWIM 2000
adds the TP18, and a 2013 catalogue the TP8 for training.[^brochure][^cat2013] These pads had a
stainless steel back, which the brochure and catalogue describe as closed.[^guide97][^brochure][^cat2013]

ALGE filed a German patent on a through-flow pad in November 2014.[^patent] The Lustenau
municipality, writing about the company's 70th anniversary in about 2016, reported that a non-slip pad
with gripping slats had recently been developed with a firm in Berneck.[^lustenau] A leaflet of April
2017 and that year's catalogue offer the TP2400, TP1890 and TP980 with
water-flow holes. ALGE put them at about 30 percent lighter than its earlier pads. Both documents
head the middle size TP1800.[^leaflet][^cat2017]

The C suffix appears on an ALGE drawing dated 31 August 2017.[^manual] The 2026 catalogue lists the
TP2400C, TP1890C and TP2300C. It does not say what the letter stands for.[^cat2026] ALGE's product pages
add the TP914C, and record edge protection against injury as new from October 2024.[^p914][^p2400]

## Design and construction

This section describes the current pad.

The case and the rear plate are stainless steel, and the face is a row of vertical PVC slats. ALGE
describes the slat surface as rough, with thousands of small bumps for grip.[^cat2026] The face is yellow
with a black target cross, and a rubber-covered angle runs along the top, with the cable box at one
end.[^manual] On the 1997 pad the rubber along the top was part of the active area.[^guide97]

ALGE's sources differ on the grade of steel. The 2002 and 2013 documents and the 2024 manual give
1.4571, and the 2017 and 2026 catalogues and the product pages give 1.4404.[^brochure][^cat2013][^manual][^cat2017][^cat2026][^p2400]

## Water flow and the patent

This section covers the openings through the pad.

Each slat has small holes along its length, and the rear plate has rows of large round ones. Water
can therefore pass through the pad to an overflow gutter behind it.[^leaflet] ALGE says a closed
pad diverts the current sideways at the end wall, and that its own design treats every lane
alike.[^leaflet][^cat2017]

German patent DE 10 2014 116 528 B4, granted in December 2023 to Alge-Timing GmbH with Wolfgang Alge
as inventor, protects a pad of this kind. Its main claim requires openings in both the front unit and
the plate behind it, with the sensor between them. Further claims cover plastic slats hooked together
in pairs and openings no wider than 7 mm. The description says the narrow openings keep children's
fingers from being caught, and that letting water through lowers the pressure that could
trigger the sensor when a swimmer dives in.[^patent] The patent names no product, and its features
match those of the TP pads since 2017.

## Contact operation and sensitivity

This section covers what makes the pad register a touch.

Pressure on the slats closes tape switches beneath them. There are four, running the full width of the
pad, and the circuit is a normally open contact that closes on a touch.[^cat2017] ALGE says splashes and
waves do not trigger it.[^cat2026]

| Source | Trigger force |
|---|---|
| Timer S4 installation guide, 1997 | About 3 kg |
| Catalogue, 2013 | 2–3 kg |
| Swimming Timing System manual, 2024 | 2.5–3.5 kg |

The figures come from different pads, and none of the documents explains how the force is
measured.[^guide97][^cat2013][^manual]

Aquatics rules, which are on the [touchpads overview](index.md#governing-body-requirements).

ALGE-Swim USA lists pads of 74 × 24 in and 62 × 24 in for gutter-type pools, without model
numbers.[^usa]

## Mounting

This section covers fixing the pad to the end wall.

For occasional use, ALGE's manual allows a pad to be tied to the starting platforms with rope, through
holes in the top angle.[^manual] On the TP2400C drawing there are four 8 mm holes under the rubber,
600 mm and 2100 mm apart, which an installer can open up for other fixings.[^manual]

Permanent points use [holders](../accessories/alge-touchpad-holders.md) that screw into M8 anchors set in the wall.
The TP-D140 holds two neighbouring pads, the TP-S holds the outer edge of the end pad, and the TP-T
reaches down from the deck. The fastener sits 75–175 mm below the top of the pad. Where turn plates
extend the wall of an overflow pool, a TP-D140 is used with a TP-L backing plate, through a gap of at
least 8.5 mm between the plates.[^manual]

The 1997 guide set the pad 30 cm above the water and 60 cm below it, with one holder between two pads in
2.5 m lanes.[^guide97] ALGE's 2002 guideline for architects asks for a completely even wall and adds
11 mm to the pool length at each end fitted with a pad.[^arch]

## Connection to the timer

This section covers how the pad's signal reaches the timer.

A 2.5 m [cable 309-2.5](../accessories/alge-cable-309.md) with banana plugs at both ends comes with
the pad.[^p2400][^p309] ALGE says the closing contact suits most other timing systems.[^cat2017]

The TM-SWIM blocks every pad for a set time once the race starts, and each pad separately once it is
touched. Both periods are 10 seconds as supplied. The manual recommends these values.[^manual]

| Pool | Pads | After the start | After a touch |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 m | One end | 20 s | 20 s |
| 25 m | Both ends | 8 s | 20 s |
| 50 m | One end | 44 s | 40 s |
| 50 m | Both ends | 20 s | 40 s |

SwimTime warns the operator if a button time is further from the pad time than a set limit, 0.30
seconds by default.[^manual] The terminal registers both the press and the release, and a training
program uses the interval as the turn time.[^manual][^trainer] Since December 2024 the TM-SWIM firmware
has also passed on a warning from suitable terminals when a pad's resistance falls.[^fwlog]

## Care and maintenance

This section summarizes ALGE's instructions.

Pads should be in the water only for competition, according to the 1997 guide, and stored on a
trolley.[^guide97] The current manual asks for hand cleaning every week to begin with, adjusted so that
salts never dry on the pad. Tap water is used for cleaning. Deposits and oxidation stains can be
treated with a non-abrasive metal cleaner or a 10–15 % nitric solution, then rinsed and dried with a
cotton cloth. A little silicone grease protects the contacts.[^manual]

Chemical dosing should be kept away from the pads, and the pads away from iron and other metals. The manual also asks
pools to control pH and disinfectant levels.[^manual] The 2013 catalogue and the 2017 leaflet say owners can carry
out repairs themselves.[^cat2013][^leaflet]

## Compared with the OCP5

The Omega [OCP5 touchpad](ocp5.md) is also a stainless steel pad with PVC slats over tape switches. ALGE
fits four switches where the OCP5 has two or three, and its 2.4 m pad weighs 24.7 kg against 16.3 kg. The
OCP5's test range is 1.5–3.5 kp, against ALGE's 2.5–3.5 kg.[^cat2026][^manual][^ocp5man][^ocp5ds]
