The PS-1300 is a discontinued electronic starting device for competitive swimming made
by [Seiko](../../../vendors/seiko.md) Time Creation Inc. of Tokyo. It generated the
start tone and an alarm tone
electronically and sent a start signal to the printing timer, and Seiko certified it as
conforming to World Aquatics rules and as Japan Swimming Federation Class AA and Class
A.[^disc] It replaced the larger [PS-1200](seiko-ps-1200.md) and was in turn replaced
by the [PS-1400](seiko-ps-1400.md), which repeats every specification Seiko published
for this unit.[^ps1400] This article is a starting point; the
[start-systems overview](index.md) covers the shared background that applies to every
starter.

## Naming and product line

The Japanese name is 電子式スタート音発生装置, "electronic start sound generator". The
same naming split between Seiko's Japanese and English material that affects the
PS-1400 applies here, and the [PS-1400 article](seiko-ps-1400.md) sets it out.

The PS-1300 is the middle of three generations of Seiko's swimming start sound
generator: the [PS-1200](seiko-ps-1200.md), then this unit, then the
[PS-1400](seiko-ps-1400.md).

Seiko publishes no production dates for it. Its discontinued-product listing gives the
model, a description and a specification table, but no 製造時期 (manufacturing period)
and no repair-support end date. Some of Seiko's discontinued listings do carry both:
the `PT-7000` printing timer, of the previous generation, prints 1998年～2010年 and a
support end of April 2017. Others carry neither, including the `PT-8000` timer that
ran alongside this unit.[^disc][^pt7000][^pt8000]

The dates can still be bracketed from archived copies of Seiko's own product index.
The PS-1200 was the listed generator in a snapshot of 8 June 2013, and the PS-1300 had
replaced it by the next surviving snapshot, of 20 October 2014, so the changeover falls
somewhere in that gap.[^wb2013][^wb2014] At the other end, the PS-1300 was still listed
as the current generator on 10 February 2026, and by the snapshot of 10 May 2026 the
PS-1400 had taken its place.[^wb2026a][^wb2026b] On that evidence it had a service life
of roughly twelve years.

The replacement was part of a wider refresh rather than a single product change: the
same pair of snapshots shows the `PT-8000` printing timer giving way to the `PT-9000`
at the same time.[^wb2026a][^wb2026b]

Readers searching for the model number should note that Ono Sokki also sells an
unrelated "PS-1300", a three-channel sensor amplifier, which dominates search
results.[^onosokki]

## Design and hardware

The main unit measures 264 W × 203 H × 390 D mm excluding protrusions and weighs about
14.5 kg. It runs from a small sealed lead-acid battery of DC 12 V and 17 Ah, so it
needs no mains connection on the deck.[^disc]

The starter's pistol is aluminium, 210 W × 40 H × 145 D mm, weighing about 410 g
without its batteries and cable, and runs on two AA cells.[^disc] Those are the same
figures Seiko publishes for the pistol of the current PS-1400, for the `PS-78` pistol
of its portable [Electronic Starting System](seiko-electronic-starting-system.md), and
for the pistol of its athletics `PS-200` start signal generator, which suggests a
common pistol body across the company's starting equipment.[^ps1400][^rikujyo] Seiko
does not give the PS-1300 pistol a part number of its own.

## Operation

Seiko describes the operating principle in the same sentence it has used for all three
generations: the unit generates the start signal sound and an alarm sound
electronically and at the same time sends the start signal to the printing timer;
because no propellant is involved there are no misfires and no smoke, unlike a powder
pistol; and the start sound can be selected from two types.[^disc]

## Audio

A speaker expansion box, the `SPE-1300`, holds the generator in a wheeled cabinet with
four speakers, two to each side. Seiko states that sounding from three directions gives
maximum volume across more than 270 degrees, counting the generator's own front-facing
speaker as the third; that description is written around the PS-1400, and Seiko
publishes no speaker specification for the PS-1300 itself.[^spe][^disc]

Despite sharing the number, the expansion box was not introduced alongside the
PS-1300. It is absent from an archived copy of Seiko's swimming product index of
13 August 2022 and present by 22 February 2024, so it arrived near the end of the
PS-1300's service life rather than at the start of it.[^wb2022][^wb2024] Its product
page is now written around the PS-1400 and treats this unit as the secondary case,
noting only that it can also be used with the PS-1300.[^spe]

Seiko's two sources disagree over which generator the loaded weight refers to. The
website gives the cart as about 35 kg, or about 50 kg 「PS-1300本体搭載時」, with the
PS-1300 mounted; the December 2025 catalogue prints the same sentence with the PS-1400
in place of the PS-1300.[^spe][^catalogue] The figures are the same either way.

Seiko publishes no output wattage, sound pressure level, impedance or speaker-output
count for the PS-1300.

## Compared with the PS-1200 and the PS-1400

The two transitions are of very different sizes.

Replacing the PS-1200 shrank the equipment substantially. The older generator was
about 380 W × 700 H × 380 D mm and weighed about 30 kg on a DC 12 V 20 Ah battery; the
PS-1300 is about 14.5 kg on a 17 Ah battery, and its 203 mm height turns a
floor-standing box into one that can be carried.[^wb2009][^disc]

The PS-1400 repeats every figure the PS-1300 listing gives. The main unit's battery
type, dimensions and weight and the pistol's power, material, dimensions and weight
match exactly, as do the description text and the certification
line.[^disc][^ps1400] Seiko does not say what changed inside.

Seiko does publish three things for the PS-1400 that it never published for this unit:
a separate starter box of 183 W × 62 H × 150 D mm weighing about 1.3 kg, a splash-proof
cover, and a note that the strobe sits on top of the pistol where it is easy to
see.[^enps1400][^catalogue] Whether those are new to the PS-1400 or simply undocumented
on the older listing cannot be settled from the sources. The battery figure differs in
the same way: the PS-1300 page gives DC 12 V 17 Ah where the PS-1400 page gives only
DC 12 V, with the 17 Ah appearing instead in Seiko's swimming
catalogue.[^disc][^ps1400][^catalogue]

One detail does separate the two listings, and it dates them. The PS-1300 page carries
世界水泳連盟（World Aquatics）ルール適合 and （公財）日本水泳連盟, while the archived
PS-1200 page carries the older 国際水泳連盟（FINA）ルール適合 and （財）日本水泳連盟,
wording that predates both World Aquatics' rename and the Japan Swimming Federation's
change of corporate status.[^disc][^wb2009]

## Specifications

Main unit:

| | |
|---|---|
| Part number | `PS-1300` |
| Power | Small sealed lead-acid battery, DC 12 V, 17 Ah |
| Dimensions | 264 W × 203 H × 390 D mm (excluding protrusions) |
| Weight | Approx. 14.5 kg |
| Start sounds | Two, selectable |
| Certifications | World Aquatics rules; Japan Swimming Federation Class AA and Class A |

Pistol:

| | |
|---|---|
| Power | DC 3 V (2 × AA dry cells) |
| Material | Aluminium |
| Dimensions | 210 W × 40 H × 145 D mm |
| Weight | Approx. 410 g (excluding cells and cable) |

Seiko publishes no output wattage, sound pressure level, speaker-output count,
connector type, operating temperature range or battery runtime for this unit, and no
instruction manual for it. Its manual index lists documents for only two swimming
products, the `SB-5010` scoreboard and the `SB-5020` controller.[^manualidx]
