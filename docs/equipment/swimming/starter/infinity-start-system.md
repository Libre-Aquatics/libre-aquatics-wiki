The Infinity Start System (part number `INF-SSM`) is a portable electronic
start system for swimming made by
[Colorado Time Systems](../../../vendors/colorado-time-systems.md) (CTS).
It combines a start horn, a strobe light, and a limited public-address system in
a single battery-powered unit carried by a molded handle. The starter speaks and
starts races through a wired microphone; the unit sounds a start tone, flashes
its strobe, and sends a start pulse to the electronic timer.[^f849][^product]
CTS lists the product as having reached end of life on 6 August 2024, replaced
by the [Infinity Pro Start System](infinity-pro-start-system.md).[^product]

## Naming and product line

The Infinity is the portable, entry-level member of the CTS start line and the
older of the two Infinity-family models: a self-contained unit with one built-in
speaker, an optional second speaker, and no lane-speaker or speedlight outputs.
Its successor is the [Infinity Pro](infinity-pro-start-system.md); the deck-wired
[Championship](championship-start-system.md) family is the larger alternative. For
the full lineup and a side-by-side comparison of all the models, see the
[start-systems overview](index.md).

An earlier revision of its manual (F849 Rev. 1005) shows the product was on the
market by the mid-2000s.[^f849old] The `INF-SSMF` variant is the unit sold with
a 230 V charger for markets outside North America; CTS does not spell out the
suffix, but it matches the convention the company uses for the Infinity Pro, where
`INF-PRO` is the 115 V model and `INF-PRO-F` the 230 V model, and the Infinity
product page notes a 240 V charging option.[^psu][^prodatasheet][^product]

CTS also sold the Infinity as the start system of its Train Right one-lane
training package (`TR-SYS-1`), paired with a relay judging platform, a Pace Clock
Pro, a pushbutton, and a training-size touchpad. Because the Infinity has no
speedlight output, the platform's speedlights do not flash with the start in this
version of the package. The package, now discontinued, is described in the
[relay judging platform](../relay-judging/cts.md) article.[^f957][^trsys]

## Design and hardware

The unit is a cylindrical enclosure about 14 in long weighing 12 lb, with the
speaker at one end, a 360° strobe dome on top, a carrying handle, and a
connector panel on the side. The connector panel carries the charger input, the
power switch, a speaker volume control, a battery-life indicator LED, two
microphone jacks (the second doubles as an external-strobe input), the start
output to the timer, and an external-speaker jack. A microphone rest is built in
beside the panel.[^f849]

The built-in speaker is a 40-watt, 8-ohm moisture- and corrosion-resistant
reflex speaker; one additional CTS external speaker can be connected for far
lanes. The microphone is from CTS's M2 family, with a push-to-talk switch and
two start buttons on top placed for either left- or right-handed
use.[^f849][^product]

The system runs only from its internal 12 V gel-cell battery; it will not
operate while plugged into AC power. CTS presents this as a safety design that
keeps AC power off the pool deck. A full charge takes about eight hours with the
18 VDC wall-brick charger and gives about eight hours of normal use; heavy
public-address use shortens that. The indicator LED shows green (charged),
yellow (5–6 hours remaining), red (1–2 hours remaining), or flashing green
(charging), and the manual notes an idle battery loses about 20% of its charge
in two to three months.[^f849]

## Operation

Pressing the push-to-talk switch turns the microphone on for announcements; the
volume knob affects only this public-address audio. Pressing push-to-talk
together with either start button fires the start: a 0.5-second dual-tone blast
at fixed full volume, a simultaneous strobe flash, and a start pulse to the
timer. Pressing a start button again signals a false-start recall, a repeating
dual tone.[^f849]

The strobe flashes with the start tone, and officials and watch timers are meant
to start from the flash rather than the tone. World Aquatics rules require the
starter's commands and the starting signal to be heard equally and
simultaneously at each block; see the [start-systems overview](index.md) for the
reason and the governing-body rules.[^f849][^wacr]

## Connections

The start output is an opto-isolated transistor circuit. It emits a normally-open (N.O.)
pulse and nothing else, which suits every CTS timer; a timer expecting a normally-closed
pulse means sending the unit back for factory modification. The Infinity
connects to a timer's cable harness or deck plate with a start jumper cable,
model `INF-SJ25` (25 ft) or `INF-SJ50M` (50 m).[^f849] The unit sits on a
tabletop, on the CTS `TR-3` tripod, or on a flagpole with the `START-FPM-2`
mount.[^f849]

## Part numbers and accessories

- `INF-SSM`, Infinity Start System, 115 V charger; `INF-SSMF`, 230 V-charger
  variant (suffix meaning inferred from the Infinity Pro convention, above).[^psu]
- `M2-25` / `M2-50` / `M2-100` / `M2-200`, replacement microphones; the number
  is the straight-cable length in feet. `M2-C15` has a 15 ft coiled
  cable.[^f849][^product]
- `INF-SP` (125 or 200), external 40 W speaker with 125 ft or 200 ft cable.[^f849]
- `INF-SJ25` / `INF-SJ50M`, start jumper cable to the timer, 25 ft or 50 m.[^f849]
- `BP-3`, spare battery with external charger.[^f849]
- `EXTSTROBE`, external strobe light (connects to a microphone jack).[^f849]
- `TR-3`, tripod; `START-FPM-2`, flagpole mount kit.[^f849]
- Battery replacement instructions are published separately as CTS document
  F851.[^f851]
