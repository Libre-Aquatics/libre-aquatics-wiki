The Seiko Electronic Starting System is a portable electronic starter made by
[Seiko](../../../vendors/seiko.md) Time Creation Inc. of Tokyo and sold as model
`PS-110J`. It replaces a
blank-cartridge pistol: pulling the trigger sounds an electronic start tone through
a battery-powered speaker box, fires a xenon strobe in the pistol itself, and closes
a relay contact that starts a connected timer.[^manual][^ps110j] Seiko sells it as
general-purpose equipment, describing it for track and field and for events
generally rather than for swimming, and publishes no governing-body certification for
it, where its swimming starter, the
[PS-1400 Electronic Start Sound Generator](seiko-ps-1400.md), is listed as conforming
to World Aquatics rules and certified by the Japan Swimming
Federation.[^ipros][^en][^catalogue]
This article is a starting point; the [start-systems overview](index.md) covers the
shared background that applies to every starter.

## Models and naming

The current system is the `PS-110J`. It is the fifth documented generation of Seiko's
エレクトロニックスタータ line, and the company's discontinued-product index dates the
earlier ones: the [PS-105](seiko-ps-105.md) to 1997, the
[PS-107](seiko-ps-107.md) from 1997 to 2002, the [PS-109](seiko-ps-109.md) from 2002 to
2008, and the `PS-110` from 2009 to 2022.[^discidx]

The `PS-110` is close to this unit but not identical. It shipped with the starter
pistol `PS-78`, the same headset microphone `PS-61` and speaker box `PS-56`, and a 5 m
cable with box, `PS-81`; the PS-110J changed the pistol and cable to the J-suffixed
`PS-78J` and `PS-81J` and shortened the cable to 0.3 m.[^ps110man][^manual] The PS-110
has its own operating manual, which unlike this one carries no document
number.[^ps110man]

Seiko applies the English name "Electronic Starting System" both to this unit and, on
its English swimming pages, to the unrelated PS-1400, so the model number is the
reliable identifier rather than the name.[^en][^enps1400]

The manufacturer is Seiko Time Creation Inc., formed on 1 April 2021 by the merger of
Seiko Clock Inc. and Seiko Time Systems Inc., both of them consolidated subsidiaries
of Seiko Holdings Corporation, which was renamed Seiko Group Corporation in
2022.[^merger] The sports timing and measurement business reached it by an earlier
step in the same corporate line: in 1998 Seiko Corporation transferred that business
to the company then named Seiko C.S.T., which was renamed Seiko Time Systems at the
same time.[^history]

The system ships as four part-numbered components: the starter pistol `PS-78J`, the
headset microphone `PS-61`, the speaker box `PS-56`, and a short interface lead
called a cable with box, `PS-81J`. Eight AA cells for the speaker box and two for
the pistol are supplied.[^manual]

Seiko's manual records which of the company's other starting equipment the system
will not work with, which is where several otherwise undocumented model numbers
appear. The `PS-110J` cannot be used with the `PS-210S` pistol, which belongs to the
`PS-200B` start signal generator, nor with the `PF-125J` strobe pistol, which
belongs to the `PS-1300B` electronic start sound generator.[^manual]

The same note in the earlier PS-110 manual names the previous generation of both:
the `PS-210S` for the `PS-200B`, and a `PF-125` pistol belonging to the
[PS-1200](seiko-ps-1200.md) electronic start sound generator.[^ps110man] Read together,
the two editions show the pistol designation tracking the swimming generator it serves,
`PF-125` with the PS-1200 and `PF-125J` with the PS-1300B. These incompatibility notes
are the only place either pistol has been found in Seiko's published material, and
neither carries a specification anywhere.

## Design and hardware

The pistol is an aluminium die-cast body measuring 210 W × 145 D × 40 H mm and
weighing about 410 g without batteries or cable, with a transparent resin section
over the strobe and a black resin battery holder. It runs on two AA alkaline cells
and carries the trigger, the microphone jack, a microphone volume control, a ready
lamp, and the switch that selects between the two start sounds.[^manual]

The speaker box is a moulded ABS enclosure in off-white, 102 W × 216 D × 219 H mm
and about 1.6 kg without cells, with a horn speaker on the front face that Seiko
directs be turned towards the athletes. Its top panel carries the volume control, a
battery-life indicator, the power switch and a whistle switch; the rear carries a
voice switch, an external power input terminal that Seiko states is not used in this
system, the combined AUX and MIC input, and the battery cover. An M8 socket in the
base takes a tripod, for which Seiko suggests, for reference, one whose legs describe
a circle 1,300 mm across when opened, with a stand height of 1,000–1,600 mm.[^manual]

The headset microphone is a condenser type weighing about 45 g including its cable,
with a pin plug that goes into the pistol rather than directly into the speaker box.
Seiko warns that connecting it straight to the speaker box will not reproduce the
starter's voice correctly.[^manual]

Neither the pistol nor the speaker box is drip-proof. Seiko's manual states this three
times, once in the safety warnings and again in each of the two specification tables,
and directs that the equipment be kept clear of splashes and rain in use and in
storage. That is a material constraint for poolside use, and it is one of the points
on which the purpose-built PS-1400, which is supplied with a splash-proof cover,
differs from this unit.[^manual][^catalogue]

## Operation

The starter wears the headset, holds the pistol, and speaks to the field through
the microphone, whose voice is amplified through the same speaker box that will
sound the start. Pulling the trigger fires the strobe and plays the start sound
together, and the start signal leaves the interface lead at the same
moment.[^manual]

Two start sounds are selectable on the pistol, an imitation gunshot and an
electronic beep.[^manual][^ipros] A separate whistle switch on the speaker box
sounds a whistle for as long as it is held; its level is fixed and the volume
control does not affect it.[^manual]

The battery-life indicator on the speaker box lights steadily while the cells hold
sufficient charge and flashes when they need replacing. Seiko directs that batteries
be changed every day and that only fresh cells be fitted, on the grounds that old
cells cause malfunctions in use.[^manual]

The voice switch carries a timing consequence. Seiko directs that it be left off in
normal use, noting that switching it on reduces battery consumption but may leave
the start sound and the strobe flash out of step with each other.[^manual] Timers
are directed to start from the flash rather than the tone, as the
[start-systems overview](index.md) describes, so that setting works against the
reason the strobe is fitted.

Seiko also warns against firing the strobe towards anyone's eyes or looking directly
into it, which it says may cause a momentary loss of vision.[^manual]

## Audio

The speaker box is rated at 6 W with a maximum of 10 W, and Seiko gives its
practical sound pressure level as 93 dBV at a distance of 10 m. Its audio input is
rated at -20 dBV; the pistol delivers the start sound at -18 dBV and the microphone
at up to -5 dBV.[^manual] United States resellers quote the same figure as 93 dB at
30 feet, which is the same measurement in local units.[^reseller]

Up to ten speaker boxes can be connected to one system, chained by 3.5 mm plug
cables into either the MIC or the AUX connector of the next box. Seiko sets two
limits on the arrangement: the total cable length from the pistol to the farthest
speaker box must be 20 m or less, and every connected box must be switched on,
because leaving one off while the system is in use is not a supported
configuration.[^manual]

Twenty metres of total cable is the practical ceiling on how far this system can
distribute a start. It is enough to place boxes along a pool deck, but it is not the
per-lane loudspeaker arrangement that World Aquatics requires for championship
swimming, which the [start-systems overview](index.md) describes.

## Connections

The start signal for external timers leaves the `PS-81J` cable with box. Seiko
specifies it as a Photo-MOS relay output, converted to a low-resistance signal by a
semiconductor relay and issued in sync with the electronic sound. The output appears
between pins 1 and 3 of an `MJ-192` connector made by Marushin Electric
Manufacturing, and the mating cable-side connectors are the `MP-132` or
`MP-132/8`.[^manual]

Because the output is a dry relay contact rather than a driven signal, Seiko sets a
load limit on whatever it is wired to: no more than 100 V AC or DC, and no more than
0.1 A.[^manual]

The same lead carries two further connections: a voice output plug, taken to whichever of
the speaker box's AUX and MIC terminals is in use, and an input connector for the pistol.
Seiko specifies that only the supplied cables be used.[^manual]

Sources disagree on the length of this lead. The `PS-110J` manual gives the `PS-81J`
as 0.3 m, while the B2B listing on IPROS gives 5 m for the same part number; Seiko's
own PS-110J page gives no length, and the earlier `PS-110` was supplied with a 5 m
`PS-81`. The manual is the more specific source for the current
system.[^manual][^ipros][^ps110j][^ps110man]

Seiko's battery-life figure for the speaker box is defined as the number of pistol
sounds it will produce before the sound pressure falls to 91 dB at 10 m, rather than
as a running time.[^manual]

The speaker box height differs between sources, and the split is largely generational.
The `PS-56` is 258 mm tall in the PS-110 manual, in the Track and Field catalogue and
on the discontinued `PS-110` listing, and 219 mm in the PS-110J manual and on the
Japanese PS-110J product page.[^ps110man][^rikujyo][^disc][^manual][^ps110j] United
States resellers, who list the older system, quote 10 inches.[^reseller] The split is
not clean, though: Seiko's own English page, which is headed PS-110J, gives 258 mm and
names the pistol `PS-78` rather than `PS-78J`, so it appears to carry PS-110-era
figures under the newer model name.[^en] The manuals are the more specific source for
each generation.

Battery life is reported differently and the manual is preferred. Seiko's Japanese
pages and the manual agree on approximately 8,000 pistol sounds for the speaker box,
while the company's English product page gives approximately 3,000
operations.[^manual][^ps110j][^en] Resellers also state that one set of batteries
powers the starter and speaker for up to 1,200 starts, which conflates the pistol's
strobe life with the speaker box's separate and much longer cell life.[^reseller]

## Part numbers and accessories

The system is `PS-110J`. Its components are the starter pistol `PS-78J`, the headset
microphone `PS-61`, the speaker box `PS-56`, and the cable with box
`PS-81J`.[^manual]

Additional `PS-56` boxes are the documented way to extend coverage, within the
ten-box and 20 m limits above.[^manual] A Malaysian distributor lists optional
`SP-1200` loudspeakers as an extension to the system.[^afn] Seiko itself documented an
`SP-1200` loudspeaker, but as an option for a different unit, the athletics
`PS-1200TR`, on its former traditional-Chinese site.[^tr] The part is therefore
attested, though no Seiko source connects it to this system.

Seiko lists the system in Japan at ¥286,000 including tax, ¥260,000 before
tax.[^ps110j] United States resellers list it at about US$2,595.[^reseller]
