The PS-1200 is a discontinued electronic starting device for competitive swimming made
by [Seiko](../../../vendors/seiko.md). It generated the start signal sound and an alarm
sound electronically and
sent the start signal to the printing timer at the same time, and Seiko listed it as
conforming to FINA rules and as Japan Swimming Federation Class AA and Class
A.[^wb2009] It is the earliest generation of the company's swimming start sound
generator that can be documented, and it was replaced by the much smaller
[PS-1300](seiko-ps-1300.md).[^wb2013][^wb2014] This article is a starting point; the
[start-systems overview](index.md) covers the shared background that applies to every
starter.

## Naming and product line

The Japanese name is 電子式スタート音発生装置, "electronic start sound generator", the
same name Seiko has applied to all three generations of the swimming unit.

The PS-1200 no longer appears on any Seiko page or in any current catalogue, and Seiko
publishes no discontinued-product listing for it, unlike the
[PS-1300](seiko-ps-1300.md) that followed it. It is documented here from archived
copies of Seiko's own product index and swimming catalogue, which is the only surviving
first-party source.[^wb2009][^wb2007]

Those archives place it as the listed generator from at least 19 December 2007 to at
least 8 June 2013, after which the PS-1300 had replaced it by the next surviving
snapshot of 20 October 2014.[^wb2007][^wb2013][^wb2014] The earliest archived copy of
any Seiko sports page dates from 2007, so the start of the PS-1200's service life is
not established and it may well be older.

The certification line on the archived page reads 国際水泳連盟（FINA）ルール適合 and
（財）日本水泳連盟AA級/A級公認 in the 2009 snapshot, where the current PS-1300 listing
reads 世界水泳連盟（World Aquatics）ルール適合 and （公財）日本水泳連盟.[^wb2009][^ps1300]
The wording is not a reliable guide to when either product was sold, because Seiko
revised it at its own pace: the last PS-1200 snapshot, from 2013, already carries
（公財）, and the first PS-1300 snapshot, from 2014, still carries the older
FINA wording.[^wb2013][^wb2014] The changes track edits to the pages rather than the
generations of equipment.

The archived index shows what the PS-1200 was listed alongside. In 2007 that was the
`PT-7000` printing timer, manufactured 1998 to 2010, the `DHS-2000` high-speed video
system, touch plates, starting blocks, lane boxes, a battery and charger, connector
boxes, an underwater lap display system, a light-stimulus start signal system, a
pacemaker and a results network system.[^wb2007][^pt7000] By the 2013 snapshot the
timer alongside it was the `PT-8000` and the video system the `DHS-3000`.[^wb2013]

## Design and hardware

The PS-1200 is a substantially larger machine than anything that followed it. It
measures about 380 W × 700 H × 380 D mm excluding protrusions and weighs about 30 kg,
running from a small sealed lead battery of DC 12 V and 20 Ah.[^wb2009] At 700 mm tall
it is a floor-standing unit rather than something carried onto the deck by hand.

The archived product page carries no pistol entry, but the pistol is named elsewhere.
The operating manual for Seiko's portable
[PS-110](seiko-electronic-starting-system.md) lists the equipment it cannot be
connected to, and identifies the `PF-125` starter pistol as the one belonging to the
Electronic Start Sound Generator PS-1200.[^ps110man] No specifications for that pistol
are published. Later generations do publish pistol figures, but those must not be read
back onto this unit.

## Operation

Seiko's description is the same sentence it would go on using for the PS-1300 and the
PS-1400: the unit generates the start signal sound and an alarm sound electronically
and at the same time sends the start signal to the printing timer; because no
propellant is involved there are no misfires and no smoke, unlike a powder pistol; and
the start sound can be selected from two types.[^wb2009]

## Audio

The archived page says nothing at all about the PS-1200's own speaker, and Seiko
publishes no amplifier rating, sound pressure level or speaker-output count for
it.[^wb2009]

The company did publish those figures for the athletics counterpart, the `PS-1200TR`,
on its former traditional-Chinese site, and they are the only amplifier ratings
published anywhere in this family. That unit shares the DC 12 V 20 Ah sealed lead-acid
battery and carries a built-in amplifier rated at up to 60 W into 1 Ω with a combined
impedance of about 4 Ω, an external amplifier output also rated at up to 60 W into
1 Ω, an operating range of -10 to +40 °C non-condensing, audio and start-contact
inputs, and start signal, alarm contact, audio and start-contact outputs. An optional
`SP-1200` loudspeaker extended the system.[^tr] These are the athletics variant's
figures and should not be attributed to the swimming unit.

The same page states the acoustic principle these systems are built on: the electronic
start sound is delivered through loudspeakers so that it reaches every competitor
simultaneously.[^tr] The
[start-systems overview](index.md) covers that principle and the governing-body
requirements built on it.

## Compared with the PS-1300

The move to the PS-1300 halved the equipment. The battery dropped from 20 Ah to 17 Ah,
the weight from about 30 kg to about 14.5 kg, and the height from about 700 mm to
203 mm, turning a floor-standing cabinet into a unit that can be carried.[^wb2009][^ps1300]
The published width and depth changed less, from about 380 by 380 mm to 264 by
390 mm.[^wb2009][^ps1300]

Seiko does not describe what changed internally, and the description text and the
selectable-sound count stayed the same across the two generations, so the size
reduction is the only documented difference.
