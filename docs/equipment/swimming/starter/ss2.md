The SS2 Electronic Start System is a discontinued loudspeaker start system for
swimming made by
[Colorado Time Systems](../../../vendors/colorado-time-systems.md)
(CTS) during the 1990s. It consists of a battery-powered start module with a
built-in strobe light, a hand-held starter's microphone, and one or more
loudspeakers. The same speakers carry the starter's voice, so the system also
works as a small public-address system. In CTS's contemporary System 5
documentation the SS2 is the start system that shipped as standard with the
company's swimming timing packages.[^f503][^f506] The deck-wired
[Championship](championship-start-system.md) family and the portable
[Infinity](infinity-start-system.md) family later replaced it in the CTS line.

## Models and naming

The instruction guide (F503 Rev. 0897, first issued 1993) covers five
configurations, which differ only in the speakers supplied with the same start
module:[^f503]

- `SS2-1` is the base system: the strobe-equipped module, a single 40-watt
  speaker, a 25-foot microphone, the internal gel-cell battery, and a charger
  for that battery.
- `SS2-2` pairs a 125-foot cable with a further 40-watt speaker and a kit for
  mounting it on the backstroke flag poles, which brings a speaker within range
  of the far lanes.
- `SS2-6`, `SS2-8`, and `SS2-10` take the base system and add six, eight, or ten
  6-watt under-block speakers. Each count has its own harness: `LS40-6`,
  `LS40-8`, or `LS40-10`.

The SS2 was the middle member of a series. The System 5 swimming software
guide of 1998 lists three families of CTS electronic start system,
the `SS-1`, the SS2, and the LS systems, alongside the `ST-15` start-pistol
transducer.[^f506] The SS2 manual's warranty covers the "Model SS-xx Series"
start-system modules as a family.[^f503] The name remained in use after the
product was discontinued: the current System 6 swimming software guide still
gives "a CTS Infinity, ChampStart or SS-2" as its examples of loudspeaker start
systems.[^f890]

CTS's current power-supply compatibility list names two legacy start systems,
`SS-01` and `SS-02`, without describing them. The
[Championship Start System (CHAMP-SSM)](champ-ssm.md) article reads these as
labels for the pre-2005 Championship generation, but the `SS-1`/SS2 series
documented here is another plausible referent. The sources do not settle it. The
numbering matches the SS2 era, but the listed power supply suits start
systems that run from an external supply, and the SS2 instead uses a dedicated
DC battery charger and cannot operate while charging.[^psu][^f503] An archived
CTS product page of December 2003 strengthens the Championship reading, since it
advertises that system as usable either from a 110 volt supply in a fixed
position or from its battery as a portable one, which is the capability the SS2
lacks.[^wbchamp] That is corroboration rather than proof, because a compatibility
list may name a charger loosely.

Archived copies of Colorado Time Systems' own site bracket the product's withdrawal.
By November 2001 the company's aquatic listing offered the Infinity and no longer
showed the SS2, yet its manuals index of March 2003 still carried SS2 documentation,
and only the Infinity and the SS2 had start-system manuals at that date.[^wb2001][^wb2003]
The SS2 was therefore out of the catalogue while remaining a supported product. A CTS
history page of the same period counts four generations of electronic horn start by
2001, though it does not name them, so the place of the SS2 in that sequence is not
established.[^wbhist]

Sources outside Colorado Time Systems are almost silent on the SS2. No dealer catalogue
listed it: the earliest archived Recreonics page for CTS start systems, from 2003, sells
the Infinity alone. That page is still useful, because it describes the Infinity in the SS2's own
accessory vocabulary, down to the 40-watt corrosion-resistant speaker, the second
speaker on a 125-foot cable for the far lane, the 6-watt under-block speakers and the
internal gel-cell battery. The accessory line therefore carried forward
intact.[^recreonics] Colorado Time Systems holds patents on its touchpad and on the
textured surface later sold as AquaGrip, but none covering a start system, a starting
signal or a false-start recall, so the SS2's design appears never to have been
patented.[^pat940][^pat799] A Colorado Time Systems advertisement in the September 1992
issue of Swimming World and Junior Swimmer places the company in the trade press during
the SS2's production life, and gives its Loveland, Colorado address five years before
the F503 manual does. The advertisement names no product, and its description of
the firm as "the leader in precision sport timing" is the company's own.[^sw1992]

## Design and hardware

The start module is an open-frame unit 13.25 in high, 18 in wide, and 4 in
deep, and it weighs 17 lb. Hook-and-loop straps hold a sliding polycarbonate weather
cover over its circuit board, and every connection lands directly on that board:
two identical speaker jacks, three identical microphone jacks, two timer-start
outputs, a backup button-start connector, and the strobe, battery, and recharge
connectors. A single volume control on the board sets the public-address level;
the start and recall tones always sound at full volume. The rear panel carries a
moulded rest that the microphone hangs on when it is not in
use. The module sits on a tabletop or mounts through a standard
1/4-20 tripod bracket or an optional wall-mount kit; the manual's setup
chapter names the optional tripod `TR-1` while its specifications page names
the `TR-2`, and it does not explain the difference.[^f503]

The module itself contains no built-in speaker: every speaker plugs into one
of the two speaker jacks on the circuit board. The base speaker is a reflex
design rated at 40 watts into 8 ohms and built to resist moisture and corrosion; the
multi-lane configurations use 6-watt under-block speakers on an `LS40` cable
harness, and the system can be expanded to a total of 20 speakers and three
microphones. Because starting blocks vary, CTS leaves permanent mounting of the
speakers to the customer.[^f503]

Power comes from an internal 12 VDC rechargeable gel-cell battery. CTS presents that as a
safety decision, since no mains supply then has to reach the deck. The
external charger (115 VAC, three-prong grounded) charges the battery only
with the module switched off, and the SS2 does not operate while charging;
CTS therefore advises recharging overnight after each day's use. A full
charge runs the system for one to two days of typical use, and heavy
public-address use shortens that; left unused, the battery drains by roughly a
fifth over a two- to three-month period. The optional `BP-3` external
battery pack matches the internal battery's capacity and extends operating
time, but cannot recharge the internal battery. A 4 A automotive blade fuse
protects the battery circuit.[^f503]

## Operation

The starter's microphone has a push-to-talk switch on its side and a
start button on top. Pressing push-to-talk alone carries the starter's voice
to the speakers as a public-address system. Holding push-to-talk and pressing
the start button fires the start: a 0.5-second dual-tone blast, a
simultaneous strobe flash, and a start pulse to the timer. Pressing the start
button a second time within five seconds signals a false-start recall, a
repeating dual tone; after five seconds the module resets and accepts another
start. With more than one microphone connected, a recall fails if two
starters press their buttons at the same time, so the manual directs that one
starter be designated to control recalls.[^f503]

The camera-type strobe flashes automatically with every start pulse and needs
about 30 seconds between flashes to recharge; a start given before the strobe
circuit has recharged sounds the tone without a flash. The manual tells anyone
timing by hand to watch for the flash rather than listen for the tone: sound
takes a measurable time to cross the deck, so a watch started on the tone runs
late by an amount that grows with distance from the nearest speaker. Research on
sprint starts has measured that penalty directly: reaction times lengthen
the farther a competitor sits from the source of the sound.[^brown] CTS notes a second
benefit, that a visible signal serves swimmers with a hearing impairment; see
the [start-systems overview](index.md) for the governing-body rules behind this
practice.[^f503] A weaker or longer start tone indicates a low battery.[^f503]

Both the tone and the flash are required by rule. USA Swimming's rule on
loudspeaker start systems obliges a meet to provide an electronic device that
generates both the starting and the recall signal. The same device must
fire a strobe, or some comparable visual signal, placed about 15 feet down the pool
from the starting end on whichever side the starter occupies, so that hand timers and
swimmers who cannot hear the tone still get the start. The rule leaves the speakers themselves to the installer,
and allows them under or beside each block, between alternate blocks, along the sides,
or behind the blocks, provided the sound reaches every lane
evenhandedly.[^usas103] USA Swimming prefers a system that meets that rule as
the starting device.[^usas103]

## Connections

The SS2 has two timer-start outputs on its circuit board: a
normally-open (N.O.) output used by all CTS timers, such as the
[System 5](../timers/system-5.md) Sports Timer, and a normally-closed (N.C.)
output for other manufacturers' timers. The later
[Championship](championship-start-system.md) dropped the built-in N.C.
output, which on that system requires factory modification.[^f503][^f875]
The start connection runs through a start jumper cable, model `SJ-25`
(25-foot) or `SJ-50M` (50-metre), to the timer's cable harness or to an
in-deck wiring plate; in System 5 installations it plugs into the start
connector of the touchpad-and-button-A cable harness.[^f503][^f506] Should the
microphone's start button fail, an optional hand-held pushbutton wired to the
button-start connector can fire the start instead.[^f503]

The manual specifies placing the module at least 10 feet (3 m) from the pool
edge, and choosing a spot where the strobe stays in view of the officials and
the crowd.[^f503]

## Part numbers and accessories

- `SS2-1` / `SS2-2` / `SS2-6` / `SS2-8` / `SS2-10`, the five sold
  configurations.[^f503]
- `LS40-6` / `-8` / `-10`, lane-speaker cable harness; the number is the lane
  count. The naming continues on the later Championship, which uses `LS40-6`
  through `LS40-12`.[^f503][^f875]
- `SP-125`, an extra 40 W speaker on a 125-foot lead, used to reach the far
  lanes; `SMK-1`, the backstroke-flag mounting kit that carries it.[^f503]
- `M-50` / `M-100` / `M-200`, replacement microphones; the number is the cable
  length in feet.[^f503]
- `SJ-25` / `SJ-50M`, start jumper cable to the timer (25 ft or 50 m).[^f503]
- `BP-3`, external battery pack.[^f503]
- `TR-1` / `TR-2`, optional tripod (the manual names both); wall-mount
  kit.[^f503]
- Factory replacement parts: circuit board, speakers, cables, strobe, battery,
  battery charger, and front panel.[^f503]
