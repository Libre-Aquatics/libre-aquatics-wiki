The StartTime III is an electronic starting device made by Swiss Timing Ltd of
Corgémont, Switzerland. It generates the start signal for a race: a sound through its
built-in loudspeaker and any connected external loudspeakers, an optical flash, and a
start pulse to the timing device.[^manual] The flash and the loudspeaker are built into
the same case, and the starter gives the start from a microphone unit or from the device
itself; the unit predates the electronic e-gun trigger, which Swiss Timing introduced
with the successor [StartTime IV](starttime-iv.md).[^manual][^egun] It succeeded the
[StartTime II](starttime-ii.md), the earlier unit in the family, which was configured by
DIP switch rather than the setup menu of the III. Swiss Timing no longer publishes the
manual for the discontinued device online, so this article cites it by document number
rather than hosting a copy.[^manual] This article covers the StartTime III specifically;
the [start-systems overview](index.md) covers the shared background that applies to
every starter.

## Design and hardware

The main unit measures 376 by 215 by 195 mm without its connectors and is protected to
IP41. Setup is menu-driven through an LCD and a turn-knob, with a POWER and a READY
status LED on the unit. Power comes from a built-in 12 volt, 7 Ah sealed lead-acid
battery with a charger; Swiss Timing rates the battery for 24 hours or 1000 starts, and
a low-battery warning shows four levels of charge. The operating range is -10 to +65
degrees C, the storage range -20 to +65 degrees C, and the unit is rated for 20 to 80
percent relative humidity, non-condensing.[^manual]

The rear panel carries connections for the microphone (an 8-pin DIN 45326 connector),
the ARES timing device (a 4-pin Tuchel connector), the flash (4-pin Tuchel), the
speaker (4-pin Tuchel), and the charger (3-pin Tuchel), which also accepts an external
12 volt battery. The unit is fused by three car-type fuses (F1 1 A slow, F2 5 A slow,
and F3 2 A slow) and one 5 by 20 mm fuse (F4 6.3 A slow).[^manual]

### Microphone unit

The starter gives the start from a microphone unit that holds a SPEAK button, which
amplifies the verbal commands, and a START button, which begins the race. The
microphone unit measures 118 by 25 by 80 mm and carries a 7 m cable. The unit can also
be run without the microphone unit; the starter then gives the start from the START
button on the device itself.[^manual]

## Operation

The StartTime III runs in one of three modes. In ARES mode it connects to a Swiss
Timing ARES 21 timing device; the READY indicator shows whether the timing operator has
cleared the system for a start, and the unit announces a false start if it is triggered
without that clearance. In PowerTime mode the unit runs on its own without an external
timing device. In its independent mode the unit is used without the microphone unit,
with the start given from the device's own START button.[^manual]

A second press of START within 10 seconds of the start signals a false-start recall and
plays the false-start sound a configurable number of times; after the 10 second window
the system resets for a new start. The setup menu selects the start and false-start
sound, sets the flash and start outputs to a normally-open or normally-closed contact,
enables or disables the false-start announcement, sets the number of false-start
signals, and sets the automatic standby and power-off timers.[^manual]

## Audio

The StartTime III contains a built-in loudspeaker driven by two integrated amplifiers
with independent outputs. The recommended external load is 3.2 to 16 ohms per channel;
at 13.2 volts into 3.2 ohms at 1 kHz the amplifiers deliver a typical 2 x 13 W at less
than 1 percent distortion and a maximum 2 x 19 W at 10 percent distortion. A SPEAKER
output drives a chain of loudspeakers so that the start signal reaches each athlete as
closely and simultaneously as possible.[^manual] In swimming, those outputs drive Swiss
Timing's [in-block and mobile lane speakers](../external-speaker/swiss-timing.md),
positioned so that all swimmers hear the start at the same instant.[^manual]

## Connections

The microphone cable can be extended to 30 m and the loudspeaker cables to 50 m.[^manual]
An external loudspeaker (article 2850.712) and mobile loudspeaker harness sets (article
3399.931, with harnessed sets carrying two to ten speakers on 1.5 mm² cable) place a
speaker near each starting block for portable installations.[^manual] The OMEGA Flash
Start (article 3399.951), a visual start signal for athletes who are deaf or hard of
hearing, connects to the unit as an auxiliary flash positioned where the athlete can see
it.[^manual] These 3399-series peripherals carried forward to the
[StartTime IV](starttime-iv.md).[^stiv]

## Compared with the StartTime IV

The StartTime IV succeeds the StartTime III and shares its overall approach: an
electronic starting device with a built-in loudspeaker, an integrated flash, and outputs
for per-lane loudspeakers. The main change is the trigger. On the StartTime III the start
comes from a microphone unit or the device's own button, whereas the StartTime IV is sold
in two versions, one with a start and microphone unit and one with an electronic e-gun
whose trigger plays the sound, fires the flash, and sends the start pulse
together.[^manual][^stiv] Swiss Timing introduced the e-gun as a new-generation flash
gun to replace the powder pistol and its transducer, and first used it at the 2010
Vancouver Winter Olympic Games with the then-new StartTime IV.[^egun][^core77] The
main-unit dimensions also differ slightly: the StartTime III measures 376 by 215 by 195
mm and the StartTime IV 376 by 215 by 205 mm.[^manual][^stiv]
