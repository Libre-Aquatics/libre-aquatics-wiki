The StartTime IV is an electronic starting device made by Swiss Timing Ltd of Corgémont,
Switzerland. It generates the start signal for a race: a sound through its built-in high
fidelity loudspeaker and any connected external loudspeakers, an optical flash, and a
start pulse to the timing device.[^datasheet] It manages up to three sports or
disciplines and is sold in two versions, with a start and microphone unit (article
3481.900) or with an e-gun trigger (article 3481.901).[^datasheet] It is the predecessor
of the [StartTime V](starttime-v.md); the later unit's e-gun and microphone unit are not
compatible with the StartTime IV.[^stv] Swiss Timing no longer publishes the datasheet
for the discontinued device online, so this article cites it by document number rather
than hosting a copy.[^datasheet] This article covers the StartTime IV specifically; the
[start-systems overview](index.md) covers the shared background that applies to every
starter.

## Naming and product line

Swiss Timing introduced the e-gun as a new-generation electronic flash gun to replace
the powder pistol and its transducer, and first used it at the 2010 Vancouver Winter
Olympic Games, operated with the then-new StartTime IV.[^egun] Independent coverage of
the design notes that the gun makes no report of its own and sounds only through the
loudspeakers, so that every athlete hears the start at the same instant rather than as
the sound reaches them across the pool or track; the same account describes an earlier,
unsuccessful attempt to wire a real pistol to loudspeakers, to which athletes reacted to
the live shot rather than the speaker sound.[^core77] The acoustic reasoning, that a
single poolside signal reaches the far lanes later than the near ones, is set out in the
[start-systems overview](index.md).

Swiss Timing presents the StartTime IV as combining a new design with what it calls the
latest security and safety standards.[^datasheet] The device was succeeded by the
[StartTime V](starttime-v.md) in 2015.[^stv]

## Design and hardware

The main unit measures 376 by 215 by 205 mm without its connectors, weighs 6.4 kg
without the charger and microphone, and is protected to IP41. Setup is menu-driven
through a multifunction LCD and a keypad, with presets for up to three sports or
disciplines, and the unit carries an enhanced internal amplifier and an integrated
optical flash. Power comes from an integrated 12 volt, 7 Ah lead-acid battery with an
indoor-only charger; the battery holds its charge for up to 12 months when not in use,
a battery indicator shows four levels of charge, and Swiss Timing rates the battery for
more than 1000 starts before a recharge. The operating range is -10 to +55 degrees C,
the storage range -20 to +65 degrees C, and the unit is rated for 20 to 80 percent
relative humidity, non-condensing.[^datasheet]

The unit has two start-out connections, and its rear panel carries connections for the
microphone or e-gun, two timing devices, the flash, the speaker, a line out, the
battery charger, a telephone line, and a serial RS422 line.[^datasheet]

### E-gun and microphone unit

In the microphone-unit version, the starter gives the start from the microphone unit
while the microphone amplifies the verbal commands. A pair of LEDs on that unit report
what the timing device is doing, and it will also take an optional external microphone
and headset. The microphone unit measures
118 by 25 by 80 mm and carries a 7 m cable. A telephone line accepts a telephone
headset for clearer communication.[^datasheet]

In the e-gun version, pulling the trigger of the electronic gun plays the sound, fires
the flash, and sends the start pulse to the timing device simultaneously, and a second
pull within a programmable delay plays the false-start sound. The start and false-start
sounds can be changed or downloaded from a computer over an RS422 serial line.[^datasheet]
Swiss Timing describes the e-gun as a new-generation flash gun that replaces the powder
gun and its transducer; the company notes that recent import and security laws make it
difficult or prohibited to import powder starting guns in most countries, and that the
e-gun is optional on the StartTime IV, which runs either way according to the sport, the
discipline, or what a given client wants.[^datasheet]

The e-gun measures 280 by 35 by 140 mm without its cable, weighs 350 g without the
cable or 1 kg with it, and carries a 7 m cable. It takes a 7 to 18 volt DC input, draws
up to 1.6 A at 12 V DC and 10 mA in standby with the flash loaded, and is protected to
IP65, a higher rating than the main unit. Its integrated flash lasts 0.1 s, is visible
in sunlight beyond 200 m, and radiates 360 degrees horizontally and 90 degrees
vertically.[^datasheet]

## Audio

The StartTime IV contains a high fidelity loudspeaker and an enhanced internal
amplifier, with a public-address output of up to 2 x 19 W. Optional loudspeakers connect
to a line out so that the start signal reaches each athlete as closely and
simultaneously as possible.[^datasheet] In swimming, those outputs drive Swiss Timing's
[in-block and mobile lane speakers](../external-speaker/swiss-timing.md), positioned so
that all swimmers hear the start at the same instant.[^datasheet]

## Connections

A cable on winder (article 1865.050) links the StartTime IV to the timing device over a
50 m cable, and a multipurpose tripod (article 9051.6339, rated up to 8 kg) mounts the
unit on deck.[^datasheet] The e-gun is supplied with a starter headset (article
3481.981, one ear and a microphone) or a starter telephone (article 3481.982, two ears
and a microphone).[^datasheet]

The OMEGA Flash Start (article 3399.951), a visual start signal for athletes who are
deaf or hard of hearing, is powered by the StartTime IV and connects with a 25 m
cable.[^datasheet] For portable installations, mobile loudspeakers (article 3399.931)
are placed near each starting block and sold as harnessed sets for two to ten speakers
(articles 3399.932.06 through 3399.940.03), where the suffix gives the cable length
between speakers.[^datasheet]

## Compared with the StartTime V

The StartTime V succeeds the StartTime IV, but Swiss Timing states that its e-gun and
microphone unit will not work on the older device.[^stv] The two share
the same overall approach, an electronic starting device with a built-in loudspeaker,
integrated flash, and outputs for per-lane loudspeakers, but the specifications differ.
The StartTime IV main unit measures 376 by 215 by 205 mm and weighs 6.4 kg, is
protected to IP41, and delivers up to 2 x 19 W; the StartTime V main unit is smaller at
395 by 192 by 160 mm and lighter at 5.8 kg, is protected to IP43, and delivers up to
2 x 20 W.[^datasheet][^stv] The StartTime IV lists a telephone line and two start-out
connections among its features; the StartTime V pairs its e-gun (820 g, IP64) with a
handheld microphone unit and a 50 m cable on winder.[^datasheet][^stv]
