The PS-1400 is an electronic starting device for competitive swimming made by
[Seiko](../../../vendors/seiko.md) Time Creation Inc. of Tokyo. It generates the start
tone and an alarm tone electronically and sends a start signal to the printing timer,
so that the tone the
athletes hear and the pulse that starts the race clock come from the same trigger
rather than from a microphone picking up a gunshot.[^jp][^en] Unlike the company's
portable [Electronic Starting System](seiko-electronic-starting-system.md), it is
sold as swimming equipment and is certified for the sport: Seiko states that it
conforms to World Aquatics rules and is certified Class AA and Class A by the Japan
Swimming Federation.[^catalogue][^jp] This article is a starting point; the
[start-systems overview](index.md) covers the shared background that applies to
every starter.

## Naming and product line

The product's name differs between Seiko's Japanese and English material, and the
model number is the only stable identifier. In Japanese it is 電子式スタート音発生装置,
"electronic start sound generator", the name used on the Japanese product page and
throughout the company's swimming catalogue. On Seiko's English swimming pages the
same `PS-1400` is labelled an "Electronic Starting System", which is also the English
name the company gives its unrelated portable
[PS-110J](seiko-electronic-starting-system.md).[^jp][^en][^enps110]

The [PS-1300](seiko-ps-1300.md) is the discontinued predecessor, replaced by this unit
between February and May 2026. It shared the main unit's DC 12 V 17 Ah sealed
lead-acid battery, its 264 by 203 by 390 mm case and its approximate 14.5 kg weight,
and the same aluminium pistol; before it came the much larger
[PS-1200](seiko-ps-1200.md).[^disc1300][^wb2026] Two further starting
products appear in the PS-110J manual, which lists the equipment that unit is not
compatible with: a `PS-1300B` electronic start sound generator, whose pistol is the
strobe-equipped `PF-125J`, and a `PS-200B` start signal generator, whose pistol is
the `PS-210S`.[^manual]

Seiko markets the starter as one part of a certified system rather than as a
stand-alone unit. The catalogue applies the same pair of compliance lines, World
Aquatics rules and Japan Swimming Federation Class AA and Class A, to the starting
block, the backstroke ledge, the lane box, the
[AQUA-type touch plate](../touchpad/seiko-aqua-touch-plate.md) and the
`PT-9000` printing timer.[^catalogue]

## Design and hardware

The main unit sits on the deck rather than being carried by hand. It measures
264 W × 203 H × 390 D mm excluding protrusions, weighs about 14.5 kg, and runs from
a small sealed lead-acid battery of DC 12 V and 17 Ah. The catalogue notes that a
splash-proof cover is supplied, a difference from the portable PS-110J, whose manual
states that neither its pistol nor its speaker box is drip-proof.[^catalogue][^manual]

The starter holds a pistol and a separate starter box. The pistol is aluminium,
210 W × 40 H × 145 D mm, and weighs about 0.4 kg without its batteries and cord,
running on two AA cells; those figures match the ones Seiko gives for the `PS-78`
pistol supplied with the portable system, which it specifies more precisely at 410 g,
though the company does not give the PS-1400 pistol a part number of its
own.[^enps110] Seiko places the strobe on top of the pistol, which
it describes as an easily viewable position.[^en][^catalogue] The starter box measures
183 W × 62 H × 150 D mm and weighs about 1.3 kg.[^en] Catalogue photographs show the
pistol supplied with a headset microphone on a coiled cable, so the starter's
commands and the start tone are given through the same equipment.[^catalogue]

## Operation

Pulling the trigger generates the start sound and sends the start signal. Seiko
states that the start signal reaches the printing timer at the same time as the
sound is produced, and that two start sounds are selectable, an imitation gunshot
and a beep.[^en][^jp] The unit also produces an alarm tone, used for
recalls.[^catalogue]

Seiko's stated reason for the design is that no propellant is involved, so there are
no misfires and no smoke, which it contrasts with a blank-cartridge
pistol.[^catalogue][^jp]

The claim that the tone and the timer pulse are simultaneous is the manufacturer's.
World Aquatics rules do not put it in those terms; they require that automatic
officiating equipment be activated by the starter, and that the timer be initiated
manually by the starter.[^wacr] What the rules do require in the terms that matter
here is that the starter have a microphone for oral commands, that a pistol, if used,
be used with a transducer, and that both be connected to loudspeakers at each
starting block so the commands and the starting signal are heard equally and
simultaneously by every athlete.[^wacr]

## Audio

The main unit carries its own front-mounted speaker. For wider coverage Seiko sells a
speaker expansion box, listed on its website as the `SPE-1300`, though the catalogue
names it only as スピーカ拡張ボックス with no model number.[^spe][^catalogue] It is a
stainless-steel cabinet with a yellow baked finish measuring 450 W × 740 H × 470 D mm
excluding protrusions, weighing about 35 kg alone and about 50 kg with the generator
mounted on it. Four speakers are fitted, two to each side, and Seiko states that this
gives maximum volume over a range of more than 270 degrees. The cabinet doubles as
the trolley the generator rides on, and Seiko notes it can also be used with the
earlier `PS-1300`.[^spe]

Seiko does not publish an output wattage, a sound pressure level, an impedance, a
driver count for the main unit, or a number of speaker outputs for the PS-1400. The
catalogue and the product pages give the physical and power specifications only.

Getting the start signal to each athlete at the block is a separate matter from the
generator's own speakers. Seiko's English page for the `SB-400` starting block lists
a built-in speaker as an option alongside the backstroke ledge; the Japanese
catalogue's starting-block entry does not mention one.[^block][^catalogue] That
option is how the system meets the per-block loudspeaker requirement described in
the [start-systems overview](index.md).

## Connections

The generator sits at the head of a wired chain that ends at the printing timer.
The pistol and headset connect to the main unit by cable, and the main unit sends the
start signal onward to the timer.[^catalogue][^jp] Seiko's Japanese material names
that timer the `PT-9000`, which replaced the `PT-8000` in the same 2026 refresh that
replaced the [PS-1300](seiko-ps-1300.md) with this starter.[^wb2026] The English
swimming pages still list a `PT-8000X` instead, a number that appears nowhere in the
current Japanese material and whose 400-race memory matches neither the `PT-9000` nor the
`PT-8000`, both of which Seiko gives as 600 races. It is not simply the English name for
the same machine.[^swimidx][^enpt][^pt9000][^pt8000] The `PT-9000` is the only printing
timer in the current catalogue.[^catalogue] The timer is rated for up to 10 lanes,
resolves finishes to a hundredth of a second, and can measure reaction time at the
start.[^pt9000]

Seiko's own figures for the `PT-9000` are not consistent. Its product page repeats the
case dimensions given on the `PT-8000` page, W474 × H136 × D328 mm, where the catalogue
gives the newer timer as W480 × H142 × D334 mm; both put the weight at about 8.0 kg. The
catalogue is the more recent document and the one specific to this system, so it is
preferred here.[^pt9000][^pt8000][^catalogue]

The lane signals travel separately. A lane box is installed for each lane, connecting
that lane's starting block and [touch plate](../touchpad/seiko-aqua-touch-plate.md) and
passing to the printing timer both the
instant the swimmer leaves the block and the instant the touch plate is
touched.[^catalogue] Signal and power distribution between the equipment runs through
connector boxes that Seiko builds to suit the competition and the
facility.[^catalogue]

Seiko does not publish the connector types or pin assignments for the PS-1400, and
the catalogue's system illustration is pictorial rather than a labelled block
diagram, so the wiring is documented only at the level described above.

## Starts for deaf and hard-of-hearing swimmers

Seiko sells a light-stimulus start signal system, 光刺激スタート合図システム, for
competitions involving athletes with disabilities. It receives a start signal
interlocked with the printing timer and presents it on display boards, flash lights
and start lamps, and it can also be operated manually. For an eight-lane pool the
configuration is two display boards, one control panel, four flash lights, sixteen
start lamps, two batteries and a cable set. The display boards carry legends reading
"Take your marks" and "Start".[^lightsig][^catalogue]

Its certification differs from the rest of the system. Seiko lists it as Japan
Swimming Federation Class A only, with no World
Aquatics compliance line, where the starter, the blocks, the lane boxes and the timer
all carry both.[^catalogue] That is consistent with the rules themselves: the World
Aquatics swimming regulations do not provide for a visual start signal, and the
provision sits instead in World Para Swimming rule 11.1.6, which requires a strobe or
starting light for athletes with a hearing impairment and allows a team leader to
request additional arm signals from the starter.[^wps]

The approach long predates the current product. A 1992 Seiko patent for a swimming
race timing system describes a loudspeaker built into the starting block together
with visual start indication for swimmers with a hearing impairment, using ready and
start lamps, a character panel reading READY and GO, and strobe flash lamps placed in
front of and behind the swimmer at both the blocks and the turning end.[^patent] The
patent is assigned to Seiko Instruments Inc., a separate company within the Seiko
group from the manufacturer of this equipment.[^patent][^patentus]

## In competition

The Japan Swimming Federation lists Seiko on its own sponsor page as an official
timekeeping partner.[^jasf] Seiko goes further, describing itself as the sole such
partner and dating the relationship to 2003, neither of which the federation's page
states, and says it provides timing support for the Japan Swimming Championships, the
National Sports Festival, the national high school and junior high school
championships, the intercollegiate championships, the JOC Junior Olympic Cup and the
Japan Open.[^catalogue][^seikohistory]

Outside Japan the record is thinner. Seiko timed the swimming at the 2001 World
Aquatics Championships in Fukuoka, the only edition it is known to have timed, and the
touchpads malfunctioned repeatedly over the eight days of competition. Times had to be
corrected from backup timing or video evidence, and the system recorded an early
take-off of minus 0.06 s that disqualified the United States women's 4 × 200 m
freestyle relay on a successful British protest. The disqualification stood, and the
governing body, then FINA, instead awarded the United States a second set of gold
medals without changing the official result. It defended the equipment and said
swimmers had to strike the pads in the right way, then signed a three-event deal with
Swiss Timing covering the 2002 world short-course championships in Moscow and the 2003
and 2005 championships in Barcelona and Montreal.[^sw2001][^wp2001] Omega has been an
official partner of World Aquatics since 1973, and timed the 2023 championships when
they returned to Fukuoka.[^omega][^wt2023]

The [Seiko](../../../vendors/seiko.md) vendor page sets this alongside the company's
much better documented record in athletics timing.
