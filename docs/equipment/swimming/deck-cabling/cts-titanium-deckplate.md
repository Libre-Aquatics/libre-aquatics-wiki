The Titanium Deckplate (`TDP`) is the in-deck lane plate made by
[Colorado Time Systems](../../../vendors/colorado-time-systems.md) (CTS). One is set into
the deck beside each starting block, and every piece of lane equipment gets its own jack on it:
touchpad, three backup buttons, start, lane speaker, and a relay judging platform together with
its speedlight. The jacks have titanium contacts set into a domed top that sheds water from
between them, a design CTS patented in 2013.[^tdpsheet][^patentplate] CTS makes the plate in
two versions. The passive plate is wired back through the wall plate to a parallel CTS timer. The intelligent
plate sits on the [Gen7 Serial Timer](../timers/gen7-serial.md)'s data bus and can report what
is plugged into it.[^f1034][^video]

## Role in the timing system

This section places the plate in a CTS installation. A deck plate is the fixed end of a
facility's in-deck wiring: cable runs from it under the deck to the timing location, and the
officials plug the lane's equipment into it for a meet. Pools wired with on-deck cable harnesses
have no deck plates.[^archg7] The Titanium Deckplate succeeded CTS's earlier 7.5 in square
`DP-7` plate, and CTS offers it as a direct replacement in the same opening.[^tdpsheet] Its
2015 catalog lists two in-deck options, the Titanium Deckplate and the
[QuickConnect deck plate](cts-quickconnect-deck-plate.md), which moves the jacks up onto the
starting block. CTS now marks the QuickConnect as discontinued.[^catalog][^g7ds]

See the [deck cabling overview](index.md) for the background shared by every deck plate and
harness: deck-box placement, conduit sizing, and the other manufacturers' layouts.

## Design and construction

The plate is a square moulding fixed with four corner screws. Its top carries seven raised
lobes, each with a moulded label. Buttons A, B and C run down the left side, the touchpad,
start and speaker jacks down the right, and the relay judging jack sits at the bottom. Each lobe
has two jacks for a two-pin banana plug, one on the lobe and one on a ring around a flat
centre. The relay judging position has three.[^tdpsheet]

The layout differs from CTS's earlier standard plate. That plate had a flat face with six
two-jack positions, for the touchpad, start, speaker and buttons A, B and C, and no position of its
own for a relay platform.[^arch] On those plates the platform shares the button A jack, through a
cable whose plug stacks with the button's, and the speedlight takes a separate round
connector.[^f968] The Titanium Deckplate gives the platform and its speedlight their own three-pin
position.[^tdpsheet]

CTS's patent explains the shape. On a flat plate, a film of splashed water can lie across two
contacts and carry a small current between them, and that current drives electrolytic
corrosion. Setting each contact on a slope steep enough for the film to drain off breaks the
path. The patent's preferred form joins the slopes into a dome with the contacts in a
semicircle, and it names titanium for the contacts.[^patentplate] Its description adds that the
raised lobes take sideways knocks that would otherwise reach the plugs, and that the cavity
under the contacts is filled with potting compound.[^patentplate] A continuation patent with
the same disclosure followed in 2014.[^patentcont]

CTS's own descriptions add that the titanium resists chlorine and bromine, that the housing
will not yellow in sunlight, and that the shape protects the banana plugs. It also claims a
better signal from the drier contacts. These are the company's claims, and no independent test
of them has been found.[^tdpsheet][^g7ds]

## Models

CTS sells two versions of the plate under the same name and patent. Neither its product page
nor its datasheets set out the difference in one place; it has to be pieced together from the
timer manuals.

### Passive plate

The original plate is a passive set of jacks with no electronics. Its pre-wired cable runs
under the deck to the facility's wall plate, and the timer connects there.[^archg7] The March
2014 sales sheet describes this version, and CTS's architectural guidelines for the [Gen7 Legacy Timer](../timers/gen7-legacy.md) list it as the facility's
in-deck plate.[^tdpsheet][^archg7] On a passive installation the speedlights on the relay
platforms are fed from the start system through the in-deck wiring, so the starter has to be
connected to the deck for them to flash.[^f1064]

### Intelligent plate

The second version is built for the Gen7 Serial system, announced in 2017, which replaces
point-to-point wiring with a powered data bus shared by the deckplates and the
[Gen7 connect hubs](gen7-serial-connect-hubs.md) for the timer, starter, scoreboard and
bulkheads.[^g7ds][^f1034] CTS titles its video on this version "Intelligent Titanium
Deckplates", and its Gen7 datasheet calls the plates "smart".[^video][^g7ds] CTS's Gen7 Serial
presentation video shows a plate with the same seven labelled lobes and fifteen jacks as the
2014 sales sheet, under the same patent number.[^g7ds][^g7video]

The Gen7 Serial Timer manual credits these plates with two abilities of their own:[^f1034]

- Input detection. The plate identifies which kind of device is plugged into each position,
  and the timer greys out a missing input and marks a mismatched one in orange. The check takes
  a few seconds and works only on in-deck systems, not on cable harnesses.
- Speedlight testing. The plate measures the current drawn by the speedlight on its relay
  platform, and the timer reports each lane's light as working, damaged or missing, without
  firing a start.

The timer's general diagnostics, which also cover cable harnesses, show each input as open,
closed, shorted or corroded, so a fault can be traced to the device or to the wiring.[^f1034]

Speedlights are also handled differently. The intelligent plate powers its speedlight itself
when the start signal arrives, so the starter needs no separate speedlight connection to the
deck.[^f1064] CTS's 2014 patent application on component detection shows how this kind of
identification can work. A processor applies a voltage step to the connected device and
compares the resulting voltage curve with stored signatures, and its example hub has the same
set of connections as this plate.[^patentdetect] CTS has not said which method its shipping
plates use.

CTS does not publish a separate part number for the intelligent plate. Dealers list the
Titanium Deckplate as `TDP-200` without saying which version that is.[^dealer]

## Installation and mounting

This section covers what is specific to this plate. The deck box, the pre-wired cable, the
flush-fit tolerance and where the box goes relative to the starting block are the same for every
CTS deck plate and are described in the [deck cabling overview](index.md). CTS's architectural
guidelines show the Titanium Deckplate set flush into a tiled deck, and it fits the opening left
by an older 7.5 in `DP-7` plate, so an existing installation can be converted without new deck
boxes.[^archg7][^tdpsheet]

The two versions make different demands on the conduit. Each passive plate's own cable runs back
through the conduit to the wall plate, which is why CTS limits how many deck boxes may share a
run: four on 1.5 in conduit and two on 1 in, with the full rule in the overview.[^archg7] For the
intelligent plate CTS specifies 1.5 in PVC conduit between every deckplate and wall-plate box,
and it says the Gen7 Serial system needs less cabling than its predecessors.[^hubsds][^g7ds]

## Connection to the timer

Most lane devices use an ordinary two-pin banana plug: touchpads,
[pushbuttons](../semi-automatic/pushbutton/cts.md) and lane speakers.[^tdpsheet] The relay
judging position takes a three-pin plug that carries both the platform's take-off signal and
its speedlight, so no separate speedlight harness is needed.[^tdpsheet] CTS's speedlight relay
platforms use cable `R-015-046` for this jack. The older DP-series plates need `R-015-027`
instead, which has a stackable banana plug for the platform and a separate round speedlight
connector.[^f968]

A passive plate reaches the timer through the facility wiring and the wall plate. An
intelligent plate reaches the Gen7 Serial Timer over the bus: the timer's cable plugs into a timer hub
in the deck or the wall, and the start system plugs into a starter hub on the same bus.[^f1034][^f1064]

## Care and maintenance

CTS's cleaning sheet for this plate is shorter than the one for its older plates. The
earlier routine for plates and harnesses calls for dielectric grease in every banana jack before
use, then alcohol, a fresh-water rinse and more grease afterwards.[^f897] For the titanium
plate, the sheet asks only that any debris be removed from the sockets before each use, and it
rules out abrasive tools and chemicals.[^f1023] Lime-scale remover is reserved for plugs that
have scaled up. If those plugs stain the plate's plastic, the same diluted remover can be
wiped over the plastic and rinsed off with fresh water.[^f1023]

The Gen7 Serial Timer manual disagrees. Its general cleaning steps for cable and deck plate
jacks include soaking them in rubbing alcohol, swabbing, rinsing, and then applying silicone
grease.[^f1034] CTS's 2024 maintenance checklist differs from both: it keeps the debris check before
each use, and adds a monthly coat of Strikehold spray on the titanium connectors, a protective
chemical the plate's own sheet does not mention.[^checklist] This article follows the plate's own
sheet as the more specific source. CTS has not reconciled the three.
