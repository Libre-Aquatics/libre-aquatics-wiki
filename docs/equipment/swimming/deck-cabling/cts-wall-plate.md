The Colorado Time Systems (CTS) wall plate is the panel set into a pool wall where a
facility's in-deck timing wiring ends. Cable from the deck plates at each lane runs under the
deck in conduit to a box behind the plate, and at a meet the officials plug the
[timing console](../timers/index.md), the start system and the scoreboard link into the plate
rather than laying a harness along the pool edge.[^arch][^archg7] CTS has made the plate in
three forms. An earlier black plate was made in layouts for one pool end or several. The
aluminium Universal Wall Plate that followed it serves several ends, the diving judges and a
video display from one panel. A Gen7 Serial plate
carries keyed connection nodes with status lights on the timer's data bus.[^f147][^uwp][^catalog]

## Role in the timing system

This section places the plate in a CTS installation. Pools with on-deck
[cable harnesses](cts-cable-harness.md) have no wall plate: the harness plugs straight into the
console. In an in-deck installation each lane's
[deck plate](cts-titanium-deckplate.md) has its own pre-wired cable, and on a parallel-wired
system those cables are terminated behind the wall plate, which gathers them onto multi-pin
sockets for the console.[^archg7] The plate is also where the start system's audio, start and
strobe lines meet the in-deck speaker and speedlight wiring, where the scoreboard data cable
leaves for the display, and, on diving installations, where the judges' terminals
connect.[^arch][^f1064]

CTS asks for the plate to be placed right beside the timing area, and its site survey
asks for each plate's location, size and use in every sport set-up.[^archg7] Larger pools can
have several: CTS's sample 50 m layouts put separate wall boxes at the timing table, the diving
well and a timing office.[^archg7]

See the [deck cabling overview](index.md) for the background shared by every deck plate and
wall plate: deck-box placement, conduit sizing, and the other manufacturers' layouts.

## Versions

CTS sells all of its plates under the one name, and its documents do not number the versions.
The three below are told apart by their photographs and the timers they serve.

### Black plate

The 2014 edition of CTS's touchpad manual illustrates its wall plate cleaning instructions with
a square black plate. It carries the older CTS logo and one group of connections for a single
pool end: one 50-pin socket for the touchpads and buttons A, B and C, two start jacks, a speaker
socket, two scoreboard jacks and a microphone socket. A small part label on the plate is not
legible in the manual.[^f147] The 2026 edition of the same manual replaces the photograph with a
drawing of the aluminium Universal plate.[^f147new] A 2024 CTS cleaning video shows a second
black plate, glossy and screwed to a block wall, with one 50-pin socket, start and speaker jacks,
and a grid of jacks for diving judging terminals.[^cleanvideo] The fullest black layout
matches the later aluminium plate's, with a timing group for each pool end and one for a short
course, each with a 50-pin socket and start and speaker jacks, and a block of ten judging-terminal jacks. It
adds scoreboard data and microphone sockets and two display connections labelled for CTS's
[Myriad](../../common/scoreboard/myriad.md) display and RGB, and its logo reads Colorado Time
Systems LLC where the single-end plate reads Inc. The black plate was therefore made in several
configurations, as the Universal plate later was.

CTS's product page for its covers mentions an older "standard ABS wallplate", and a retrofit
cover is made to fit it.[^uwp] That establishes an earlier plastic plate. No CTS document says
whether the black plate in the touchpad manual is that ABS plate, and the identification here
rests on the photograph alone. CTS was already warranting its wall plates in
1997, when its start-system manual listed them beside the Model 4000A timer.[^f503]

### Universal Wall Plate

The Universal Wall Plate is a 15 in (38.1 cm) square aluminium panel, 0.25 in (0.64 cm) thick,
delivered with its wiring and terminals in place.[^uwp] CTS describes it in a 2014 sheet and in
both editions of its architectural guidelines, for the [System 6](../timers/system-6.md) and the
[Gen7 Legacy Timer](../timers/gen7-legacy.md).[^uwp][^arch][^archg7] The plate is ordered by
how many pool ends it times:[^uwp]

- `WLPA`, near end only
- `WLPA-F`, near and far ends
- `WLPA-S`, near end and a short course
- `WLPA-FS`, near end, far end and short course
- `K-DV`, diving option
- `K-12`, 12-lane option

Blank aluminium plates of the same size are made to cover a box kept for later
expansion.[^uwp] As of 2026 CTS still lists the plate as a current product, quoted on request,
and one US dealer sells the basic `WLPA` for $850 as a part that needs professional
installation. Two UK dealers list it under the shared code `CE-3310`, one at £897 before VAT,
and one of them describes options for start systems and Titanium Deckplates.[^uwp][^g7ds][^dealer]

The fully equipped plate in CTS's photographs has three framed timing groups, labelled near-end
timing, far-end timing and short course. Each group has a 50-pin socket for the touchpads and
the three backup buttons, plus start and speaker jacks. The near-end and short-course groups
also have a round start-system socket and sockets for a strobe and the
speedlights. A separate socket carries lanes 11 and 12. Below these are two scoreboard sockets
(A and B), each with an auxiliary socket beside it, two fiber-optic outputs for a matrix video
display, and a row of ten judging-terminal jacks, J1 to J10.[^arch][^f147new] The judging jacks
are the diving option; see [judging terminals](../../diving/judging-terminals.md).

CTS's current start-system manuals call this the "legacy wallplate", as opposed to the Gen7
Serial plate.[^f1064][^f1063]

### Gen7 Serial wall plate

The [Gen7 Serial Timer](../timers/gen7-serial.md) replaces point-to-point wiring with a powered
data bus, and its wall plate is built around the bus's
[connect hubs](gen7-serial-connect-hubs.md). CTS's catalog shows a square plate with the Gen7
mark and four keyed positions in a two-by-two grid, labelled timer, starter, scoreboard and
bulkhead, each hub coloured to match its cable. Small status lights sit beside each position,
and the photographs show them lit once cables are plugged in.[^catalog] The diving manual gives
one use of the lights: the scoreboard node's light shows red when the scoreboard data line is
short of power.[^f1022]

A Gen7 wall plate has no fixed layout. Each one is fitted with whichever connect hubs are needed
at that spot, so one plate may carry a timer and a starter node while another elsewhere in the
building carries only a scoreboard node. The plate in CTS's catalog has its bulkhead position
blanked off.[^catalog] CTS presents the bus as letting a facility choose where the timer, start
system, deck clocks, portable scoreboards and bulkheads plug in, through a wall plate or a plate
in the floor.[^g7ds][^catalog] CTS's patent on the system calls the wall-mounted connection a
course connection node. One such node can stand in for several conventional wall plates and
scoreboard and data connections. A pool end may have more than one, and outdoor pools without
walls can house them in tombstones, monuments or parapets.[^patentnodes]

On this system the wall plate is also optional. The same hubs can be set in the deck instead,
and CTS's catalog says the bus lets a facility do without wall plates altogether.[^catalog][^g7ds]
Course layouts are set in the timer's software, so a pool no longer needs a different plate for
each course.[^g7ds] CTS also offers an office
wall plate, which lets the timer stay in a locked room away from the deck; this is offered for
the Gen7 Legacy as well.[^g7ds][^catalog] CTS has not published a part number or dimensions for
any Gen7 wall plate. The starter node mounted in a wall plate is `WPI-S2`, against `TDPI-S2` for
the same node in the deck.[^f1064]

## Installation and mounting

This section follows CTS's architectural guidelines for the aluminium plate. The plate fits a 12 × 12 × 6 in (30.5 × 30.5 × 15.2 cm) wall junction box supplied by the building
contractor. CTS prefers a PVC box to a steel one where the electrical code permits, and it
specifies PVC for outdoor pools.[^arch][^archg7] Its sample layouts name a Carlon E989R-UPC box
for the purpose.[^archg7]

The plate is mounted 18–36 in (45.7–91.4 cm) above the floor indoors, or above the deck
outdoors. It has to sit flush with the wall or be set back into it, and must not stand out from
the wall surface.[^arch] Conduit from the deck boxes enters the wall box, and the scoreboard
data runs on its own conduit to a small junction box at the display.[^archg7]

The Gen7 Serial system uses smaller boxes: each connect hub fits a 4 × 4 × 6 in PVC box, and CTS
specifies 1.5 in PVC conduit between every deck and wall box on the bus.[^hubsds]

A lockable aluminium [wall plate cover](cts-wall-plate-cover.md) protects the plate between
meets. It measures 16 × 16 × 2 in (40.6 × 40.6 × 5.1 cm), with a 13.5 × 11.5 in
(34.3 × 29.2 cm) opening for the plate, and it is made in a version for new plates and a
retrofit version for the older ABS plates.[^arch][^uwp]

## Connection to the timer

On a parallel-wired installation the console's cable plugs into the 50-pin sockets on the
plate. CTS's Gen7 Legacy manual connects the plate's near-end and far-end sockets to the timer's
rear panel with a [timer-to-wallplate cable](../timers/cts-timer-wallplate-cable.md), `CHI-xx-CPUA`, with a
further `CHI12-xx` cable for a 12-lane pool.[^f1034old] CTS sells the 25 ft version as
`CHI-25-CPUA`.[^shop] A US dealer also offers a 50 ft length, and a UK dealer sells a 3 m cable
for linking a Gen7 timer to a wall plate.[^dealercable]

CTS start systems connect in one of two ways. The Championship and Championship Elite starters
take a [CMP-WP cable](../timers/cts-cmp-wp-cable.md) from their wallplate connector to the plate's
start-system socket. One cable carries the start signal, the starter's audio for the lane
speakers, the speedlight signal and power for an external strobe.[^f875][^f1064] The Infinity Pro starter
uses a start jumper to the plate's start jacks instead.[^f1063] On a
Gen7 Serial plate, the timer uses cable `R-015-715-xx` to the timer node and the Elite starter
uses `R-015-706-xx` to the `WPI-S2` node.[^f1034][^f1064]

Video display data also passes through the plate. CTS's matrix-display manuals route the data
from the [DisplayLink](../../../software/displaylink.md) computer into a wall plate and on to
the display. Where several plates in a building each carry a display switch, only the one in
use is set to local.[^f853][^f850]

## Care and maintenance

CTS's cleaning sheets give the same wall plate routine in their 2011 and 2016 editions. After
each use the sockets are scrubbed with isopropyl alcohol on a soft toothbrush, and the touchpad
manual adds that no water rinse is needed because the alcohol evaporates.[^f897][^f1023][^f147]
Every connector except the fiber-optic scoreboard data connection then gets a coat of dielectric
grease. Lime-scale remover, which CTS allows on badly scaled banana plugs, must never be used on
a wall plate or on its 50-pin connectors.[^f897][^f1023] CTS's short video on the job adds pipe
cleaners for the individual contact points, working through the 50-pin connector, the start and
speaker jacks and the judging-terminal jacks.[^cleanvideo] Its 2024 maintenance checklist also
calls for the plate to be tested when each season begins.[^checklist]

The plate is also where most in-deck faults turn up. CTS's System 5 and System 6 manuals point
to the plate's connectors as the usual source of trouble in an in-deck system, and the Elite
starter's troubleshooting steps separate a short in the start-system cable from a short in the
plate's own wiring, which needs an authorized service provider.[^f506][^f870][^f1064]
