The QuickConnect deck plate is an in-deck connection system made by
[Colorado Time Systems](../../../vendors/colorado-time-systems.md) (CTS). Instead of putting the
lane's jacks in the deck, it set a sealed, military-style connector there and moved the jacks to
a connection hub mounted under the starting block, joined to the deck connector by one short
cable.[^f507][^product] The hub carried a socket for the speedlights on a relay judging platform,
which CTS's standard deck plate of the time did not have.[^arch][^f507] CTS documents describe it
from 2004, and its product page now lists it as discontinued.[^f507][^product]

## Role in the timing system

This section places QuickConnect among CTS's deck-cabling options. A deck plate is the fixed
point where a lane's equipment joins the facility's in-deck wiring; the
[deck cabling overview](index.md) covers that background. The System 6 edition of CTS's
architectural guidelines offered two in-deck styles, the regular plate and QuickConnect.[^arch]
CTS also sold an on-deck option, the speedlight cable harness, for pools without in-deck
wiring.[^f507] By 2015 its catalog listed
QuickConnect beside the [Titanium Deckplate](cts-titanium-deckplate.md), and CTS's current
architectural guidelines describe only the Titanium Deckplate.[^catalog15][^archg7]

## Design and construction

The system has three parts.[^f507][^product][^f881]

- The deck connector is a round flange screwed into the deck around a recessed circular socket.
  The socket is a military-style connector with gold-plated contacts, keyed by a flat side so the
  plug can only go in one way, and held by a locking collar.
- One short lead joins the deck connector to the hub.
- The connection hub is a rectangular plate fixed under the block's platform. It has six two-jack
  banana positions, for the touchpad, start, speaker and buttons A, B and C, with button A also
  labelled for the relay platform. It also has a round socket for the speedlights.[^f897]

The jack layout on the hub follows CTS's standard deck plate, which had the same six positions;
the speedlight socket is the addition.[^arch][^f897] CTS says that leaving only one sealed
connector at deck level reduces corrosion from pool water and chemicals. No independent test of
that claim has been found.[^arch][^product] The hubs, lane speakers and relay
platforms could be taken off the blocks and stored dry between sessions.[^product]

CTS's own documents are inconsistent about what to call the parts. Its cleaning sheet captions a
photo of the hub "Under-block Connection Hub", while the 2014 touchpad manual uses the same photo
captioned "Deck Plate (Quick Connect)".[^f897][^f147]

## Speedlights and the Colorado Block

CTS lists speedlight support first among the system's features.[^f507][^product] Its
architectural guidelines say the system was required for relay platforms with speedlights.[^arch]
The 2004 relay platform guide is less strict. It names the speedlight cable harness as another way
to connect speedlights, which suggests the requirement applied only to in-deck pools.[^f507] On a QuickConnect lane the platform's take-off
signal shares the button A jack, through a cable whose plug stacks with the button's, and a 6 ft
speedlight cable with a two-pin DIN plug goes into the hub's speedlight socket.[^f507] CTS's later
relay platform cable for these plates combines the two.[^f968]

The [Colorado Block](../starting-blocks/colorado-block.md) was built around the system. CTS
describes it as a [Paragon Aquatics](../../../vendors/paragon-aquatics.md) Track Start
Competitor starting block fitted with a CTS relay platform with speedlights, an under-block
speaker and the connection hub, and its catalogs credited QuickConnect with removing extra cables
from the deck.[^f507][^catalog11]

## Installation and mounting

This section covers what is specific to QuickConnect; deck boxes and conduit follow the general
CTS rules in the [deck cabling overview](index.md). The deck connector is sold with its run of
cable already attached, 120 ft as `DP-QC120` or 200 ft as `DP-QC200`.[^product] At a meet,
officials connect only the hub cable at each lane, rather than plugging every touchpad, button and
platform into the deck.[^arch] The hub stays on the block, and the lane equipment plugs into it
there.[^product]

## Connection to the timer

The deck connector's cable runs under the deck to the wall plate, where the timer connects, as on
CTS's other in-deck plates.[^arch] The hub cable may stay connected whether or not the timing
system is on. Before power is applied, every lane's plate must hold either a hub cable or a
terminator.[^f881] CTS's test meter checks a hub in the same way as a deck plate: plugged into the
touchpad jack with the timer on, it should read 4.5–5 V.[^f147]

## Care and maintenance

CTS's rule for the deck connector is that it is never left open. It must always hold one of
three things: the hub cable, a blue cover plug, or a terminator plug.[^f881]

- A terminator goes into each unused plate when only some lanes are in use. CTS's explanation is
  that current flowing between the contacts of an open connector causes electrolysis that damages
  them.[^f881]
- Otherwise the blue cover plug goes in. It keeps dirt out of the socket and shields the
  connector's rubber from sunlight, which dries it out. CTS's remedy for rubber that has dried is a
  2–3 minute soak in fresh water before connecting.[^f881]
- Before connecting, the plug comes out and the plate and socket are rinsed with fresh water,
  which also softens the plastic so the cable goes in more easily. After use the deck and plates
  are rinsed again and the cover plug refitted. CTS sells replacement cover and terminator
  plugs.[^f881]

The two CTS sources disagree on lubricant. The 2004 instructions call for a very small amount of
Molykote 44 Medium on each contact during heavy use. CTS's 2024 maintenance checklist instead
calls for Dow Corning #111 dielectric grease after every use and monthly, and a check of the
connector prongs for corrosion before each use.[^f881][^checklist] The hub's banana jacks are
cleaned like any CTS deck plate's, and its speedlight socket with alcohol and a soft
toothbrush.[^f897]

## Compared with the Titanium Deckplate

CTS's later [Titanium Deckplate](cts-titanium-deckplate.md) brings the jacks back into the deck.
It handles the speedlight on the plate itself, through a three-pin relay judging jack that carries
both the platform's signal and the speedlight.[^tdpsheet] CTS has not described the Titanium
Deckplate as QuickConnect's successor, and the two were sold side by side in 2015.[^catalog15]

## Part numbers and accessories

This section lists the parts CTS names.

- `DP-QC120` and `DP-QC200`, the deck connector with 120 ft or 200 ft of cable.[^product]
- `DP-4.5QC`, the 4.5 in connection hub.[^product]
- Blue deck plate plug and terminator plug, sold as replacements; CTS gives no part numbers.[^f881]
- Speedlight cable for under-block hubs, 6 ft with a male two-pin DIN plug.[^f507]
- `R-015-027`, the later relay platform cable for DP-series plates and hubs.[^f968]
- `F881`, the care instructions.[^f881]
