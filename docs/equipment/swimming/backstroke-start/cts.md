The Backstroke Start Device made by
[Colorado Time Systems](../../../vendors/colorado-time-systems.md) (CTS) is a
[backstroke start device](index.md): a mechanical foot wedge that gives a backstroke
swimmer a fixed, non-slip foothold on the wall at the start of a race. A swimmer in the
water pulls an angled wedge down the face of the wall or touchpad, locks it in place,
braces both feet against it, and starts from it. Once the swimmer leaves the wall the
wedge retracts automatically, up and clear of the water, so it does not obstruct turns
or finishes and is ready for the next swimmer.[^f1024][^product] It carries no wiring
and generates no timing signal.

CTS describes the device as compliant with the rules of World Aquatics, USA Swimming,
and US Masters Swimming; company materials variously add NFHS (US high school) or NCAA
(US college) to that list.[^product][^swpatent] The actual regulatory status differs by
body and is covered in the [backstroke start devices overview](index.md), along with
the history of the equipment and the foot-placement rule that governs its use.

## Design and components

The device is a self-contained mechanical unit that clamps or bolts across the top of
the wall above a lane. Its main parts are a locking lever, a manual adjustment knob, a
wedge, and a strap plate.[^f1024] The wedge is the foot rest: a bar 25.6 in (65 cm)
wide and 3.1 in (8 cm) deep, set at a 10° angle and finished with a non-slip surface
for foot placement.[^product]

The wedge hangs from straps wound on a spring-loaded tube inside the case. CTS's patent
describes the mechanism as a rotatable winding tube driven by a torsion spring: pulling
the wedge down unwinds the straps against the spring, a ratchet lock holds the wedge at
the set depth, and releasing the lock lets the spring wind the straps back in and lift
the wedge out of the water. The device senses the drop in load when the swimmer pushes
off and retracts on its own, without power or an operator.[^patent] The manual
adjustment knob lets an operator wind the wedge back by hand if it does not retract
fully during a race.[^f1024] The patent, "Start assist device for swimmers with
positioning and removal mechanism," was filed on 31 July 2013 by inventors Christian
Stockinger and Eric Solanyk, assigned to Everlast Climbing Industries (the CTS parent),
and granted as US 10,245,524 on 2 April 2019; the corresponding Australian patent,
2013382748, was granted on 9 November 2016, and the family also includes European,
Canadian, Chinese, and Brazilian patents.[^patent][^swpatent]

## Installation and mounting

The device can be attached to a starting platform with straps or bolted to the deck
with anchors. Strapped mounting is the default and suits a single or dual post block
with a setback of up to 36 in; the straps route behind the block's anchor posts,
beneath or behind the backstroke bars, and tension through a ratchet.[^f1024] Deck
mounting uses a plate fixed to the deck with two drop-in anchors, for which CTS gives a
drilling and anchor-setting procedure, and custom mounts can be fabricated for unusual
blocks.[^f1024]

A bracket matched to the pool's gutter sets how the device sits at the wall. CTS's
documents give two bracket series and their part numbers differ between editions, which
appears to reflect a change in the product line rather than one correct value. The 2016
user guide lists a long 9.25 in bracket (`143060-L`) for bull-nose, rimflow, and similar
gutters and a short 5.75 in bracket (`143060`) for trough, haunch, and recessed
gutters.[^f1024] The current datasheet lists retrofit brackets in a `143081` series
instead: a long 7.7 in bracket (`143081-CL`) for bull-nose and rimflow gutters and a
short 5.7 in bracket (`143081-C`) for trough, haunch, and recessed gutters.[^product] In
both cases CTS notes that the long bracket shortens the pool's measured length by
0.103 in, which matters where the touchpad already sets the official wall.[^f1024][^product]

Whatever the mounting, CTS requires that the device be centred between the block
handles, that it not pose a risk to swimmers, and that it fully retract clear of the
water and off the touchpad when not set.[^f1024]

## Operation

The device is made to be set by the swimmer, in the water, without an official. The
sequence is to pull and hold the locking lever, pull the wedge out to the chosen depth
on the wall, hold it there with the feet while releasing the lever so it locks, take the
ready position, and start on the signal; the wedge then retracts on its own and is ready
for the next swimmer.[^f1024] CTS advises that the first swimmer of a meet fully extend
and submerge the wedge once to wet the straps so they retract evenly afterward.[^f1024]

Some rule sets limit how high the wedge may sit relative to the water. To support this,
the straps and wedge carry red marking zones; where a governing body requires the top of
the wedge to be within 4 cm above or below the water line, the swimmer positions it so
the water line falls within the red areas.[^f1024]

## Effect on timing

The device does not connect to the timing system, but its motion can disturb the
touchpad. The manual warns that with the backstroke start device fitted, any reaction time
the touchpad reports should not be trusted: as the wedge retracts it can
bump the touchpad, which makes the timing system read the swimmer as still on the wall
and lengthens the apparent start reaction time.[^f1024] The recorded race time is taken
from the swimmer's finish touch and is unaffected; the caveat applies to the
start-reaction figure.

## Care and maintenance

CTS calls for periodic care to keep the mechanism working. The device should be rinsed
in fresh, not pool, water and not sprayed with high-pressure water. Discoloured
stainless-steel parts are cleaned and passivated with a mild abrasive pad and a
stainless cleaner, and the case and wedge are washed with fresh water and a mild
detergent. Before long storage the unit is removed, its wedge fully extended and locked
so it can dry in the extended position, then dried and allowed to retract slowly before
being stored in a clean, dry place.[^f1024]
