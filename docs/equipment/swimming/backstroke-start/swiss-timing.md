The OBL2 Pro Backstroke Ledge, made by
[Swiss Timing](../../../vendors/swiss-timing.md) and sold under its Omega brand, is a
[backstroke start device](index.md): a removable ledge that hooks over the top of an
Omega starting platform and gives a backstroke swimmer a non-slip foothold on the wall
at the start of a race. The swimmer rotates a spring-assisted mechanism to set the
footrest at one of five heights, and after the start an official lifts the whole unit
off the block. Swiss Timing states that the OBL2 Pro received FINA approval at the
federation's December 2014 convention in Doha and describes it as the second generation
of the device, used at the highest level of competition.[^datasheet] It is the ledge
model that has been evaluated in independent biomechanical research.[^study]

The device is matched to Swiss Timing's own starting blocks: the company lists it as
fully compatible with the OSB11 and OSB14 blocks and the OSB12 platform, and its
mounting assumes the slots of those blocks.[^datasheet][^manual] This contrasts with
strap-around designs such as the [FINIS wedge](finis.md), which fit a range of generic
blocks, and with the self-retracting [CTS device](cts.md); comparisons across the class
are covered in the [backstroke start devices overview](index.md).

## Design and components

The unit has a lower part that leans against the end wall of the pool and carries the
footrest, and an upper part that hooks the assembly over the starting platform. The
footrest is molded from polypropylene reinforced with 10% fiberglass and covered with
an anti-slip surface; the housings are ABS and the springs and screws are stainless
steel. The upper part holds a rotating adjustment mechanism, described by Swiss Timing
as patent pending, with a return spring that allows one-handed adjustment, including by
a swimmer in the water, and a handlebar for lifting the unit off after the
start.[^manual][^datasheet]

Numbered service parts include the footrest (`3454.110`), rims (`3454.111`), left and
right supports (`3454.120`, `3454.121`), protection membranes (`3454.114`, `3454.115`),
adjustment ring (`3454.119`), warning label (`3454.117`), protection cap (`3454.118`),
and safety clasp (`3454.116`), along with the straps, bars, spring, and locking
ring.[^manual]

In 2025 Swiss Timing was granted a US patent for a starting block with a backstroke
ledge built into the block itself, held on flexible straps with return springs and
stowed by an official through a positioning rod. That patent (US 12,465,833, priority
2021) describes an integrated successor design rather than the separate OBL2 Pro
unit.[^patent]

## Installation and mounting

The ledge installs on the platform top: the block's own footrest is set to position 3,
the fins of the ledge's supports drop into the top slots of the block, and the unit
slides forward to its stop, with the adjustment ring on the right as seen from behind
the block.[^manual]

Calibration ties the numbered scale to the water level. With the Z scale at 0, four
screws on the adjustment ring are loosened (maximum torque 3 Nm when retightening), the
straps are wound until the upper edge of the footrest sits at water level, and the ring
is fixed again. Swiss Timing notes that a pool's filtration system raises the water
level by about 2 cm, so the setting is made with the pump off, as it normally is during
racing. Both straps must run under the rims; routed over them, the unit can unhook
under load at the start. A safety clasp on the right rim keeps the strap positioned and
is checked before each competition.[^manual]

## Operation

Each swimmer can adjust the footrest before the start, including from the water: pulling
the bar sideways unlocks the mechanism, the footrest is rotated to the chosen number on
the scale, and the return spring recalls and locks the setting. The standard unit
offers five positions in 2 cm steps, from 4 cm below to 4 cm above water level, the
range the World Aquatics facilities rule allows; removing a locking screw frees further
2 cm steps, which the manual leaves to the governing rules of the
competition.[^manual][^fr]

The ledge does not retract. After the start, the assigned officials, such as the
inspectors of turns, immediately lift the units off the starting blocks, either by the
handlebar onto the deck or by folding the footrest behind the block's own footrest, so
the end walls and touchpads are clear for turns and finishes.[^manual][^datasheet] The
manual also warns against loading the ledge when it is rotated to a 90 degree
position.[^manual]

## Effect on the start

The OBL2 is the ledge used in the independent study of backstroke ledge starts: a
biomechanical comparison found starts with the ledge reached the 10 m mark about 0.13 s
sooner, with less variation and a longer head-entry distance, than starts from the bare
wall.[^study] The details of that study, and the history of the equipment class, are
covered in the [backstroke start devices overview](index.md).

## Care and maintenance

Swiss Timing advises rinsing the unit with fresh water after each use to remove
chlorinated water, drying it fully before storage, and periodically checking the
stainless steel screws and springs for oxidation, which can appear in aggressive
environments such as salt water and is cleaned with soapy water or a mild detergent.
The ABS parts are wiped with a damp cloth or chamois; hypochlorite bleach,
chloride-containing cleaners, high-proof alcohol, acetone, and other solvents are to be
avoided. The unit should not be stored under heavy objects, and after a severe impact
or drop it should be checked and not used in case of doubt.[^manual]
