A backstroke start device, also called a backstroke ledge or wedge, is an adjustable
foot rest fixed at the wall above a swimming lane that gives a backstroke swimmer a
non-slip surface to push from at the start of a race. The swimmer, already in the
water holding the starting grips, sets an angled ledge against the face of the wall
or touchpad at the chosen height, braces both feet against
it, and starts from it. Once the swimmer leaves the wall the ledge lifts or retracts
clear of the water so it does not obstruct turns and finishes. The device aids the
start; it is not part of the electronic [start system](../starter/index.md) that
signals the race or the [timing system](../timers/index.md) that records it.

## The backstroke start and the slip problem

In a backstroke start the swimmer braces both feet against a wet, near-vertical wall.
World Aquatics rules bar standing in or on the gutter, and equally bar hooking the toes
over its lip, so the feet press flat against the wall and can slip under load,
costing time and risking injury.[^warules][^swhistory] A ledge gives the feet a
slightly raised, angled, textured surface to push from, which reduces slipping.

## History

FINA (now World Aquatics) approved backstroke wedges for competition in 2013 and 2014,
and the first commercial ledges reached the market in September 2014. FINA asked meet
hosts to defer using them until a full launch during that autumn's World Cup series,
and the ledge was first used at the Olympic Games at Rio in 2016.[^swimswam-fina][^swhistory]

## Governing-body requirements

The World Aquatics backstroke rule was updated to cover the ledge start. SW 6.1 requires a
swimmer starting from a ledge to keep at least one toe of either foot against the end wall
or the touchpad face, and forbids curling the toes over the pad's top edge.[^warules]

The facilities rules set the ledge's dimensions. Under FR 2.1.11 of the World Aquatics
Facilities Rules 2021–2025, its adjustment range runs from 4 cm above the water surface to
4 cm below it. Length is at least 65 cm, and the ledge itself measures 8 cm high and 2 cm
wide, sloped at 10 degrees.[^fr] World Aquatics reduced the minimum length from 65 cm to 60 cm in a
regulations update effective with the 2025 events calendar.[^wa2025]

Regulatory acceptance varies by body. World Aquatics permits the ledge and sets the
foot-placement rule above.[^warules] For US high school swimming the NFHS made
backstroke ledges legal only by state-association adoption, effective in the 2025-26
school year: each state decides whether to allow them, a host that offers ledges must
provide identical ledges for every lane, individual swimmers may decline to use them,
and the pool must have at least 6 ft (1.84 m) of water depth from the starting wall
out to 16 ft 5 in (5 m).[^nfhs] Because a meet runs under one body's rules, whether a
ledge may be used depends on the sanctioning organization for that competition.

## Effect on the start

Independent study has found a measurable effect on start performance. A biomechanical
comparison using the [Omega OBL2 Pro Backstroke Ledge](swiss-timing.md) reported that
ledge starts reached the 10 m mark about 0.13 s sooner, with roughly 2.5% less
variation and about 0.14 m greater head-entry distance than starts without a
ledge.[^obl2]

## Products

This section catalogs the devices of three manufacturers, whose designs differ mainly
in how the ledge attaches and how it clears the water after the start:
[Swiss Timing](../../../vendors/swiss-timing.md) (Omega) makes the OBL2 Pro, which
mounts on the company's own starting blocks and is lifted off by an official;
[Colorado Time Systems](../../../vendors/colorado-time-systems.md) (CTS) makes a device
that retracts automatically; and [FINIS](../../../vendors/finis.md) makes a manual
strap-hung wedge.

| Product | Manufacturer | Attachment | Retraction | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Backstroke Start Device](cts.md) | Colorado Time Systems | Strap or deck anchors | Automatic, torsion-spring | Current |
| [Backstroke Start Wedge](finis.md) | FINIS | Straps to block posts, hand grips, or block edge | None (manual) | Current |
| [OBL2 Pro Backstroke Ledge](swiss-timing.md) | Swiss Timing (Omega) | Hooks over an Omega OSB platform top | None; removed by an official | Current |

Each product article covers its device in full, its parts, mounting, operation, and
care; this page is the shared overview they refer back to.
