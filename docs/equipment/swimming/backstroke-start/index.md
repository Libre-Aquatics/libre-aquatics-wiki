A backstroke start device, also called a backstroke ledge or wedge, is an adjustable
foot rest fixed at the wall above a swimming lane that gives a backstroke swimmer a
non-slip surface to push from at the start of a race. The swimmer, already in the
water holding the starting grips, sets an angled ledge against the face of the wall
or touchpad at the chosen height, braces both feet against
it, and starts from it. Once the swimmer leaves the wall the ledge lifts or retracts
clear of the water so it does not obstruct turns and finishes. The device aids the
start; it is not part of the electronic [start system](../starter/index.md) that
signals the race or the [timing system](../timers/index.md) that records it.

## Ledge or wedge

The two names describe the same device. The rulebooks call it a backstroke ledge: World
Aquatics, USA Swimming, the NCAA and the NFHS all use that term, and so does Swiss Timing
for its Omega product.[^fr][^usas][^ncaanews][^nfhs][^obl2ds] Wedge is the name used by
FINIS, which sells a Backstroke Start Wedge, and by Colorado Time Systems, whose user guide
calls the foot rest of its Backstroke Start Device the wedge; swimming press coverage uses
both words.[^finis][^f1024][^swhistory][^swimswam-fina]

## The backstroke start and the slip problem

In a backstroke start the swimmer braces both feet against a wet, near-vertical wall.
World Aquatics rules bar standing in or on the gutter, and equally bar hooking the toes
over its lip, so the feet press flat against the wall and can slip under load,
costing time and risking injury.[^warules][^swhistory] A ledge gives the feet a
slightly raised, angled, textured surface to push from, which reduces slipping.

World Aquatics sets the ledge's dimensions in its facilities rules (FR 2.1.11, repeated in
FR 2.2.11 for the Olympic Games and World Championships), and
USA Swimming, the NCAA and World Para Swimming use the same figures, with one difference
in the minimum length.[^fr][^usas][^ncaanews][^wps]

| Dimension | Value |
|---|---|
| Adjustment range | 4 cm above to 4 cm below the water level |
| Minimum length | 60 cm under World Aquatics since 2025 and under the NCAA; 65 cm in the 2021–2025 Facilities Rules, the 2026 USA Swimming rulebook and the 2026 World Para Swimming rules |
| Height | 8 cm |
| Width | 2 cm |
| Slope | 10° |
| Setting when installed | 0, set by the inspector of turns at the starting end |

World Aquatics cut the minimum length from 65 cm to 60 cm in a regulations update that
took effect with the 2025 events calendar, and the NCAA rule approved in July 2026 also
specifies 60 cm.[^wa2025][^ncaanews] The 2026 USA Swimming rulebook and the February 2026
World Para Swimming rules still print 65 cm.[^usas][^wps] The rule texts give the height,
width and slope without a drawing reference; the diagram that accompanies the World
Aquatics rule shows the 2 cm at the base of the profile and the 10° slope on the face the
feet push against, measured from the vertical.[^fr]

The setting is read at the top edge of the foot rest. Swiss Timing's manual sets position 0
with the upper edge of the foot rest at the water level, and Colorado Time Systems marks the
4 cm band on its straps and wedge for rules that require the top of the wedge to sit within
4 cm of the water line.[^obl2man][^f1024] Because the ledge is 8 cm high, even the highest
legal setting leaves its lower half below the surface. The drawing that accompanies the World
Aquatics rule lists positions up to 180 mm above the water; Swiss Timing's unit can reach
further positions in 2 cm steps once a locking screw is removed, but its manual leaves their
use to the competition rules, which allow only the 4 cm range.[^fr][^obl2man]

## History

FINA (now World Aquatics) approved backstroke wedges for competition in 2013 and 2014,
and the first commercial ledges reached the market in September 2014. FINA asked meet
hosts to defer using them until a full launch during that autumn's World Cup series,
and the ledge was first used at the Olympic Games at Rio in 2016.[^swimswam-fina][^swhistory]

Swiss Timing's ledge has gone through two generations. The company describes its OBL2 Pro
as the second generation of the device and states that it received FINA approval at the
federation's December 2014 convention in Doha; its datasheet is dated February 2015 and
revised in October 2015, and the user manual has run from version 1.0 to version 1.4,
issued in April 2026.[^obl2ds][^obl2man] The first-generation model is not named in the
Swiss Timing documents. The [OBL2 Pro](swiss-timing.md) article covers the device.

Colorado Time Systems filed its patent for a self-retracting start device in July 2013,
and FINIS introduced its strap-hung wedge in March 2018; both are described in their own
articles.[^ctspatent][^sw2018]

## Governing-body requirements

The World Aquatics backstroke rule was updated to cover the ledge start. SW 6.1 requires a
swimmer starting from a ledge to keep at least one toe of either foot against the end wall
or the touchpad face, and forbids curling the toes over the pad's top edge.[^warules] When a
ledge is in use, the inspector of turns at the starting end of each lane puts it in place,
sets it at 0, and takes it out again.[^warules]

Regulatory acceptance varies by body. USA Swimming's rule adopts the World Aquatics
specification, though its 2026 rulebook still sets a 65 cm minimum length, and it uses the
same foot-placement rule. Its rulebook bars ledges in water shallower than 4 ft (1.22 m)
at the starting end, and where a meet uses them, every lane must have an identical ledge.
Teaching racing starts from a ledge needs 6 ft (1.84 m) of water from 1 m to 5 m out
from the wall, and until a coach certifies a swimmer as proficient in racing starts, that
swimmer practises starts from in the water, without the ledge.[^usas]

The NCAA rulebook for 2025–26 and 2026–27 already carries the World Aquatics
foot-placement rule for starts from a ledge.[^ncaarules] In July 2026 the NCAA announced
an adjustable-ledge rule, approved for Division I from the 2026–27 academic year and by
the Playing Rules Oversight Panel for Divisions II and III, which sets the ±4 cm range,
the 60 cm minimum length and the 8 cm by 2 cm, 10° profile.[^ncaanews]

For US high school swimming the NFHS made backstroke ledges legal only by
state-association adoption, effective in the 2025-26 school year: each state decides
whether to allow them, a host that offers ledges must provide identical ledges for every
lane, individual swimmers may decline to use them, and the pool must have at least 6 ft
(1.84 m) of water depth from the starting wall out to 16 ft 5 in (5 m).[^nfhs] Because a
meet runs under one body's rules, whether a ledge may be used depends on the sanctioning
organization for that competition.

World Para Swimming permits the ledge on the same specification, with the same
inspector-of-turns duty and the same foot-placement rule, so Para backstroke races can be
started from a ledge where the competition provides one.[^wps]

## Effect on the start

Independent study has found a measurable effect on start performance. A biomechanical
comparison using the [Omega OBL2 Pro Backstroke Ledge](swiss-timing.md) reported that
ledge starts reached the 10 m mark about 0.13 s sooner, with roughly 2.5% less
variation and about 0.14 m greater head-entry distance than starts without a
ledge.[^obl2]

## Products

This section catalogs the devices of three manufacturers, whose designs differ mainly
in how the ledge attaches and how it clears the water after the start:
[Swiss Timing](../../../vendors/swiss-timing.md) (Omega) makes the OBL2 Pro, which
mounts on the company's own starting blocks and is lifted off by an official;
[Colorado Time Systems](../../../vendors/colorado-time-systems.md) (CTS) makes a device
that retracts automatically; and [FINIS](../../../vendors/finis.md) makes a manual
strap-hung wedge.
