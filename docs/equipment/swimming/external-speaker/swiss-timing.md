Swiss Timing Ltd, of Corgémont, Switzerland, is the Swatch Group timing company
whose equipment appears under the Omega brand at major international meets. It
places the lane speaker in the starting block rather than on an under-block horn
as the United States manufacturers do: a fixed installation fits an internal
loudspeaker inside the block, and a mobile installation uses a set of external
loudspeakers positioned near each block.[^osb11][^stconcept] Both are driven by
the [StartTime V](../starter/starttime-v.md) electronic starting device. Swiss
Timing does not disclose who makes the loudspeakers. Shared background on lane
speakers is in the [external-speakers overview](index.md).

## In-block loudspeakers

For fixed wiring, an internal loudspeaker, article 3399.600, is an option
fitted inside the OSB11 and OSB14 starting blocks. Two banana plugs make the
speaker connection, and the installer reaches the cabling through a hatch in
the block's rear face.[^osb11][^osb14] The timing system's other lane devices
connect into a lane deck plate installed under the centre of each block, which
the datasheets say protects the cables, the connections, and the
speaker.[^osb11][^osb14]

The division between in-block and mobile speakers is less clean than the datasheets
imply. Swiss Timing's Australian distributor lists a second in-block loudspeaker,
article 3399.610, built for mobile wiring and terminating in a Tu4pM connector rather
than banana plugs, so a portable installation can also put the speaker inside the
block.[^hts] No Swiss Timing document cited here names that article, and the OSB11,
OSB12 and OSB14 user manuals mention no loudspeaker at all, so the distributor listing
is the only record of it.[^hts]

Swiss Timing's installation manual calls these units lane speakers and prefers
them for an in-deck installation. The speaker sits inside the block and the
wiring runs beneath the deck, so the start area stays clear of clutter in line
with FINA (now World Aquatics) facilities recommendations, and the blocks can
be removed between competitions without disturbing the permanent wiring.[^stconcept]

## Mobile loudspeakers

Where the cabling is mobile rather than in-deck, Swiss Timing supplies external
loudspeakers placed close to each block. They are sold as sets of three to ten
speakers with cable harnesses, and the final digit pair of the article number gives
the cable length between two loudspeakers, 3 m or 6 m.[^stvds][^stvman]

| Article | Set |
|---|---|
| 3399.933.06 | 3 mobile loudspeakers, 6 m cables |
| 3399.934.03 / .06 | 4 mobile loudspeakers, 3 m or 6 m cables |
| 3399.935.03 / .06 | 5 mobile loudspeakers, 3 m or 6 m cables |
| 3399.936.03 | 6 mobile loudspeakers, 3 m cables |
| 3399.938.03 | 8 mobile loudspeakers, 3 m cables |
| 3399.940.03 | 10 mobile loudspeakers, 3 m cables |

The installation manual shows the external loudspeaker as a unit of about
180 × 215 × 192 mm, and lists a multipurpose tripod (article 9051.6339,
rated to 8 kg) among the start-system options.[^stconcept][^stvds]

Swiss Timing's distributors publish the same sets independently. The Australian and
New Zealand distributor lists the full run from 3399.933.06 to 3399.940.03 with the
9051.6339 tripod beside it, and describes the accessories as belonging to the
StartTime IV. That places this generation of harness with that device rather than
with the StartTime V page they now sit on.[^hts] The Vienna dealer AVK Group
catalogues a current external loudspeaker as article 3399.930, marking it FINA
approved and intended for mobile cabling around the starting block.[^avk] The two
dealers disagree on one number: the optical start signal for hearing-impaired
swimmers is 3399.951 in the Australian listing and 3481.951 in the Austrian
one.[^hts][^avk] The older article numbers this page cites from the 2007 and 2015
documents, including the external loudspeaker 2850.712 and the 3399.902 to 3399.910
harnesses, appear in no dealer catalogue and rest on Swiss Timing's own
documentation.

## Governing-body requirements

World Aquatics requires a speaker at every block in a competition installation, so
that every swimmer hears the spoken commands and the start signal at the same volume
and the same instant.[^wafr] That per-block rule is
specific to World Aquatics, which is the market this design addresses. USA Swimming
asks only that the sound reach every lane equitably, and accepts speakers ranged along
the sides of the course or behind the blocks as alternatives; the NCAA and NFHS
rulebooks set no placement requirement at all.[^usas103][^nfhs] The French swimming
federation, documenting an Omega installation for its own officials, takes the stricter
reading: one loudspeaker per lane is the compliant arrangement, and halving that to one
between every two lanes is the cheaper option that falls short of it.[^ffn] The
[start-systems overview](../starter/index.md) covers why a single poolside horn is
not sufficient.

## Connections

The loudspeakers are driven by the [StartTime V](../starter/starttime-v.md)
starting device, which is covered in its own article. The amplifier's load window
sets the lane wiring. The recommended load is between 3.2 and 16 ohms, and any
speaker combination inside that range is acceptable, so an installation combines
the per-lane loudspeakers to stay within that range. Speaker polarity must be
kept consistent across the installation or sound quality degrades.[^stvman]

## Specifications

Swiss Timing publishes article numbers, set sizes, and system-level electrical
limits, but no per-speaker wattage, impedance, or frequency figures.

| | |
|---|---|
| In-block loudspeaker | Article 3399.600; fixed wiring; 2 banana plugs; fits OSB11 and OSB14 |
| Mobile loudspeaker sets | 3 to 10 speakers, 3 m or 6 m harness cables (3399.933.06 to 3399.940.03) |
| Amplifier load | 3.2 to 16 Ω recommended; 2 Ω possible with distortion |
| Speaker cable runs | Up to 30 m at 0.75 mm²; up to 50 m at 1 or 1.5 mm² |
