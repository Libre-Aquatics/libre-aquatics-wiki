An external speaker, in competitive swimming, is any loudspeaker driven by a
[start system](../starter/index.md) beyond the speaker built into the start
system itself. The most numerous type is the lane speaker, a small loudspeaker
mounted under or inside each starting block that reproduces the starter's
commands, the start tone, and any false-start recall tone for the swimmer in
that lane. A start system drives one speaker at each block from a common
circuit so that every athlete hears the commands and the starting signal at
the same instant. The reason for a speaker at each block rather than a single
poolside horn, and the governing-body rules that require it, are covered in
the [start-systems overview](../starter/index.md); this section describes the
speaker hardware itself and catalogs the models sold by each manufacturer.

Lane speakers are also called under-block speakers, after their most common
mounting position on the foot or front face of the block, or block speakers.
Some manufacturers mount the speaker inside the block instead.

## Types of external speaker

Start systems drive several kinds of speaker, usually on separate circuits:

- Under-block lane speaker: a small horn behind each block, one per lane,
  wired through a multi-lane cable harness. A deck-wired start system
  typically drives up to ten or twelve of them on one circuit.
- In-block lane speaker: the same role filled by a loudspeaker fitted inside
  the starting block, connected through the block's cable access.
- Auxiliary or backstroke speaker: a single larger horn, mounted on a
  backstroke flagpole or beside the start system, that carries the same audio
  to officials, spectators, and backstroke starters in the water. It is
  usually driven from a separate output with its own volume control.
- Far-end speaker: a second large speaker placed at the far side of the pool,
  or wherever the start system's own speaker does not reach, on a long cable.
- Underwater speaker: a submerged speaker that broadcasts an underwater recall
  tone and lets a coach address swimmers mid-pool. These are documented with
  the start systems that drive them.

Most start systems also contain a built-in speaker of their own, typically a
40 watt unit. Whether a given model has one, and its rating, is documented in
that start system's article under [start systems](../starter/index.md);
external speakers either replace the built-in speaker at the blocks or extend
it across the pool.

## How an external speaker works

Lane speakers are compact horn loudspeakers built for a wet, corrosive
environment: the horn body and mounting base are moulded from impact- and
UV-resistant plastic, the fasteners are stainless steel, and the driver
diaphragm is moisture-resistant, so the unit tolerates splash, humidity, and
pool chemistry.[^fourjay306] Installers point the horn slightly downward so
that water drains out of the bell rather than collecting inside it.[^dak]
Mounting is to the block itself, to a standard electrical box behind it, or
inside the block on designs built for it.[^fourjay306][^osb11]

Electrically, a start system must drive many speakers from one amplifier, and
manufacturers solve this in one of two ways. One is to use high-impedance
speakers, rated at 45 ohms rather than a loudspeaker's usual 8 ohms, so that a
dozen of them wired in parallel on one circuit still present a load an
ordinary start-system amplifier can drive.[^fourjay306][^f875] The other is to
specify a permissible load window for the amplifier and let the installer
combine speakers to land inside it; Swiss Timing's StartTime amplifier, for
example, accepts any speaker combination presenting between 3.2 and
16 ohms.[^stvman]

## Products

Five manufacturers sell, or have sold, lane speakers for swimming start
systems. Each has its own article:

| Article | Manufacturer | Lane speaker | OEM horn | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Colorado Time Systems](cts.md) | Colorado Time Systems | `SP-6/45` (6 W, 45 Ω) | Fourjay 306-45 | Current |
| [Daktronics](daktronics.md) | Daktronics | `306-45-54` (`DS-1059`) | Fourjay 306-45 | Current |
| [International Sports Timing](ist.md) | International Sports Timing | `SPEAKER6W` (6 W), `SPKRBLOCK` system | Not disclosed | Not in current catalog |
| [Superior Swim Timing](sst.md) | Superior Swim Timing | SST Lane Speaker | Not disclosed | Current |
| [Swiss Timing](swiss-timing.md) | Swiss Timing | In-block loudspeaker (3399.600); mobile sets | Not disclosed | Current |

Colorado Time Systems and Daktronics sell the same under-block horn under
different part numbers: the 306 Series reflex horn made by Fourjay Industries
of Dayton, Ohio, a general-purpose paging horn that electronics retailers also
stock for public-address use.[^fourjay306][^f875][^dak][^pe306]

International Sports Timing sold an under-block speaker system as an accessory
to its [SWIMSTART Electronic Start](../starter/swimstart.md); the product no
longer appears in the company's current catalog.[^ist]

Superior Swim Timing sells a lane speaker and matching multi-lane harnesses
for its [Atlantis Swimming Starter System](../starter/atlantis.md), without
publishing electrical ratings or an OEM.[^sst]

Swiss Timing, whose equipment times major international meets under the Omega
brand, fits an internal loudspeaker inside its OSB starting blocks for fixed
installations and supplies sets of mobile loudspeakers, positioned near each
block, for mobile ones; both are driven by its
[StartTime V](../starter/starttime-v.md) starting device.[^osb11][^stconcept]
