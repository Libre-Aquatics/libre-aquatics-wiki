The OSB12 is a swimming starting platform made by [Swiss Timing](../../../vendors/swiss-timing.md)
and sold under the Omega name. It is a platform with an adjustable inclined footrest, sold
without a block of its own, that bolts onto an existing starting block so that a pool can offer
the kick start without replacing its blocks.[^manual][^datasheet] Like the
[OSB11](osb11.md), it is built either as a standard platform or with a relay break detection
(RBD) contact for judging relay take-offs.[^manual] The same platform, part `3454.041`, is the
top of the [OSB14](osb14.md).[^osb14m]

## Role in the timing system

This section places the platform in a Swiss Timing installation; starting blocks in general are
covered in the [starting blocks overview](index.md).

The RBD version of the OSB12 gives an existing block the same relay contact as the
[OSB11](osb11.md#role-in-the-timing-system); the standard version has no electrical
function.[^manual][^datasheet] Because the platform goes onto someone else's block, the manual
says nothing about deck plates or in-block loudspeakers. Its only connection detail is the RBD
lead, a cable of about 140 cm with a two-pole plug.[^manual]

Colorado Time Systems makes relay judging platforms to fit Omega footrest blocks. Its
RJPLD-2016 straps onto OSB11 and OSB12 blocks, its straps threaded under the footrest. A
separate RJPLD-OSB12 hooks over the front edge of an OSB12 and is bolted through two holes
drilled at the rear.[^f955][^f976] Both are described on the
[Colorado Time Systems RJP](../relay-judging/cts.md) page.

## History

This section covers the platform's place in the Omega line.

The OSB12 manual was first issued in November 2009, two months before the first edition of the
OSB11 manual in January 2010.[^manual][^osb11m] Both followed FINA's April 2008 approval of
starting platforms with an adjustable back plate.[^fina2008] The OSB12 has carried the same
marketing text as the OSB11: Swiss Timing calls the footrest a patented feature, says the rear
knee drives the start at a 90° angle, and says tests with top swimmers produced faster races
than a conventional block.[^manual][^datasheet] These are Swiss Timing's claims, and its
documents give no patent number.

In 2014 Swiss Timing put the OSB12 platform on a new, narrower base to make the OSB14.[^osb14m]
Distributors sell the OSB12 as the option for a pool whose blocks only need a new top. Its
Scandinavian and Benelux distributors describe it that way, and say the RBD version meets FINA's
competition requirements.[^swimprod][^varioplay] The Swedish distributor lists one such
retrofit, an OSB12 top with RBD fitted to an existing block at the Aq-Va-Kul pool in
Malmö.[^swimsystems]

## Design and construction

This section describes the platform and its layers.

The platform is 740 mm long and 520 mm wide, 38 mm thick, and 165 mm high at the
footrest.[^manual] Its footrest sits at the back and can be moved over 200 mm in five steps, or
taken off with ordinary tools.[^datasheet][^matus26] The datasheet gives fibreglass as the
material and lists an anti-slip top.[^datasheet]

The platform sits on intermediate layers. On the RBD version these are the RBD assembly, part
`3454.600`, and an intermediate plate, part `3393.024`. On the standard version a second plain
plate, part `3454.019`, takes the place of the RBD.[^manual] The RBD assembly is the one used in
the OSB11 and OSB14.[^osb11m][^osb14m] Swiss Timing says a standard platform can be converted to
RBD later.[^datasheet]

Unlike the OSB11 and OSB14, the OSB12 has no fixed slope of its own: it follows the top of the
block it is bolted to. Swiss Timing's drawing marks FINA's maximum of 10°.[^manual] A 2026
study of an OSB12 at the University of Prešov describes it as inclined at 5° in its methods but
at about 10° in its discussion.[^matus26]

The manual gives the colours as a white platform, RAL 9010, with a non-slip surface in dark
blue. The blue changed from RAL 5002 to RAL 5017 in 2019.[^manual] The datasheet makes RAL 5017
the standard and offers red, ivory, black and grey.[^datasheet] The
[OBL2 Pro Backstroke Ledge](../backstroke-start/swiss-timing.md) is made to fit the
OSB12.[^obl2]

## Relay break detection

This section covers the RBD contact.

The RBD assembly, its 13–18 kg setting and the buzzer-and-dynamometer adjustment are the same as
on the OSB11, described in the [OSB11 article](osb11.md#relay-break-detection).[^manual][^osb11m]
On the OSB12 the platform has to be lifted off before the adjusting screws can be reached.[^manual]
The OSB12 manual's version history records a correction to the force figures in April 2014,
three years after the OSB11 manual logged its own change to the action force.[^manual][^osb11m] The datasheet rates the contact at
100 mA and 12 V at most.[^datasheet]

## Mounting on an existing block

This section covers fitting the platform to a block.

Swiss Timing says the OSB12 can be adapted to almost any kind of existing block.[^datasheet]
Its drawing shows the platform on a block of concrete, steel or plastic, held by six M6 stainless
screws in two rows 200 mm apart.[^manual][^concept] A drilling gauge, part `3454.018`, marks the
holes. In concrete they are drilled at 8 mm to a depth of 60 mm and take M6 anchor pegs. The
drawing also shows a through-bolted version with nuts and washers under the top.[^manual] The
same drawing places the block behind an [OCP5 touchpad](../touchpad/ocp5.md) on the
wall.[^manual]

The finished block must still meet the World Aquatics limits set out in the
[starting blocks overview](index.md#governing-body-requirements). The OSB12's 740 × 520 mm top
meets the size rule on any block, but the height and slope depend on the block
underneath.[^manual]

## Care and maintenance

This section summarizes the OSB12's service schedule.

The OSB12 manual asks for more frequent attention than the block manuals. The platform is to be
cleaned every month with soft water and any oxidation deposits removed. The cable connections
are checked every six months, and the fixing of the platform and footrest once a
year.[^manual] The rest of its maintenance chapter is the stainless-steel cleaning text shared by
all the OSB blocks, summarized in the [OSB11 article](osb11.md#care-and-maintenance).[^manual][^maint]

To take the footrest off, the six screws on the platform are removed, the footrest is set to
position 3, and the four side screws are undone.[^manual]

## Research on starts from the platform

This section summarizes independent studies that used the OSB12; the kick start technique itself
is covered in the [starting blocks overview](index.md#the-kick-start).

Researchers at the University of Prešov in Slovakia have used OSB12 platforms in a series of
kick start studies. In a 2021 study of 20 young female swimmers, starts were quicker to 2 m and
5 m when the dominant leg was at the front edge. Most swimmers did best with the footrest at
positions 3 or 4 and a neutral or rear-weighted stance.[^matus21] A 2024 study of 15 swimmers
found a narrow foot stance faster to 5 m than a wide one. It suggested marking the centre of the
platform and footrest to help swimmers place their feet.[^matus24] A 2026 study compared the
OSB12 with a prototype training block and reported a block time of about 0.78 s for starts from
the OSB12.[^matus26]

## Compared with the OSB11 and OSB14

This section sets out how the platform relates to the two full blocks.

The OSB11 has its own platform, part `3454.012`, built into a 570 mm wide body with a fixed 9°
slope. The OSB14 carries the OSB12 platform itself on a base with a 200 × 320 mm
footprint.[^osb11m][^osb14m] The OSB12 has neither body nor base. It weighs 21.2 kg with RBD,
against 47 kg for the OSB11 and 29 kg for the OSB14 in their manuals.[^manual][^osb11m][^osb14m]
All three share the RBD assembly, the 13–18 kg setting and the 200 mm footrest
travel.[^manual][^osb11m][^osb14m]

## Specifications

This section lists the figures given by Swiss Timing; where the manual and the datasheet differ,
both are shown.

| Specification | Value |
|---|---|
| Platform | 740 × 520 × 38 mm; 165 mm high at the footrest[^manual] |
| Footrest | Adjustable over 200 mm in 5 steps[^datasheet][^matus26] |
| Slope | Set by the block it is fitted to; 10° maximum (FINA)[^manual] |
| Weight | 21.2 kg (RBD) and 19.5 kg (standard) in the manual; 22 kg in the datasheet[^manual][^datasheet] |
| Material | Fibreglass[^datasheet] |
| Colours | Platform white RAL 9010, blue non-slip surface RAL 5017 since 2019 (RAL 5002 before); red, ivory, black, grey optional[^manual][^datasheet] |
| RBD contact | Normally open, closes at the start; 100 mA, 12 V maximum[^manual][^datasheet] |
| RBD actuation force | 13–18 kg[^manual] |
| RBD connection | Cable with a two-pole plug, about 140 cm (manual); cable with two banana plugs (datasheet)[^manual][^datasheet] |
| Fixing | Six M6 × 20 stainless screws[^manual] |
| Certification | FINA approved; RoHS compliant (Swiss Timing)[^datasheet] |

The manual's table gives the contact, force and connection figures for the RBD version only.
The Swimming Concept drawing gives the platform width as 518 mm.[^manual][^concept]
