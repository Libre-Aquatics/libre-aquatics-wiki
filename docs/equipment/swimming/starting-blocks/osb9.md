The OSB9 is a discontinued swimming starting block made by
[Swiss Timing](../../../vendors/swiss-timing.md) and sold under the Omega name. It was a stainless
steel block with a flat 60 × 50 cm top and no footrest, with a built-in contact that signalled
the moment the swimmer left the block.[^omega2008][^avk] Swiss Timing replaced it with the footrest blocks: researchers treat the
[OSB11](osb11.md) as its successor, and Swiss Timing presents the [OSB14](osb14.md) as the
direct replacement, built to fit the same deck fixings.[^kibele][^osb14ds] No manual for the OSB9
has been found. Its description comes from an archived 2008 Omega product page, and most of its
figures from a single dealer listing.

## Role in the timing system

This section covers what the block did electrically; starting blocks in general are covered in
the [starting blocks overview](index.md).

Omega's 2008 product page describes the OSB9 as a block with a built-in detector that sends an
impulse when the swimmer leaves it. At relays the [ARES 21](../timers/ares-21.md) timer used that
impulse to record the gap between the outgoing swimmer's departure and the incoming swimmer's
touch, as a negative or positive figure.[^omega2008] A dealer listing adds that the contact is
electro-mechanical and operated by the horizontal force of the swimmer's push. It says the
platform rides on four rubber shock absorbers that let it move about 1.5 mm under a full
load.[^avk] This is the force-operated principle Omega introduced with the
[OSB6](../relay-judging/osb6.md) for relay take-off judging, and the one the later blocks keep in
their relay break detection assembly.[^osb6comm][^osb14m] Swiss Timing's 2001 note on the method
explains the signed result: a negative relay gap means the next swimmer left before the
touch.[^osb6comm]

## History

This section covers the block's place in the Omega line.

No source found gives the OSB9's introduction date. Researchers at the University of Kassel
describe it as the old block and the OSB11, which followed FINA's April 2008 approval of an
adjustable back plate, as its replacement, with a new foot plate
and a platform that is slightly longer and steeper.[^kibele][^fina2008] They cite comparative studies that
found starting advantages for the OSB11 over it. One such study, by the same group in 2010,
compared the OSB11 with a "traditional" block of 50 × 50 cm sloped at 5°, but its abstract does
not name that block's model.[^biel] Omega gives the OSB9's top as 60 × 50 cm, so the
traditional block in that study was probably not an OSB9, or was measured differently.[^omega2008]

Swiss Timing's product site still listed the OSB9 in January 2008, a few months before FINA's
rule change, with a datasheet that has not survived.[^omega2008][^fina2008]

By October 2015 the OSB9 was out of production. Swiss Timing's OSB14 datasheet of that date
presents the OSB14 as its replacement and says the two share a similar mounting system, so that
an OSB9 can be swapped for the newer block.[^osb14ds] The dealer listing marks the OSB9 as no
longer supplied.[^avk]

## Design and construction

This section describes the block as Omega and the dealer listing give it.

Omega describes the block as stainless steel with a 60 × 50 cm top.[^omega2008] The dealer
listing agrees on the platform size. It gives the colours as white RAL 9010 with a dark blue
RAL 5002 non-slip surface, on a white base of 218 × 322 mm. The listing gives the height as 403/495 mm without saying what the
two figures measure, and the weight as 27 kg.[^avk] The platform sits on four rubber shock
absorbers, which allow about 1.5 mm of vertical travel at a load of about 100 kg and about
0.5 mm of horizontal adjustment.[^avk] The contact trips at 4–9 kg and is rated at 100 mA and
12 V, and the block connects through a cable of about 50 cm with a two-pole plug.[^avk]

The listing names the model "Sirocco" and opens its description with a different model
designation before switching to the OSB9, so parts of the text may describe an earlier
block.[^avk] Sirocco is the name of a Swiss Timing wind gauge for athletics, which suggests the
model field was carried over from an unrelated product.[^sirocco]

## Mounting

This section covers how the block was fixed to the deck.

Swiss Timing's instructions for anchoring a starting block cover the OSB9, OSB11 and OSB14 with
one procedure and list a drilling gauge for each; the OSB9's is part `2659.078`.[^drill] The
holes are drilled in three stages to 16 mm and to a depth of 90–95 mm, cleaned, and filled
about two-thirds with a two-part resin. An M8 threaded anchor, part `9039.8582`, is then turned
into each hole, and the resin is left to cure before the block is fitted. The sheet's table
gives curing times from 90 minutes near freezing to 23 minutes at 31–35 °C.[^drill]

The OSB9 base footprint of 218 × 322 mm is close to the OSB14's 200 × 320 mm, which fits Swiss
Timing's statement that the OSB14 can take the place of an OSB9 on the same fixings.[^avk][^osb14ds]

## Documentation status

This section records what is known about the sources for this block.

Swiss Timing's surviving documents name the OSB9 as the block the OSB14 replaced and as the
owner of drilling gauge `2659.078`.[^osb14ds][^drill] Omega's 2008 product page survives in a
web archive with its description, but not the datasheet it linked to.[^omega2008] Archived copies
of Swiss Timing's later datasheet and manual folders hold OSB11, OSB12 and OSB14 documents but
nothing for the OSB9. Apart from the material, top size and relay function given by Omega, the
figures in this article rest on one distributor's listing, which mixes model names, and should be
treated as provisional.[^avk]
