A starting block, or starting platform, is the raised platform at the end of each lane that
swimmers dive from when a race starts and at relay take-overs. Competition blocks are
anchored to the deck or the bulkhead and many carry timing equipment: a contact that signals the
moment a swimmer leaves, a relay take-off platform, a lane speaker, or a connection
hub.[^wa-fr][^usas][^garcia]

## History and development

This section covers the change from flat blocks to blocks with a back plate.

Before the back plate, a competition block had a flat, gently sloped top. Swimmers used either
the grab start, feet side by side at the front edge, or the track start, with one foot set back on
the same surface.[^yang][^welcher] Writing in 1986, Richard J. LaRue said the track start had been
allowed in competition since 1980 but was held back by the rules on platform design.[^larue] In 1984 Swimming World reported a US swimmer who had dropped
it because it was not accepted at international meets.[^sw1984] The 1985–86 high school rules
required the block top to be flat, so that the back of it could not be raised to help track
starters.[^sw1985]

The idea of a rear foot support is older than the rule that allowed it. In the same 1986
editorial, LaRue cited his own 1980 study, which found faster starts when swimmers had a support
for the back foot. He backed a proposal before the NCAA rules committee for 1985–86 to allow
permanently mounted, adjustable rear foot blocks, available to every swimmer.[^larue] In 2002 Colorado Time Systems and Paragon Aquatics
launched the [Colorado Block](colorado-block.md), a Paragon track start platform fitted with a
CTS relay judging platform.[^sw2002]

## Omega and the 2008 rule

This section covers the rule that allowed a back plate and the first block built for it.

On 7 April 2008 FINA's Extraordinary Congress approved a starting platform that may carry an
adjustable setting back plate, an inclined plate at the rear for the back foot, to apply after the
Beijing Olympics.[^fina2008] The rule is still part of the World Aquatics facilities
rules.[^wa-fr] Omega had announced a block with an adjustable panel for the back foot, the
block sold as the [OSB11](osb11.md), in February 2008. Swimming World reported that FINA had approved it but that it
was not made available until the FINA World Cup stop in Stockholm, after the Games.[^sw2009jan]

The block's first championship is disputed. In March 2009 the FINA Bureau put off introducing
the Omega block, according to a Swimming World report on its rulings for that year's World
Championships.[^sw2009may] A 2013 study of 26 international championships nonetheless dates OSB11
use from those championships, held in Rome in 2009.[^garcia] By 2012 the block was in use at the
international meets.[^garcia][^murrell]

US rules also allow back plates. The NCAA permits track-style blocks with wedges, and USA Swimming
and the NFHS set rules for how a back plate or wedge may be used at relay
take-overs.[^ncaa][^usas][^nfhs] US makers now sell blocks with a wedge. S.R. Smith's
[Velocity](velocity.md), advertised from late 2015, has a wedge that folds away under the footboard when
it is not wanted.[^velocity] Swimming World quoted a coach in 2013 who credited the wedge with
changing how starts are taught.[^sw2013]

## How a starting block works

This section covers the parts of a block that matter to timing.

A block has a base fixed to the deck, a top with a non-slip surface, handgrips at the front or
sides for the forward start, and grips for the backstroke start.[^wa-fr] Where it carries timing
equipment, a contact between the top and the base senses the swimmer's feet. The contact is
closed while the swimmer stands on the block and changes state as the feet leave, so the timer
can record block time, the interval from the start signal to the moment the feet leave.[^garcia]

At relay take-overs the timer sets that signal against the moment the previous swimmer touches
the [touchpad](../touchpad/index.md). Omega's blocks have used a force-operated contact for this
since the [OSB6](../relay-judging/osb6.md) of 1984, which responds to the push of the swimmer's
feet rather than to the swimmer's position. Swiss Timing and FINA agreed a 0.03 s allowance to
cover the small delay between the force falling away and the toes leaving the block.[^osb6comm]
The [relay take-off platforms overview](../relay-judging/index.md) covers relay judging in full,
including add-on platforms for blocks that have no built-in contact.

How the timer treats the block's signal depends on the system. On a Colorado Time Systems Gen7
timer connected to older wiring, a relay platform shares an input with a lane's first pushbutton
and cannot be told apart from it. The serial version of the timer keeps the two separate. The
same timer takes start reaction times from the relay platform for forward starts and from the
touchpad for backstroke starts, and CTS warns that a backstroke start device can bump the pad as
it retracts and spoil the reading.[^gen7]

World Aquatics allows a lane read-out board to be fitted under the block, provided it does not
flash and its figures stay still during a backstroke start.[^wa-fr] Each block must carry its
lane number on all four sides.[^wa-fr]

In a pool wired in the deck, the block also covers a connection point for the lane. Swiss
Timing blocks stand over a [deck plate](../deck-cabling/swiss-timing-deck-plate.md), while
Colorado Time Systems uses a [QuickConnect](../deck-cabling/cts-quickconnect-deck-plate.md)
plate and hub under the block.[^osb14ds][^cts-qc]

## The kick start

This section covers the start technique that the back plate made possible.

With the back plate, the rear foot pushes against an inclined surface rather than the flat top.
Swimmers and researchers call this the kick start.[^yang] A 2018 review of the research on the
new blocks found the kick start ahead of both the grab start and the track start. It credits a
wider base of support, a shorter time on the block and a higher take-off speed. It recommends a
neutral or slightly rear-weighted stance and notes that the evidence on the best technique is
not settled.[^yang] Most of the published studies were done on Omega blocks, and the
[OSB11](osb11.md#research-on-starts-from-the-block) and
[OSB12](osb12.md#research-on-starts-from-the-platform) articles summarize the ones made on each.

## Backstroke starts

This section covers the parts of the block used for a backstroke start.

Backstroke swimmers start in the water, holding grips on the block. World Aquatics and USA
Swimming place the grips 0.3–0.6 m above the water, and the NCAA no more than 30
in.[^wa-fr][^usas][^ncaa] Omega's OSB11 has two horizontal grips and one vertical
grip.[^dejesus2015] A study of video from the 2012 Olympics and the 2013 World Championships
found that elite backstrokers mostly set their feet partly out of the water and held either the
highest horizontal grip or the vertical grip. Men used fewer variations than women.[^dejesus2014]
Backstroke ledges, which hang from the block to give the feet a support at the wall, are covered
in the [backstroke start devices overview](../backstroke-start/index.md).

Diving from a block needs deep enough water at the start end, and every governing body ties the
use of blocks to depth. World Aquatics requires at least 1.35 m from 1.0 m to 6.0 m out from the
end wall in any pool with starting blocks, 1.0 m everywhere else (the first metre from the wall
included), and 2 m throughout for Olympic and World Championship pools.[^wa-fr] USA Swimming
measures depth from 1.0 m to 5.0 m out and sets nothing for the first metre, except at USA
Swimming championships and international meets, which need 2 m (6 ft 7 in) throughout the
course. Below 4 ft (1.22 m), swimmers must start in the water and backstroke ledges may not be
used. Racing starts are taught before a swimmer is certified only in water at least 6 ft (1.84 m) deep.[^usas] The NCAA asks for at least
4 ft at the starting end of an existing course and calls 7 ft desirable.[^ncaa] The high school
rules have the same 4 ft line but measure it from the wall itself out to 5 m. Shallower than that, starts are made from the water, and the depth
and platform-height rules cannot be waived by agreement between teams.[^nfhs2012]

All three require a non-slip top.[^wa-fr][^ncaa][^usas] The NCAA also requires the lane number
to be visible from every side and the blocks in all lanes to be identical, and it requires
wedge-equipped blocks to take relay judging platforms where a meet uses them.[^ncaa] USA
Swimming requires the front edge to sit flush with the wall, and the block to be fixed down,
to the deck or to a bulkhead, so that it never moves.[^usas]

At relay take-overs, the back plate may not be used as the launch point. USA Swimming and the
NFHS require the outgoing swimmer to keep part of at least one foot on the platform in front of
the back plate or wedge. The NCAA requires the take-off to be from the block or relay platform,
not the wedge.[^usas][^nfhs][^ncaa]
