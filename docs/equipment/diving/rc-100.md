The RC-100 is a wireless hand-held controller made by
[Daktronics](../../vendors/daktronics.md). It runs Daktronics scoreboards directly, and with a
base station on an [OmniSport 2000](../swimming/timers/omnisport-2000.md) timing console it
serves as a wireless judge console for diving, synchronized swimming and rodeo.[^manual][^support]
It uses a 900 MHz radio.[^manual] Daktronics drawings for the system date from 2004, and its FCC
authorization was applied for in 2005.[^charger][^fcc] Daktronics changed its drawings to the
2.4 GHz [RC-200](rc-200.md) in May 2017.[^genvi] The wired equivalent is the
[JC-100](jc-100.md).[^os2000]

## Role in the scoring system

This section places the RC-100 in a Daktronics judging setup; judging equipment in general is
covered in the [diving equipment overview](index.md). Each judge holds an RC-100 and keys in a
mark. The handhelds send their marks by radio to an
[RC-100 base station](rc-100-base-station.md). For judging, the base station is a tabletop unit
cabled to judge port J14 or J15 on the back of the OmniSport 2000, and the console gathers the
panel's marks.[^os2000][^riser]

One base station takes up to nine handhelds. Synchronized swimming can use a second base station
on the other judge port, set to a different channel, for up to 18 judges.[^riser] The
OmniSport 2000 allows panels of 2, 3, 5, 7 or 9 in diving and synchronized swimming, and 7, 9 or
11 in synchronized diving.[^os2000] Daktronics' computer scoring programs list the RC-100 and the
JC-100 as alternatives: [OmniSport Pro Diving](../../software/omnisport-pro-diving.md) takes 2 to
11 judge consoles and [OmniSport Pro Synchro](../../software/omnisport-pro-synchro.md) 2 to
18.[^prodiving][^prosynchro] In rodeo the base station usually goes on J14, and the console can be
set for one, two or four judges in scored events, or one, two, three or five in cutting.[^rodeo]

## Design and hardware

The handheld has a 4 × 4 keypad below a 97 × 32 LCD, with the radio antenna inside the
case.[^manual] A printed insert slides into a slot at the bottom to label the keys for each use.
Judging uses insert `LL-2606` and rodeo uses `LL-2608`. Most of the insert drawings date from
January 2005.[^manual] The same inserts later went into the RC-200.[^rc200]

A 2000 mAh nickel-metal hydride battery runs the handheld for 8–10 hours.[^manual][^support] The
charger is inside the unit. Plugging the supplied adapter into the socket on top switches the
handheld on and charges it, and a flat battery takes about an hour and a half.[^manual] On battery,
the handheld turns itself off after 45 minutes without a key press unless power save has been
disabled. Daktronics says to disable it when the handheld is used as a goal judge.[^manual] A
charging station holds six handhelds face down and charges them together. Its drawing, dated
January 2004, is the earliest dated RC-100 document in the manual, and the RC-200 later used the
same station design.[^charger][^rc200]

The sources disagree on whether a user can change the battery. The 2004 charging station drawing
says the battery is not field replaceable and a failed one goes back to Daktronics.[^charger] The
2018 manual says it can be replaced, using part `BT-1032`, on any handheld with assembly number
`0A-1110-0053` and on `0A-1110-0033` units at revision 7 or later, which were built after
30 August 2007. Earlier units cannot be done in the field.[^manual] The drawing predates that
change, so the manual's account is taken as the current one.

## Operation

This section covers connecting a handheld and scoring with it. The handheld has two modes. In
config mode the user sets the radio channel (1–15), the LCD contrast and power save. Holding
CONFIG for five seconds enters config mode, and a handheld that has never been set up starts in
it.[^manual] Pressing CONNECT then links the handheld to a base station on the same channel.
After that the base station's function setting decides how the handheld behaves, and
signal-strength bars appear at the top of the display.[^manual]

For judging, the base station is set to its CAN judges' console function, and no sport code is
entered on the handheld. The handheld shows WAITING FOR EXTERNAL CONTROL until the base station
finds the OmniSport 2000 on the CAN link, and then takes its prompts from the console.[^manual]
On first connection it asks for a judge number. When the operator opens scoring, it shows the dive
number and the degree of difficulty, and the judge keys a mark and presses ENTER. The EDIT key
reopens a mark until the operator has accepted the panel's scores. That procedure is the same on
the JC-100 and the RC-200.[^os2000] Rodeo judging follows the OmniSport 2000 rodeo
manual.[^manual][^rodeo]

Judging is one of the applications in which several handhelds share a base station. Most of the
scoreboard applications allow only one connected handheld, and a second one is refused with a
CHANNEL IN USE message.[^manual] Daktronics support says the number of judges must be set on the
OmniSport 2000 before the handhelds connect.[^kbsetup] For a facility that owns both RC-100 and
RC-200 handhelds, Daktronics suggests giving each model its own base station on its own judge
port.[^kbsync]

## Base station settings

This section summarizes the settings on the base station that matter for judging; the
[RC-100 base station](rc-100-base-station.md) page covers the unit itself. The base station has
two rotary switches, one for the function and one for the radio channel, and a jumper that makes
it a server or a client. Units ship as servers.[^manual] The function table in the manual is:[^manual][^install]

| Setting | Use (server mode) |
|---|---|
| 0 | The function last used |
| 1 | CAN handheld judges' console (OmniSport 2000) |
| 2 | All Sport scoreboard controller, Gen I |
| 3 | A Daktronics non-sports message display |
| 5 | All Sport scoreboard controller, Gen II |
| F | Reset memory and test |

The diving riser drawing sets judging base stations to function 1.[^riser] Daktronics' 2017
aquatics maintenance checklist gives a different figure: it says base stations on firmware 3.0
or later should be set to function 6, and earlier ones to function 1. The same checklist asks for
firmware 3.0 or later on the consoles and the base station.[^maint] The 2018 manual, published
after the checklist, still lists 6 as reserved and 1 for judging.[^manual] Neither
source explains the difference.

## Generations and firmware

Daktronics distinguishes two generations of RC-100 by firmware. A first-generation handheld
shows `ED14905` version 1.6 or lower when it starts, and a first-generation base station is
labelled `ED14906` version 2.1 or lower. Second-generation units read version 2.2 or
higher.[^manual][^kbgen] Daktronics support dates the second-generation handhelds to shipments from
August 2006.[^kbgen] The function 5 setting for the second generation was added to the
drawings in January 2007.[^riser]

The generations work together within limits. A second-generation handheld runs with a
first-generation base station only in function 2, and a first-generation handheld needs a
second-generation base station to be set to 2 as well. Function 5 is for second-generation
equipment on both ends.[^manual][^kbgen] Daktronics updated the handheld part number on its
drawings in April 2012. The judging riser and other drawings now show `0A-1110-0053`, which a
2013 kit list calls the "G2" handheld.[^riser][^manual]

## Scoreboard control

This section summarizes the RC-100's main use outside aquatics. With its base station set to an
All Sport function, the RC-100 runs a Daktronics scoreboard or timer directly. The operator picks
the application by entering the code printed on its insert.[^manual] The applications are:[^manual]

- a basic clock and score
- volleyball, sand volleyball, basketball, baseball, football and tennis
- a play clock or pitch timer
- a segment timer
- a remote start and stop for the clock of an [All Sport 5000](../common/scoreboard-control/all-sport-5000.md) console, which also sounds the horn or resets a shot or play clock depending on the jack used
- goal lights

Daktronics' support page names baseball and tennis as its main uses.[^support] A scoreboard can
hold an RC-100 base station alongside a
[Gen VI radio](../common/scoreboard-control/gen-vi-radio.md) receiver, so that a facility can run
practices from the handheld and games from a wireless All Sport console. Whichever transmitter
the scoreboard hears first keeps control. That arrangement needs base station firmware 3.7 or
later.[^genvi]

## Range and radio

The handheld must be at least 10 ft (3 m) and no more than 500 ft (152 m) from the base
station. Daktronics recommends a clear view of the base station's antenna, with the antenna
pointing straight up.[^manual] Daktronics support gives the radio as 902–928 MHz with 15 channels
and 200 mW output.[^kbradio] The FCC equipment authorization record lists it as a Part 15
spread-spectrum transmitter in the 902–928 MHz band.[^fcc]

The radio hops between frequencies. Only one server base station can use a channel. At
power-up a server checks for another server on its channel, and if it finds one its IN RANGE
light keeps flashing.[^manual] Server base stations within about 2000 ft (610 m) of each other
have to synchronize their hopping sequences. Channels 1, 6 and 11 act as sync channels for groups
of other channels. The manual's worked examples are baseball complexes with several diamonds,
where the base stations are either tied to one central unit or split into three groups.[^manual]
The judging riser asks only that two base stations at one pool use different channels.[^riser]

## Succession and status

This section covers the move to the RC-200 and the RC-100's status as of 2026. Daktronics
redrew its scoreboard radio drawings from the RC-100 to the RC-200 under one engineering change
on 17 May 2017, and the RC-200 judging riser is dated the same month.[^genvi][^rc200] The RC-100
manual was still being revised in February 2018.[^manual] The RC-200 kept the RC-100's keypad
inserts, charging station design and judging procedure. It replaced the 900 MHz radio with a
2.4 GHz one, and replaced the single 1–15 channel setting with a broadcast group and a
channel.[^rc200]

Daktronics still has a support page for the RC-100, which does not give a sales status.[^support]
Its judging role depends on the OmniSport 2000. In a support article headed as its end-of-life
plan as of fall 2023, Daktronics says that console is being sunset, with no new orders and up to
seven years of service.[^kbsetup]

## Compared with the RC-200 and JC-100

The three consoles score the same way on the OmniSport 2000. They differ in how they connect
and how they are powered.[^os2000]

| | RC-100 | RC-200 | JC-100 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Link | 900 MHz radio and base station | 2.4 GHz radio and base station | CAN cable |
| Radio channels | 15[^kbradio] | 64 (8 groups × 8)[^kbradio] | None |
| Power | 2000 mAh NiMH battery[^manual] | 2000 mAh NiMH battery[^rc200] | 12 V AC from the host[^jcspec] |
| Battery replacement | In the field on later units[^manual] | Not in the field[^rc200] | No battery |
| Consoles per input | 9 per base station[^riser] | 9 per base station[^rc200] | 9 per port[^os2000] |
| Edit key | EDIT | EDIT | RE-ENTER/EDIT[^os2000] |
