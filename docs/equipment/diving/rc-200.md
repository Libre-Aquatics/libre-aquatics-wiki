The RC-200 is a wireless hand-held controller made by
[Daktronics](../../vendors/daktronics.md). On a Daktronics scoreboard it works as a small
remote control console. Paired with a base station on an
[OmniSport 2000](../swimming/timers/omnisport-2000.md) timing console, it becomes a wireless judge
console in four OmniSport events: diving, synchronized diving, rodeo and synchronized swimming.[^manual][^os2000] It
uses a 2.4 GHz radio. Daktronics drawings switched from the 900 MHz [RC-100](rc-100.md) to the
RC-200 in May 2017.[^genvi][^riser] The wired equivalent is the [JC-100](jc-100.md).[^product]

## Role in the scoring system

This section places the RC-200 in a Daktronics judging setup; judging equipment in general is
covered in the [diving equipment overview](index.md). Each judge holds an RC-200 and keys in a
mark. The handhelds send their marks by radio to an
[RC-200 base station](rc-200-base-station.md), which plugs into judge port J14 or J15 at the rear
of the OmniSport 2000. The timing console collects the panel's marks.[^os2000][^riser]

A base station takes up to nine handhelds.[^riser][^brochure] The OmniSport 2000 can be set for
2, 3, 5, 7 or 9 judges in diving and synchronized swimming, and 7, 9 or 11 in synchronized
diving. Synchronized swimming can use two panels and up to 18 judges.[^os2000] Anything over
nine needs a second base station on the other judge port, set to a different channel from the
first.[^riser][^kbsyncdive] A smaller panel can also be split across both ports. Daktronics
suggests this for a facility that has both RC-100 and RC-200 handhelds, with each model on its
own base station.[^kbsync] According to Daktronics support, eleven-judge synchronized diving
also needs OmniSport 2000 firmware 11.7.20 or later and a computer scoring program, such as the
OmniSport Pro Diving software or [DiveMeets](../../software/divemeets.md).[^kbsyncdive]

## Design and hardware

The handheld is a small ABS plastic unit with a 16-key keypad below an LCD.[^spec][^manual] The
two Daktronics documents give different display figures. The specification sheet calls it a
128 × 64 graphic LCD without a backlight and with adjustable contrast. The operation manual says
97 × 32.[^spec][^manual]

A printed insert slides into a slot at the bottom of the case to label the keys for each use.
The judging insert, `LL-2606`, has the digits 0 to 9, a 10 key and a .5 key for half-point
marks, and EDIT, HELP, CLEAR/NO and ENTER/YES keys. The rodeo insert is `LL-2608`.[^manual] The
manual's insert drawings date from 2005 and still carry the RC-100 name, so the RC-200 takes the
same inserts as its predecessor.[^manual]

A 2000 mAh nickel-metal hydride battery gives 8–10 hours of use.[^spec][^manual] The charger is
built into the handheld. The manual says a flat battery takes about an hour and a half to
recharge from the supplied adapter. A charging station holds six handhelds at once.[^manual]
The station drawing says the battery cannot be replaced in the field and that a failed battery
goes back to Daktronics.[^charger] With the adapter plugged in, the handheld switches on and
stays on. On battery power it switches itself off after 45 minutes without a key press, unless
power save has been turned off.[^manual]

## Operation

This section covers setting up a handheld and scoring with it. The handheld has two modes. In
config mode the user sets the radio broadcast group (1–8) and channel (1–8), the LCD contrast
and the power save setting. Holding CONFIG for five seconds enters config mode, and a handheld
that has never been set up starts in it.[^manual] The handheld and its base station must be on
the same group and channel. Units sold for use outside the United States offer only groups
1–4.[^manual] Pressing CONNECT links the handheld to a base station on that group and channel.
Once connected, the handheld does whatever job the base station's function setting gives it,
and signal-strength bars appear at the top of the screen.[^manual]

For judging, the base station is set to function 1, the CAN judge console function.[^riser][^install]
No sport code is entered on the handheld. It shows WAITING FOR EXTERNAL CONTROL until the base
station finds the OmniSport 2000 on the CAN link, then takes its prompts from the
console.[^manual] Daktronics support says the number of judges must be set on the OmniSport 2000
before the handhelds connect. Otherwise they will not join, and may report that the channel is in
use.[^kbsetup]

Scoring then works as it does on the wired [JC-100](jc-100.md), and the OmniSport 2000 manual
gives one procedure for all three consoles. On first connection the handheld asks for a judge
number, which must lie between one and the panel size set on the console. When the operator opens scoring, the
handheld shows the dive's number and its degree of difficulty. The judge keys a mark and presses ENTER.
EDIT reopens the mark until the operator accepts the panel's scores.[^os2000] Rodeo judging uses
the `LL-2608` insert and follows the OmniSport 2000 rodeo manual.[^manual]

Unlike most of the scoreboard applications, judging lets several handhelds share one base
station. The manual lists the applications that allow only one handheld at a time, and judging
is not among them.[^manual]

## Scoreboard control

This section summarizes the RC-200's main use outside aquatics. With its base station set to
the All Sport function (function 2 in the base station drawings), the RC-200 runs Daktronics
scoreboards and timers directly.[^manual][^install] The operator chooses a sport by entering a
code number printed on its insert. Applications include:[^manual][^spec]

- a basic clock and score
- volleyball, sand volleyball, basketball, baseball, football and tennis
- a play clock or pitch timer
- a segment timer
- a remote start and stop for the clock of an [All Sport 5000](../common/scoreboard-control/all-sport-5000.md) console
- goal lights

A scoreboard can hold an RC-200 base station alongside a [Gen VI radio](../common/scoreboard-control/gen-vi-radio.md)
receiver, so a facility can run practice from the handheld and games from a wireless All Sport
console.[^genvi]

## Range and radio

The base station must be at least 10 ft (3 m) and no more than 500 ft (152 m) from each
handheld.[^spec][^manual] The manual recommends keeping the base station's antenna upright
and in view of the handhelds.[^manual] Daktronics support gives the radio as frequency-hopping spread
spectrum in the 2.4 GHz band, with 64 channels at 125 mW on United States units and 32 channels at
10 mW on international units.[^kbradio] The 64 channels are the eight broadcast groups times eight
channels set on the handheld.[^manual] Only one base station may act as server on a channel.
Within range of another server, the base station keeps flashing its IN RANGE light.[^manual]
Daktronics support traces handhelds that receive the dive information from the Pro Diving
software only some of the time to judges sitting too close to the base station, and gives the
same 10 ft minimum as the remedy.[^kbrandom]

## Status

This section covers availability as of 2026. Daktronics still lists the RC-200 among its
controllers, naming aquatics among its uses.[^product] The OmniSport 2000 is being withdrawn. In
a support article headed as its end-of-life plan as of fall 2023, Daktronics says it is
sunsetting the OmniSport timing system. It plans no new orders, and existing systems get up to
seven years of service.[^kbsetup] The RC-200's judging role depends on that console, so it ends
with it, while the scoreboard uses continue.

## Compared with the RC-100 and JC-100

The three consoles score the same way on the OmniSport 2000. They differ in how they connect
and how they are powered.[^os2000]

| | RC-200 | RC-100 | JC-100 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Link | 2.4 GHz radio and base station | 900 MHz radio and base station | CAN cable |
| Radio channels | 64 (8 groups × 8)[^kbradio] | 15[^kbradio] | None |
| Power | 2000 mAh NiMH battery[^spec] | Not established | 12 V AC from the host[^jcspec] |
| Consoles per input | 9 per base station | 9 per base station | 9 per port[^os2000][^kbsync] |
| Edit key | EDIT | EDIT | RE-ENTER/EDIT[^os2000] |
