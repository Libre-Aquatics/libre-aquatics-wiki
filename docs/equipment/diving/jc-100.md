The JC-100 is a wired hand-held judge console made by
[Daktronics](../../vendors/daktronics.md). Judges use it to key their marks into an
[OmniSport 2000](../swimming/timers/omnisport-2000.md) timing console in diving and synchronized
diving, in synchronized swimming and in rodeo. Fitted with a different keypad insert, it also
programs Daktronics pace clocks.[^spec][^manual] Daktronics drawings show it in 2003. Its
wireless counterparts are the [RC-100](rc-100.md) and [RC-200](rc-200.md).[^drawing][^manual]

## Role in the scoring system

This section places the JC-100 in a Daktronics system; judging equipment in general is covered
in the [diving equipment overview](index.md). Each judge enters a mark on a JC-100. The
OmniSport 2000 collects the marks, and the console or its Pro Diving and Pro Synchronized
Swimming software works out the result.[^manual][^prodiving] The console can be set for 2, 3,
5, 7 or 9 judges in diving and synchronized swimming, and for 7, 9 or 11 in synchronized
diving.[^manual] The Pro Diving software
documentation gives the range as 2 to 11 consoles.[^prodiving] In synchronized swimming one or two
panels can score, up to 18 judges in all.[^manual]

The same console serves the OmniSport 2000's rodeo program, where one, two or four judges score
riding events and up to five score cutting.[^rodeo] Daktronics' 2018 brochure pairs the JC-100
with the wireless RC-200, and up to nine of either can be used per input.[^brochure]

## Design and construction

The JC-100 is an upright hand-held unit in ABS plastic, about 8 in tall, with a two-line,
16-character LCD above a 16-key keypad.[^spec] The keypad takes a printed insert that labels
the keys for the job.[^manual][^pc2002]

- The judging insert, `LL-2570`, has the digits, a 10 key and a .5 key for scores in half
  points, and keys for HELP, CLEAR, ENTER and RE-ENTER/EDIT.[^manual]
- The pace clock insert, `LL-2584`, relabels the same keys for starting, stopping and stepping
  through a workout (START, STOP, SWIM, REST, CIRCUIT, HORN, MENU).[^pc2002]

The console has no battery or power supply of its own. It draws at most 2 W at 12 V AC from the
device it is plugged into.[^spec] Daktronics rates it for 0–54 °C (32–130 °F), and lists ETL, CSA,
CE and FCC approvals.[^spec]

## Operation

The first time a JC-100 is plugged in, it asks for a judge number. The console beeps and asks
again if the number belongs to another judge or exceeds the panel size set on the timing
console.[^manual] When the operator opens scoring for a dive, each console shows the dive number and its degree of
difficulty. The judge keys in a mark and presses ENTER, and OK appears beside the judge number.
Until the operator accepts the panel's marks, RE-ENTER/EDIT reopens the score.[^manual]

The console layout changes with the event:

- In synchronized diving the judge numbers are split into three groups by role. With eleven
  judges the groups are 1–3, 4–6 and 7–11; with nine, 1–2, 3–4 and 5–9.[^manual] The manual's
  table labels the first group as synchronization and the last, largest group as the second
  diver's execution. World Aquatics rules are the other way round: five judges mark
  synchronization and two or three mark each diver's execution.[^wacr] The group sizes match the
  rules, so the manual's labels appear to be transposed.
- In synchronized swimming each judge enters two marks for a routine. The manual's prompts are
  execution and overall impression for a technical routine, and technical merit and artistic
  impression for a free routine; with two panels, each panel enters one of the two.[^manual]
- In rodeo the judges enter a combined score or separate rider and stock scores, can mark a
  re-ride, and an additional console controls the event clock.[^rodeo]

The timing console's judge-console test screen shows which port each numbered console is on and
echoes each key pressed, so every unit can be checked before a meet.[^manual]

## Pace clock programming

With the `LL-2584` insert, the JC-100 controls Daktronics' [PC-2001](../common/pace-clock/pc-2001.md)
and [PC-2002](../common/pace-clock/pc-2002.md) pace clocks. On the PC-2001 it plugs into the
clock's four-pin auxiliary port; on the PC-2002 it connects through a junction box.[^pc2001][^pc2002]
From the console a coach sets the clock's time, brightness and horn, chooses its program, and
builds or edits workouts, which the clock keeps for next time.[^pc2001] In the start-reaction
programs the console also shows whether the relay platform is pressed or clear before the
start.[^pc2001]

Daktronics' specification sheet adds the [TI-2022](../common/pace-clock/ti-2022.md) timer to the
displays the JC-100 operates. The TI-2022's own manual does not mention the JC-100: it treats the
timer as standalone, with All Sport and OmniSport consoles as the only external
controllers.[^spec][^ti2022]

## Connection

The JC-100 talks to the timing console over a Controller Area Network (CAN) link.[^spec] The
OmniSport 2000 has two judge-console ports on its back, J14 (near) and J15 (far), and each takes up
to nine consoles.[^manual][^drawing] The consoles are daisy-chained: each has a short lead with a
male and a female connector, so the next console plugs into the one before it, and the last in the
chain takes a termination plug.[^drawing] Extension cables come in 30, 100 and 200 ft
lengths.[^drawing]

On a deck-wired pool the console runs a cable to a wall plate, and the judges' consoles plug into
judging connectors in junction boxes around the pool, wired back through the wall.[^drawingdeck]
A wireless base station for the RC-100 or RC-200 plugs into the same J14 or J15 port instead of a
chain of JC-100s.[^manual]

## Firmware and compatibility

Daktronics' maintenance checklist asks for judge-console firmware 3.0 or later on both the JC-100
and the RC-100.[^checklist] According to a Daktronics support article, a JC-100 that cannot
select the pace clock program has the wrong firmware, and Daktronics' remedy is a replacement
unit.[^kb]

## Compared with the RC-100 and RC-200

The JC-100 and the wireless consoles do the same job in the same way: the OmniSport 2000 manual
gives one set of instructions for all three, and they differ only in the name of the edit key,
RE-ENTER/EDIT on the JC-100 and EDIT on the others.[^manual] The wireless consoles reach the
timing console through a base station, nine to each; the JC-100 needs a cable to every judge's
seat but no radio, and takes its power from that cable.[^manual][^rc100riser][^rc200riser][^spec]

| | JC-100 | RC-100 | RC-200 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Link | CAN cable | 900 MHz radio and base station | 2.4 GHz radio and base station |
| Power | 12 V AC from the host | Not stated in the sources held | 2000 mAh NiMH battery[^rc200spec] |
| Consoles per input | 9 | 9 per base station | 9 per base station |
| Drawings dated | 2003 | 2004 | 2017 |
